# Through-lines — what is planted, where it lands

Status: **REFERENCE**, and it is the one file in this folder whose job is to stop other files from
becoming lost threads.

`lore/` has twenty-four testimonies, four scenes, three mission scripts and a hundred and four
anthology pieces. Every one of them is finished on its own, which is a strength and is also exactly how
a folder this size ends up as a pile of good writing that does not accumulate. A reader should be able
to feel a shape building across a whole playthrough, and the only way that survives forty more pieces
is if the shape is written down where the next author will see it.

**How to use this.** Before adding a piece, find the through-line it belongs to and put it in the
right position on that line. Before *editing* a piece, check whether it is somebody's plant or
somebody's payoff. `tests/lore-internal-consistency.test.js` asserts each payoff below still exists —
it will fail if one is quietly deleted, which is the failure mode this file exists for.

**One rule that outranks everything here.** Law 25: the thing behind the quarantine is never designed,
named, or shown. Two of the six through-lines below are *about* that thing, and they stay legal
because they only ever record **behaviour**. A shape would break the setting. Manners will not.

## T1 · The Consideration — the strongest line in the folder, and it was not connected

**The claim, which no document states and five witnesses each half-state:** whatever the Trellis holds
back is not a beast at a door. It is careful. Five species noticed courtesy, independently, across
ninety-five years, and none of them had the vocabulary to call it that.

| When | Who | What they actually said | Series |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BU 40** | Tender Ossa (Bioform) | Their hulls **refused** certain lanes. Forty years early, and it was a feeling, and you cannot file a feeling | 9 · 07 |
| **AU 0** | Elder Sarn (Crystalline) | *"It did not feel like breaking. It felt like being **set down** — carefully… There was **consideration** in it"* | 9 · 04 |
| **AU 0** | Shipmaster Vaun (Titan) | At the far end, for some hours, **one Lamp came back on.** Somebody checking a door | 9 · 11 |
| **AU 31** | Keth (Star Nomads) | The *Ninefold Patience* arrives thirty-one years late. Correct lane, correct approach, decelerating normally, **not a mark on the paint.** *"Something brought it home"* | 9 · 08 |
| **AU 55** | Osk (Quantum) | One arrival that is **not on the schedule.** Future tense, and the AU 31 ship had already arrived — so this is a different one, and it has not happened yet | 9 · 10 |

**Why this is the line to protect.** It is an escalation nobody wrote as one: *refusal* → *handling* →
*a door checked* → *a thing returned* → *a thing still coming*. Each step is more deliberate than the
last. And it is assembled from five doctrines that cannot compare notes — the Bioform had an instinct
they could not file, the Crystalline had a sensation, the Titans had a log, the Nomads had an object,
and the Quantum have a tense nobody else can read.

**The three that pair hardest, and were the ones left unconnected:**

- **Sarn and Keth are describing the same manners.** *Set down carefully, not intended to be picked up
  again soon.* *Undamaged — not a scratch, after thirty-one years in a dark that eats everything.*
  Something handles things without breaking them. Sarn felt it happen to a world; Keth can walk into
  the hull it happened to.
- **Vaun turns handling into intent.** A Lamp does not come back on by itself. It came back on at the
  far end, for some hours, in the middle of the nine days — and then stopped. Read after Sarn, that is
  not a fault recovering. That is something operating equipment.
- **Ossa moves the whole line forty years earlier.** Which means it was there, and attentive, and
  behaving considerately, *before* anything happened. The Unarriving was not its arrival. It was
  already home.

**Law 25 compliance, stated so nobody has to guess.** Nothing above describes a body, a size, a
motive, or a species. *Careful* is not a shape. If a future piece gives this thing an outline, cut the
outline and keep the courtesy — the courtesy is what is frightening, because a thing with manners has
reasons, and we are never going to be told what they are.

**Payoff, and where it is allowed to land.** Nowhere on screen. This line's ending is a player
noticing, and the campaign's only permitted acknowledgement is the one already scheduled: Rell's
single slip in mission 10, *"destroyed."* A player who has assembled T1 knows why that word is wrong
before Rell does. If anybody ever writes a document that states T1 outright, that document is the
mistake and this section is the argument against it.

## T2 · The clock — 9-D

Already built and already the folder's engine. Recorded in `20-master-timeline.md`, which is the
authority; repeated here only so nobody breaks it while editing something adjacent.

**AU 12** four heavy Mechanicus hulls enter 9-D, nothing returns, annotation reads *requires heavier
hull, revisit* — with a review interval → **AU 68** the Ancients' watch logs seven patched hulls
passing the relay on a schedule, no reconnaissance screen, nobody dispatched → **AU 74** Yard Nine's
variance is accepted, the annotation comes due this decade → **then** the Endless Yard arrives at 9-D
having not looked, because looking was never on the order.

**The keystone that makes it a designed set:** 9-D is adjacent to Analyst Nine's origin coordinate
(9 · 02), and the origin coordinate is empty space. Two documents, two empires, one sector, and nobody
has put them next to each other.

**Do not soften the ending.** The Yard is not a villain and does not know what it is doing. It is a
work order that cannot be cancelled because there is no office that could cancel it.

## T3 · Three Terrans, three irregular acts, three careers

The Terran Empire's doctrine is *write everything down*, and this line is what that costs the people
who do it. It is built; the fifth code (T4) is now its spine.

| Who | The irregular act | What it cost | Series |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clerk Ames** | Filed the four-sentence letter **correctly** | Nothing. He was right, and it ended the galaxy | 9 · 01 |
| **Deputy Warden Halloway** | Twenty-two words in a margin, on fourteen forms | Her grade progression. Permanently | 12 · 01 |
| **Yard Nine, Third Shift** | Filed a variance to the variance — *the hull is to be capable of returning with its crew* | Accepted, AU 74, which is worse | 12 · 06 |
| **Chart-Warden Rell** | Writes **unreconciled** three times, in their own hand | Their own want, deliberately unmet | `16-rell.md` |

**The shape:** in this empire the only way to tell the truth is to put it in the wrong place, and
everyone who does it pays, and none of them explains. Read in order it is one argument getting louder
across a hundred and fifteen years, made by four people who never met.

**Ames and Halloway are the hinge and must stay paired.** Same institution, sixty years apart: one
filed correctly and the galaxy ended; one filed irregularly and her career ended. Neither is bitter.
Both would do it again.

## T4 · The fifth code — planted, and now it lands

**The plant, already on the page and previously going nowhere.** Halloway's account (12 · 01) names
section four's coded field: *hazard, enemy action, structural, navigational error, other.* Five codes.
And she says, in passing, that she *"checked the schedule of amendments back to AU 11 and there has
never been one."*

**The answer, which her own detail gives away.** The Registry's schedule of amendments **begins** at
AU 11. Nothing before it survived the Shortening. The field had four codes when the Concord kept it,
and **"other" was added in the nine days or just after, by somebody whose name is not recorded, and
there is no amendment authorising it.** It is the only code in the Terran Registry with no provenance.

Which means every crew filed under it since AU 0 — and that is most of them, because *hazard* needs a
known danger and *navigational error* needs somebody to blame — has been filed under a word an
unknown clerk invented in the dark because there was no word.

**Why this is worth having.** Four separate things become one:

1. **It sharpens Halloway.** She needed a sixth code and was refused three times. The fifth code is
   proof that somebody once had exactly her problem and *won* — and nobody remembers who, and nobody
   thanked them, and the institution that punished her is built on their margin.
2. **It is the ancestor of the idiom.** Canon already holds that *ships are destroyed; crews did not
   arrive* (`07-glossary.md`). "Other" is where the galaxy put that distinction before it had language
   for it. The idiom is a bureaucratic scar that grew into a courtesy.
3. **It is where Ilsa's three names are.** A husband and two brothers, three manifests never
   reconciled — filed under code five, because there was nothing else to file them under. Rell writes
   *unreconciled* beside a code somebody invented for exactly this, and does not know that, and it is
   better if the player works it out and nobody says it.
4. **It gives Rell's want a physical object that is not sad.** Three cards, one coded field, one word
   with no author. That is a Chart-Warden's whole world in three objects.

**Constraint:** nobody in the world may state points 1–4 aloud. Halloway's line in `16-rell.md` —
*"Nobody knows why the field has five codes"* — is the only permitted acknowledgement, and it is a
complaint rather than a clue, because she does not know either.

## T5 · The count

**Vance's figure**, revised downward six times in sixty years and known to be wrong for all of them →
**Keth's thirty-one thousand**, which he will not surrender to be filed against a baseline he calls a
lie → **Rell's choice**, which is to leave three entries open rather than close them against a number
that erases his.

Built out in `16-rell.md`. It is the only through-line whose payoff is a *refusal*, and the refusal is
the arc's ending, so nothing here may be resolved in a later pass by having the figure corrected. The
figure stays wrong. That is the point: *then we lose the only thing we saved.*

## T6 · Thirty-one refusals

Ilsa Rell asked the Ancients for testimony **thirty-one times** across forty years and was refused
every time. She died in AU 68. **The Emissary answers in AU 74** — six years after her death, six
sentences long — and the answer says *we were wrong about what she was asking for.*

The payoff is not the six sentences. It is that Rell spent three years being the person who comes back
— Tessen, Ossa, Ysse, Onn, Ordry Vaun — and has therefore been doing the thing their grandmother
wanted done, for other people, without noticing. Keth gave it away in AU 66 without knowing:
*you are the only one who ever comes back.*

Epilogue: Rell files the six sentences as Testimony 12, and goes back to work.

## What is still a single thread, honestly

Not everything is woven and it should not all be. Recorded so the next pass picks a real target
instead of re-solving what is done.

- **`24-anthology/`, 104 pieces.** Deliberately standalone — one per hull, building, tech level and
  sector type, because they are codex-shaped and a codex entry cannot depend on reading another. Four
  of them touch T1 and T3; the rest are texture, correctly.
- **`23-interludes.md`, three comic scenes.** Unattached on purpose. Comedy that carries plot stops
  being relief.
- **Acts Two and Three do not exist.** `22-act-one.md` is three missions and the clock's ending is
  written in `20-master-timeline.md` rather than dramatised. That is the largest genuine gap in the
  narrative and it is a scope decision, not an oversight — see `STATUS.md` decision 4.
- **T1 has no scene.** Every other through-line has at least one moment on the page. The Consideration
  is assembled entirely from documents, which is currently correct — a scene risks giving it a shape —
  but it means the line's whole weight rests on a player reading five testimonies. If Act Two is ever
  written, the *Ninefold Patience* is the asset: a real hull, in a real berth, that a player can stand
  inside and find not a scratch on.
