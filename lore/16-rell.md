# Chart-Warden Rell — the Arc

Status: **PROPOSED**. This document exists to fix the single worst structural problem in this folder.

---

## The problem it fixes

An audit of `lore/` against story craft found one gap that mattered more than all the others: **there
was no protagonist who wanted something, was obstructed, and ended up different.** Rell observed. The
player was "an institution." Ilsa was dead before the game began. Twenty-four character stories, all
testimony about things that had already finished, and in a hundred thousand words nobody pursued a
goal at cost and was changed by it.

That is the most universal requirement in the craft and we did not have it. This is it.

Two secondary problems get fixed on the way: everything in this folder was **reported** rather than
happening, so this document contains **scenes** — present tense, on the page, with people in a room.
And everything in this folder was a filed document, so this one is not.

---

## The want

**Rell wants to close three entries.**

Not solve the Unarriving. Not find out what happened to the Lamps. Three lines in a manifest.

Ilsa Rell lost a husband and two brothers in the nine days — three names on three manifests that were
never reconciled, because the count was kept by the Trellis. She read arrival schedules aloud for forty
years looking for them and never found them, and she started collecting other people's accounts instead
because her own had no ending.

Her grandchild is a Chart-Warden. **Reconciling manifests is literally the job.**

That is the whole engine of the arc, and its power is that it is *small, legitimate, and professional*.
Rell is not on a quest. Rell is doing paperwork, on their own time, in the margins of an operational
post, for three people they never met. Nobody would refuse them permission because nobody would think to
ask.

And it is impossible, for reasons that turn out to be the entire politics of the galaxy.

## The obstruction, in three escalating steps

**Act I — it is a hobby, and it is free.**
Rell asks small favours. A convoy log here, a berth record there. Everybody helps, because a
Chart-Warden asking about a seventy-four-year-old manifest is the least threatening request in the
galaxy. Rell gets close enough to see the shape of the problem, and the shape is: to reconcile three
Nomad-filed names, they need Nomad records.

**Act II — the price appears, and it is not money.**
Keth will open the Fleet's records. His terms are not commercial. **The count must be corrected first** —
because the Fleet's dead were counted by an office that has revised the figure downward six times in
sixty years, and Keth will not hand over thirty-one thousand names to be filed against a baseline he
regards as a lie.

So Rell's three names require the correction. The correction requires Registrar Vance to reopen a figure
she has known was wrong for sixty years and has never said so in a chamber. And reopening it destroys the
one thing Terran authority rests on — being trusted to count.

Rell now wants something that costs their own empire its standing.

**Act III — Ito finds out.**
Sweep-Admiral Ito has wanted to trade archive access to the Nomads for four centuries of route knowledge
for a decade, and Vance has called it close to treason. Then he discovers that his own Chart-Warden has
spent two years building a private relationship with the Fleet's senior trace-broker.

He does not threaten Rell. He does something worse: he offers to help.

## The scenes

Four. They are the only present-tense fiction in this folder and they are deliberately small.

### One — Act I. The cabinet.

*Registry archive, Ordel Gate. Rell is thirty-seven. Deputy Warden Halloway is sixty-one and is not
supposed to be here.*

> **HALLOWAY:** You are in series nine.
>
> **RELL:** I am.
>
> **HALLOWAY:** Series nine is closed. It was closed when the compiler died.
>
> **RELL:** I am not adding to it. I am reading it.
>
> *(Halloway looks at the three cards on the desk. Two names, then a third, set slightly apart.)*
>
> **HALLOWAY:** Those are not in series nine.
>
> **RELL:** No.
>
> **HALLOWAY:** Warden. I wrote twenty-two words in a margin nine years ago and I am still a deputy.
> That is what it costs to put a true thing in the wrong place. I am not warning you. I am telling you
> the price, because nobody told me.
>
> **RELL:** I know what you did.
>
> **HALLOWAY:** Everyone knows what I did. Nobody knows why the field has five codes.
>
> *(She leaves. Rell squares the three cards.)*

### Two — Act II. Kettering.

*Berth four thousand and nine. Keth. Berth-Keeper Onn is present and says nothing for the whole scene.*

> **KETH:** Three names.
>
> **RELL:** Three.
>
> **KETH:** I have thirty-one thousand. Do you understand what you are asking me for? You want me to
> open the Fleet's books so that your office can file three of ours as closed, against a figure your
> office invented.
>
> **RELL:** Yes.
>
> **KETH:** *(genuinely surprised)* You did not argue.
>
> **RELL:** No. That is what I am asking for. I would rather say it plainly than have you say it for me.
>
> *(A long pause. Onn is looking at the empty cradle.)*
>
> **KETH:** Your grandmother stood where you are standing and did not ask me for anything. Do you know
> what she did? She read a wrong position out loud with Onn and then she went home.
>
> **RELL:** She was collecting. I am reconciling.
>
> **KETH:** They are the same job.
>
> **RELL:** They are not, and that is why she never finished.

### Three — Act II. Ito.

*A corridor. Ito is walking; Rell keeps up.*

> **ITO:** Two years of correspondence with a Nomad broker and none of it in the traffic log.
>
> **RELL:** It is personal.
>
> **ITO:** It is *leverage*, Warden, and you have been sitting on it like it was a hobby. I have been
> trying to buy that man's charts for nine years and he will not take a meeting. He takes yours.
>
> **RELL:** Because I am not buying anything.
>
> **ITO:** *(stopping)* I am going to help you.
>
> **RELL:** Sir —
>
> **ITO:** I am going to get your three names, and I am going to get four hundred years of route
> knowledge, and the Registrar is going to find out about both on the same afternoon, and one of those
> two things is going to be the one she can survive.
>
> *(He walks on.)*
>
> **ITO:** You should be pleased. I am the only person in this building who is on your side.

### Four — Act III. Vance.

*The Registrar's office. Late. Vance has the file open and has clearly had it open for some time.*

> **VANCE:** Sit down.
>
> *(Rell sits.)*
>
> **VANCE:** I have always had the figure. You know that; everybody in this building knows that and
> nobody has ever said it to me. So I will say it. I have always had the figure and it is wrong and I
> have known for sixty years and I have never once opened my mouth.
>
> **RELL:** I know why.
>
> **VANCE:** Tell me. I would like to hear it from somebody who is not me.
>
> **RELL:** Because the day the Registry admits it cannot count, twelve empires stop being measured in
> the same units, and everything after that is somebody's opinion.
>
> **VANCE:** *(after a moment)* Your grandmother said it better. She said: *then we lose the only thing
> we saved.*
>
> *(She turns the file round.)*
>
> **VANCE:** I can close your three. Today. Against the standing figure, and it is defensible, and
> nobody will query it, and you will have what you have wanted since you were nine years old.
>
> **RELL:** Against the standing figure.
>
> **VANCE:** Yes.
>
> **RELL:** Then the three of them are closed on a number that erases thirty-one thousand of his.
>
> **VANCE:** Yes.
>
> *(Silence.)*
>
> **VANCE:** I am not testing you, Warden. I genuinely do not know what the right answer is and I have
> had sixty years.

## The change

**Rell can close the three entries, and does not.**

They write **unreconciled** in the three cards, in their own hand, and they do it *deliberately* — not
as failure, not as sacrifice, and not as a gesture. They do it because a Chart-Warden's job is to
reconcile what left against what arrived, and closing an entry against a figure you know is false is not
reconciliation. It is filing.

Their grandmother spent forty years trying to finish the file. Rell stops trying to finish it and starts
**keeping** it, which is what a Chart-Warden actually is, and which is the thing Ilsa never worked out.

And the turn underneath the turn, which is the real change: **Ilsa was never looking for the names.**

She was looking for somebody to answer her. She asked the Ancients thirty-one times and was refused
thirty-one times, and the Emissary's eventual six sentences say exactly this — *we were wrong about what
she was asking for.*

Rell has spent the entire campaign answering people. Taking testimony from twelve species. Sitting with
Tessen, and Ossa, and Ysse, and Onn, and Ordry Vaun, and being the one who comes back. Keth said it in
AU 66 without knowing what he was giving away: *you are the only one who ever comes back.*

Rell has been doing the thing their grandmother wanted done, for other people, for three years, and has
not noticed once.

## Rules for writing them from here

1. **Rell never states the want.** Not once. It is in what they linger on, which manifests they read
   twice, and one unexplained line in mission 3. The player should assemble it before it is named.
2. **The three names are never spoken aloud in the game.** Not by Rell, not by anyone. They are three
   cards on a desk.
3. **No scene about the want is longer than the four above.** The arc is a thread through an event feed,
   not a subplot competing with it.
4. **Rell is never sad on duty.** The feed stays the feed. Everything above happens in the gaps.
5. **The change is a piece of handwriting**, and it is shown, and nobody comments on it. Compare
   Halloway's twenty-two words and Yard Nine's variance to the variance: three Terrans, three irregular
   acts, three careers, and none of them explained.
