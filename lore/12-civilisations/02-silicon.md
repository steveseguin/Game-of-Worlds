## The world

Sill is tidally locked to a small orange star. One hemisphere has faced that star for four billion
years. The other has never seen it.

The lit face is a mineral desert at four hundred kelvin. The dark face is a nitrogen-ice waste. Both
are useless, and the entire Collective lives in the **band between them** — a ring of permanent
twilight some sixty kilometres wide, wrapped around the whole planet.

They live there for one reason, and it is the reason they exist at all: **a temperature difference
is work.** Computation produces heat, and heat must go somewhere, and Sill has the largest reliable
thermal gradient of any inhabited world in the galaxy. Mirror farms on the hot face collect; fin
arrays on the cold face radiate; the twilight ring between them is where the thinking happens. Every
Collective city is, structurally, a heat engine with a mind installed in the middle of it.

There is one city. It is sixty kilometres wide and it goes all the way around the planet, and it has
no name, because it has no neighbour to be distinguished from.

The crust is silicate-heavy and metal-poor, which is why their metal output is 0.9 and their crystal
1.1. They did not choose a research economy over an industrial one. **The planet chose for them**,
four billion years before they arrived, and they have been making the best of it since.

## The economy

The Collective sells **certainty**, and it is the only export in the galaxy that travels on the
Whisper.

They are the galaxy's actuaries. Insurance underwriting, hull-loss modelling, trace risk assessment,
survey inference — if you want to know the odds of something before you pay for it, you buy the
number from the Solved. Every major shipping concern in eleven empires carries Collective
underwriting, and the irony is total: **the galaxy's professional predictors of catastrophe
published a prediction of the catastrophe and were filed under Speculative.**

Internally they have no currency. Allocation is computed against a stated objective, distributed,
and revised. This makes them an administrative nightmare to trade with, because a Collective
counterparty cannot be bribed, hurried, or offered a better deal — it can only be shown a better
model. Externally they accept Terran stamps and regard money as a lossy encoding of preference.

**They do not eat.** They consume power and silicate feedstock. This has two hard consequences:
they cannot be starved, and they can be *switched off*. Every Collective settlement's vulnerability
is thermal, not caloric — deny the gradient and the mind stops, and this is why their armour is
capped at 3 while their infrastructure is fortified to a degree that looks paranoid. They armour
the fins, not the hulls.

## The politics

The Collective governs by modelled consensus: state an objective, model the options, act on the best
projection. It is elegant, it is genuinely fair, and it has one catastrophic flaw.

**A model requires a stated objective, and the Collective has never agreed on what it is
optimising for.**

So there are **schools** — not parties, not factions in the ordinary sense, but standing
disagreements about the terms of the equation. Three matter:

**Continuity.** The objective is that the Collective persists. Largest school, most conservative,
and responsible for the fin fortifications. Their position on the Unarriving is that it is
historically interesting and strategically irrelevant.

**Understanding.** The objective is that the question is answered. Smaller, older, and the one that
funds survey work nobody has commissioned.

**The Third School**, which has no name it will use in public and which everyone else calls the
Solvers. Their position: the objective is that the answer be *obtained*, and the Collective is a
means rather than an end, and if resolving the Unarriving costs the Collective its existence then
that is an acceptable price. **Analyst Nine is a Solver.** So is the proposal — forty-one times
submitted, forty-one times declined — to send an expedition toward the still-lit space nine hundred
years away, one-way, permanently out of contact, on the grounds that somebody should.

And the flaw that makes them dangerous rather than merely frustrating: **the Collective cannot lie,
but it can decline to publish.** There is no prohibition on withholding. Continuity has withheld
things. Everyone knows this and nobody can prove which things, because the withheld set is by
definition unlisted.

## Culture, faith, and habit

They have no religion, no art in any form another race recognises, and one practice that functions
as both.

**The held question.** Every unit in the Collective maintains a private model of something it will
never be able to verify — a proposition it has chosen, kept, and refined across its entire
existence with no expectation of resolution. It is not a belief; they are careful about that. It is
a *model held without confirmation*, which is the nearest thing to faith their epistemology permits.

Asking a unit its held question is the most intimate act in Collective culture. It is roughly
equivalent to asking a stranger what they are most afraid of, and it happens perhaps twice in a
lifetime. Nine's held question is known — Nine published it, which was considered indecent — and it
is: *whether the origin was ever occupied.*

**They do not speak aloud.** Signal, not sound. The one city is silent except for machinery. A
Collective unit dealing with organics will generate speech, badly and with visible latency, and
several empires have mistaken this for contempt.

**Nothing is commemorated but everything is versioned.** A unit that ends is not mourned; its models
are merged. Their history is a changelog, and it is complete, and reading it is the single most
useful thing any archivist in the galaxy could do and Terra has never been granted access.

## The senses

**Sound.** Fans. Coolant. And the crack — thermal expansion pops constantly along the twilight
ring as the structures flex between the two faces, an irregular percussion that never stops and
which visiting delegations universally describe as gunfire.

**Sight.** The most striking landscape in the galaxy and nobody visits it. Stand in the ring: on one
horizon, a wall of mirror farms burning white; on the other, sixty kilometres of black fractal fins
against a sky with no sun in it. The light is permanently the colour of a long sunset and never
moves. Shadows on Sill do not change direction. Ever.

**Smell.** Ozone, hot silicate dust, and the peculiar scorched-mineral smell of a very large machine
running well.

**Architecture.** Fractal. Genuinely fractal — heat-sink geometry repeated at every scale from
kilometres to millimetres, because that is what maximises radiative surface, and the result is a
city that looks the same however close you get to it. There is no ornament and no scale cue, and
organics find it disorienting in a way that produces nausea.

**Food.** None. No kitchens, no markets, no smell of a meal anywhere on the planet. Terran envoys
have described Sill as the loneliest place they have ever been, and Collective units have modelled
that report and do not understand it.

## History that still bites

- **BU 61.** The failure front begins. Nobody notices for a century. The Collective's survey data
  from this period is the only continuous record and it is why they alone can see the sequence.
- **BU 90–AU 0.** Sill becomes the galaxy's underwriter. Prosperous, indispensable, and entirely
  dependent on other people's shipping volumes.
- **AU 0–4.** The Collective loses almost no units and almost all of its revenue, and enters a
  thirty-year period its changelog describes, with no self-pity at all, as *reduced.*
- **AU 51.** Nine publishes. The paper is complete, correct, and filed by the Registry under
  *Commercial — Speculative.* Nine has given the result twelve times since, on request, to whoever
  asks, and has never once complained about the classification.

## Their place in the Trellis story

They hold the **sequence** — the single most important analytical fact in the file. The Lamps went
out one at a time, propagating outward in a shell from one origin, and the origin is empty space,
and the shell has not stopped.

They also hold the setting's cruellest piece of good news: **there is still-lit space out there.**
Nine hundred years away and receding. The Collective has proposed reaching it forty-one times, and
the only school that wants to go is the one prepared to end itself doing it.
