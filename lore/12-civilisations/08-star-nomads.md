## The world

The Star Nomads have a homeworld and they do not live on it.

Kettering is a thin, cold, unremarkable rock whose only asset was that five Trellis lanes crossed
above it. It was never a colony in the ordinary sense; it was a **way-station** — bunkerage, repair,
bonded transit, and berths. You stopped at Kettering. You did not settle there, because there was
nothing on the ground worth having, and for nine hundred years the entire population of the Nomad
people lived *aboard*.

This is why their metal is 0.8 and their crystal 1.1: they never developed a ground industry because
they never had ground. Everything they know how to make, they make in a hull.

Their geography is therefore not planetary. **Their cities are convoy formations**, and they have
names, and lineages, and reputations: the Ninefold, the Slow Consort, Hallam's Ring. A convoy is a
neighbourhood. It has a market, a school, a clinic, three generations, and a wall of names, and it
moves.

Kettering itself is now the largest berthing complex in the galaxy, almost entirely empty. About four
per cent of the Nomad population lives there — the very old, the medically grounded, and the
keepers — and the rest of it is treated, openly and formally, as a **cemetery**. The berths that
were assigned to hulls that did not arrive in AU 0 have never been reassigned. There are eleven
thousand of them. They are swept, lit, and maintained, and they are empty, and the maintenance
budget is the single largest line item in the Nomad accounts and has never been questioned.

## The economy

**Brokerage.** They move things and they sell knowledge of how to move things.

The Nomads are the galaxy's carriers and the galaxy's trace market. A verified route is the most
valuable object in the setting, and the Fleet holds more of them than anyone including Terra —
though Terra holds the *certified* ones, which is the whole quarrel. Keth's trade is legal, informal,
and indispensable.

**They invented money.** Before the Terran stamp there was the **chit** — a bearer note redeemable
at any Fleet berth, issued against a convoy's credit, transferable, and still circulating. The chit
is the only currency in the galaxy backed by nothing but the promise of somebody who might not
arrive, and Nomads consider this the most honest instrument ever devised, and Terran economists
consider it an outrage that persists.

**Food is hydroponics plus whatever the last port had.** Chronically diverse, chronically
insufficient, and endlessly inventive — Nomad cuisine is the most sophisticated in the galaxy
precisely because it is made from constraint. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is standard. A convoy's
cooking is its identity, and asking a Nomad where a dish is from will get you a route rather than a
place.

## The politics

**Berth-rights and convoy councils.** A convoy governs itself; the Fleet coordinates through
councils weighted by tonnage and seniority. It works well, at the scale of a convoy, and it has one
devastating flaw at the scale of the galaxy.

**They have no territory, so they have no sovereignty, so they have no standing.**

The Fleet is the largest single population in the galaxy — larger than Terra's — and in every
galactic forum it is seated as a **commercial party**, not a power. It cannot bring a claim. It
cannot be a signatory. Every treaty it has ever entered has been a contract, enforceable in
somebody else's jurisdiction, and it has lost every dispute that mattered.

And the wound underneath all of it, which is the whole of Nomad politics:

**The count.** The Registry counted the hulls it held manifests for. The Nomads filed with each
other — a sovereign choice, made proudly, for nine centuries, because filing with Terran ports meant
accepting Terran jurisdiction. So when the schedule stopped, the largest population in the galaxy
lost most of its people and could not prove it. The figure has been quietly revised downward six
times in sixty years, each revision individually defensible.

Keth is precise about this and refuses the word *lie*: **a great many careful people each made one
small decision.** Which is worse, and which cannot be litigated, and which is the reason a Nomad
will always, eventually, bring up the count.

Terraforming capped at 2 is a political fact, not a technical one. The Fleet will not seriously
invest in making planets habitable, and the reason is not incapacity — **wanting a planet is what
killed everyone they knew.** Three motions to fund terraforming have reached council since AU 30. All
three failed. Two of the three sponsors lost their berths.

## Culture, faith, and habit

**The reading of the schedule.** The old Concord timetables are kept, and they are read. Aloud, at
intervals, in every convoy — arrival times for services that no longer exist, to ports nobody can
reach. It is not a lament. It is closer to a liturgy: a recitation of a world that worked, performed
by people who intend to have it back.

**A funeral is a route.** When a Nomad dies, their route is recited — every port, in order, over a
lifetime. A long-lived broker's funeral takes most of a day and is attended by anyone who shared any
part of it, which is how you learn who your grandmother travelled with.

**The rite for a salted probe.** The Nomads hold a funeral for an enemy probe destroyed by
counter-intelligence, and they are the only people in the galaxy who do, and everyone else finds it
sentimental or affected.

It is neither, and the reason is the coldest thing about them. A salted probe is a machine that was
**given a false schedule and flew into it.** That is precisely what happened to the Nomad people:
they were handed a timetable that turned out to be a lie, and an entire civilisation flew into it on
faith. They do not mourn the probe. They *recognise* it. This is also why the Fleet considers
salting the single most obscene act one power can commit against another, and will not do it, and has
never done it, and has lost sectors over the refusal.

**Music.** They are the only race with a music tradition anyone else can name, for the flattest of
reasons: eight hundred years of long transits with nothing to do. It is string-based, complex, and
performed in confined spaces. Every other empire's popular music is derivative of it and two of them
will not admit this.

## The senses

**Smell.** Recycled air first — that particular flat, warm, slightly metallic smell of a hull that
has been closed for months — and then, over it, cooking. Always cooking. A convoy smells of spice,
frying, and hot electronics, and Nomads find planetary air unpleasantly *empty*.

**Sound.** The subsonic of a hull under way, which nobody notices until it stops. Voices at close
range; there is no distance on a ship. Music, most evenings, badly and enthusiastically. And the tannoy
— convoy announcements read aloud in a formal cadence inherited from Concord port protocol.

**Architecture.** Interior only. Corridors, low ceilings, hammocks and fold-down berths, everything
padded, everything within reach, nothing wasted. And on every Nomad vessel in the Fleet, without
exception, **a wall of names** — the unreconciled from that convoy's lineage, engraved, in the main
passage where everybody has to walk past it.

**Food.** The best cooking in the galaxy from the worst ingredients. Small dishes, many of them,
intensely flavoured, eaten seated, in company, at length. A Nomad meal is the opposite of a Void
Walker's, and the two peoples find each other's eating habits genuinely upsetting.

## History that still bites

- **BU 900–0.** Nine centuries as the Trellis's carriers. Rich, mobile, and structurally homeless by
  choice.
- **AU 0, fourth day.** The *Ninefold Patience* leaves Kettering with thirty-one aboard. It does not
  arrive.
- **AU 0.** The Fleet loses more of its people in nine days than any other power loses in the entire
  Shortening, and cannot document it.
- **AU 31.** The *Ninefold Patience* arrives. Correct lane, correct approach, decelerating normally,
  transponder reporting, hull undamaged, empty. See `10-the-long-file/08-star-nomads.md`.
- **AU 31–present.** They have never told the Registry. They have had it forty-three years.
- **AU 66.** Keth gives Ilsa Rell the whole thing for nothing, having asked a price and received
  silence.

## Their place in the Trellis story

They hold the **one physical object** in the entire mystery: a hull that went into the dark on the
fourth day and came back thirty-one years later without a mark on it and without a crew. Something
brought it home. It is in a berth Keth will not name, and the campaign can put the player inside it,
and there are thirty-one places set in the galley.

They also hold the moral centre of the story. The Nomads are the people the Unarriving happened *to*,
and the count is the setting's argument that the worst injuries are administrative.

`RACE_ACCESS[8]`: `ORBITAL: 1, TERRAFORM: 2`, **all nine hulls.**

They are the only non-Terran race with the full hull roster and the worst reason to use it. **Terraform
cap 2** is a political refusal, not a technical limit — three failed council motions since AU 30.
**Orbital cap 1** because fortifying a place means intending to be at it. Colony ships at +50% speed
are nine centuries of living aboard, expressed as a stat line. A colony that can leave again is a
future Wonder proposal rather than a shipped race modifier.
