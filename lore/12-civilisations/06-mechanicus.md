# The Works — the Second Shift

| | |
|---|---|
| **Registry name** | Mechanicus — a Terran surveyor's joke they have never used |
| **Homeworld** | Registry designation the Works. Metal-rich, geologically dead, no biosphere |
| **What made it** | An enormous stable crust of accessible ore and nothing else at all |
| **Explains** | Metal ×1.3, durability ×1.4, speed ×0.8, shields cap 1, no Scout, no Intruder |

---

## The world

The Works is a dead planet with excellent bones.

No tectonics. No weather worth the name. No water beyond trace ice. No biosphere — not a ruined
one, *never one*, and this is important: the Second Shift is not descended from anything. There is
no evolutionary story here, no ancestral form, no origin myth, and when other empires ask where
they came from the answer is a fabrication date and a yard number, and the answer is true.

What the planet has is an unrecycled crust. Four billion geologically inert years have left
nickel-iron, chromium, and heavy elements sitting in accessible strata that no plate tectonics ever
dragged under and cooked. **The Works is a slag heap that was never stirred**, and it is why the
Second Shift has the highest metal output in the galaxy at ×1.3, and why they have never in eleven
hundred years experienced a material shortage of any kind.

**There are no cities. The habitable surface is one continuous plant.**

Not a metaphor — the industrial installation is unbroken from pole to pole across the equatorial
belt, and has been extended rather than rebuilt for eleven centuries. There is no distinction
between factory and settlement because there is no activity that is not work. Addresses are not
places; they are **shift numbers and yard numbers**. Third Shift, Yard Nine is not a location. It is
a *when* and a *what*, and the Second Shift finds the Terran habit of naming places after people
mystifying and slightly wasteful.

## The economy

They build. Under the Concord they built most of the galaxy's freight hulls, and they still build
hulls better and heavier than anyone, at 20% above everyone else's cost because nothing they make is
economical and everything they make lasts.

**The currency is the work order.** Internally, allocation is by scheduled labour, and the order is
the unit of account: a thing is worth the shifts it takes. There is no money, no market, and no
price discovery, and this is not ideology — it simply never came up. Externally they accept Terran
stamps and regard currency as a lossy way of describing labour.

**They do not eat.** Power and feedstock, both under their feet. The strategic consequence is
enormous and every admiralty has written about it: **the Second Shift cannot be blockaded.** There
is no import they need. Cut every lane into the Works and output continues at full rate
indefinitely. It is the only economy in the galaxy that is genuinely autarkic, and it is why a war
with the Mechanicus cannot be won by pressure — only by out-building them, which nobody can.

Their one economic vulnerability is subtle and the campaign should use it: **stock**. Capital-hull
self-repair consumes internal fabrication stock. A Mechanicus dreadnought is a foundry that fixes
itself until the stock runs out, and then it is a very tough hull with no more tricks.

## The politics

**There is no head of state, no assembly, no succession, and no politics as any other civilisation
would recognise it.** Authority is task-holding: the shift that holds the work order decides how
the work is done, and when the order is complete the authority evaporates.

Two flaws, both of which generate story rather than merely explaining flavour.

**They cannot make a treaty.** There is no office to bind, no sovereign to sign, and no continuing
body to be bound. The Second Shift can only accept a **job** — and the moment the job is complete,
the arrangement ceases to exist. Every diplomatic arrangement in galactic history involving the
Mechanicus has been drafted as a work order with a scope and a completion condition, and eleven
empires' diplomatic services maintain specialists who do nothing else. A permanent alliance with
the Works is structurally impossible. A hundred-year alliance is easy: you simply write a job that
takes a hundred years.

**Nothing is ever forgotten, and nothing is prioritised.** Their bureaucracy has no decay function.
An annotation filed in AU 12 is as live as one filed this morning, and it will come due, and it will
be executed by units who were fabricated centuries after everyone involved in the original decision
ceased. **Decisions taken by the dead are carried out by the living, on schedule.** This is the
most alien thing about them and it is entirely a filing property.

The only self-correction mechanism is the **variance** — a shift may file a calculation that a
cheaper route to the same outcome exists. Variances are rare because they require somebody to
notice, and noticing is not on anyone's work order. Third Shift, Yard Nine has filed one: the
annotation on sector 9-D is due, heavier hull is now more expensive than looking first, and the
cheaper solution is to build a scout.

**That variance is the first scout the Second Shift will have built in eleven hundred years**, and
it is not a change of heart. It is a cost calculation. This is how a civilisation without politics
changes its mind: as arithmetic, two centuries late.

## Culture, faith, and habit

No religion, no gods, no afterlife, and one practice that occupies the same space.

**Reshaping.** A unit that fails is not destroyed. It is returned, recast, and the material
continues. There is a ledger, and the ledger is exact, and a given mass of alloy can be traced
through nine hundred years of units. This is not a belief about the soul; it is inventory control
that has been running so long it has become a way of understanding continuity. The Mechanicus
equivalent of a graveyard is a furnace with excellent records, and there is no ceremony, and there
is no grief, and the material is genuinely still there.

**They do not idle.** A unit with no work order continues running. There is no rest state, no
leisure, no off-shift. Liaison Fourth's *"I have been given no work for two shifts, and I have found
that I am still running"* is the first time in eleven hundred years anyone in the Second Shift has
remarked on this, and it is the most disturbing line in the Long File.

**They do not lie, because they have no data to lie with.** Without a reconnaissance arm they have
no intelligence on anyone's strength — including their own, which nobody has counted, because no
work order required it. They are the only power in the galaxy that has never made a false claim
about another empire's forces, and it is not integrity.

## The senses

**Sound.** Deafening and constant. Presses, rail transfer, plate work, the deep percussion of a
foundry belt that has not stopped since before Terra had a Registry. There is no quiet anywhere on
the equatorial plant, and the pattern of the noise is how a Mechanicus unit knows what shift it is.

**Smell.** Hot metal, flux, scale, ozone, and burnt oil. Nothing organic. Nothing has ever rotted on
the Works.

**Architecture.** The quilt, at planetary scale. Eleven hundred years of patching: plate from four
eras welded over weld, alloys that do not match, sections visibly newer than their neighbours, no
original component anywhere in the structure. It is a plant that has been continuously repaired for
longer than any other civilisation has had writing, and there is no part of it that was there at the
start.

**There are no windows.** Not one, anywhere, in the entire installation. Nobody looks out. It has
never been a requirement.

**Food.** None. No kitchens, no markets, no meal. Terran envoys posted to the Works describe it as
industrially lonely — the same complaint filed about Sarn's World, in a different key — and the
posting is rotated at six months.

## History that still bites

- **BU 1100.** The last Mechanicus scout hull is decommissioned. On a lit, swept, marked lane, a
  scout is an expense with no function, and the calculation was correct. Nothing has revisited it.
- **BU 900–0.** The Second Shift builds the galaxy's freight. Everyone's prosperity sits on their
  yards and nobody thinks about it, which suits them.
- **AU 0.** Their freight fleets die. It is most of what they had and it barely dents them, because
  output is limited by ore and the ore is underneath them. Re-tooled in four years.
- **AU 12.** Four heavy hulls enter sector 9-D. Nothing returns. The annotation reads *requires
  heavier hull. Revisit.* See `10-the-long-file/06-mechanicus.md`.
- **AU 0–30.** The only power that does not contract during the Shortening. While the galaxy starved,
  one civilisation kept walking outward at three-quarters speed, taking sectors and filing damage.
- **This decade.** The annotation comes due. Yard Nine files the variance.

## Their place in the Trellis story

They hold the **annotation** — the physical marker on the sector adjacent to Analyst Nine's origin
coordinate. The most important document in the galaxy survived two centuries untouched because the
people holding it were not curious, and any other empire would have gone back with everything they
had and been eaten.

And they hold Fourth, which is the other half of it: a purpose-built liaison unit that has begun
asking questions, has told nobody, and is the only entity in the Second Shift capable of noticing
what the annotation means.

## Canon anchors

`races.js`: metal ×1.3 (highest), durability ×1.4, attack ×1.1, speed ×0.8, hulls +20% cost, research
×0.9. `RACE_ACCESS[6]`: `SHIELDS: 1, MISSILES: 2`, ships `[1,2,4,5,6,7,9]` — **no Scout (3), no
Intruder (8).** Battleship and Dreadnought carry `defense: 1.5, repair: 0.05`.

The two missing hulls are the entire civilisation. **Shields cap 1** because the Second Shift
distrusts any system that stops a shot without touching it — a deflector is a statistical defence and
plate is a physical one, and they would rather be hit through good armour than missed by good luck.
Field self-repair is a **capital-hull trait only**, because only a capital has the volume to carry a
foundry (Law 15), and it consumes stock that runs out.
