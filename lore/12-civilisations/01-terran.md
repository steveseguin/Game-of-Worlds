## The world

Terra is not a rich planet and never was. Middling ore, middling crystal, a temperate band of
grain plains, forests in the north, and a star with nothing remarkable about it. Every advantage
Terra ever had came from the fact that **eleven Trellis lanes met above it.**

So the geography of Terra is the geography of a junction. Nobody built inland. The population sits
in **eleven port-cities**, each one grown beneath the terminus of the lane it served, each one
named for that lane: Ordel Gate, Vail Gate, Kettering Gate, and eight more. They are not harbours.
They are freight arcologies — orbital elevator anchors, twelve-kilometre marshalling yards,
customs cathedrals, bonded warehousing on a scale nothing now requires.

Nine of the eleven lanes go nowhere anyone can reach.

**This is the most important physical fact about the Terran Empire.** Their planet is a set of
eleven colossal ports for roads that no longer exist. The infrastructure cannot be repurposed —
you cannot turn a bonded warehouse district into farmland — so each Gate is now a small, warm,
busy administrative city of perhaps two hundred thousand people, living inside a structure built
for forty million tonnes of daily throughput. They keep the lights on in the empty parts. There is
a maintenance budget line for it, argued over annually, and it has never once been cut.

The grain plains between the Gates feed the whole world and are relentlessly dull. The northern
forests matter far more than their acreage suggests, because Terra is the only power in the galaxy
that still makes **paper** at industrial scale.

## The economy

Terra's economy is a service economy that lost its customers and reinvented itself as a
*bureaucracy the galaxy cannot function without.*

They were arbitration, insurance, standards, warehousing and clearing. All of that depended on
transit. What survived was the only product that travels on the Whisper: **certification.**

- A trace certified by the Registry sells for three to four times an uncertified one, because a
  Registry seal means the crossing was independently verified against a filed manifest.
- The Registry sets the galactic standard for mass, distance, hull class and casualty
  reporting — not by authority, which it does not have, but because during the Shortening it was
  the only institution still publishing.
- **The currency is the stamp**: a Registry seal on a bearer document. The galaxy's reserve
  currency is a Terran certification mark, and it is backed by nothing except the fact that
  everyone has agreed to be measured by the same office.

**Food is a religion disguised as logistics.** Terra survived the Unarriving because it had eleven
months of everything sitting in orbital bond when the schedule stopped. Every Terran alive knows
this, and the consequence is a civilisational compulsion to over-store. There is a **Standing
Reserve** — a national grain and preserve stockpile maintained since AU 0, audited publicly,
reported in the news like weather. Every household keeps a pantry it does not need. A Terran home
with bare shelves is a scandal, and a Terran feast is not a display of wealth but a display of
*reserves*: eleven kinds of preserved fish, pickled roots, tinned fruit, salt-cured everything.
Fresh food is for peasants and optimists.

## The politics

**The Registrar is a chief archivist with executive power, and nobody meant that to happen.**

The office had no political function before AU 0. It acquired one during the Shortening because it
was the only part of the state still working, and seventy-four years later there is still no
constitutional basis for it, no succession mechanism beyond the incumbent's recommendation, and no
mechanism to remove one. Registrar Vance has been in post nineteen years and there is no legal
process by which she could leave.

Three fault lines, all live:

**Registry against Admiralty.** The Registry's power is that it holds the only copy. Sweep-Admiral
Ito's position is that a document nobody may read is not an asset, and that trading archive access
to the Star Nomads for four centuries of route knowledge would double Terran reach in a decade.
He is right about the reach and wrong about what it costs, and he says so in open session, and
Vance considers it close to treason.

**The eleven Gates against each other.** Each Gate has a dead lane, a maintenance bill, and a
constituency. Terran domestic politics is almost entirely a fight over which corpse gets funded.
Two Gates — Ordel and Vail — are on lanes that *might* be reachable again if a Lamp were ever
lit, and their delegations have voted as a bloc for sixty years on every question, regardless of
subject.

**The count.** Somebody in the Registry has been revising the Unarriving casualty figure downward
for sixty years, each revision individually defensible. Cumulatively it is a lie of several
million. Vance knows. The Nomads know. It has never been said aloud in a chamber.

## Culture, faith, and habit

Terrans are not religious. They have **rites**, and they are civic, and they are taken more
seriously than most gods.

**The Reading of the Names.** Once a year, in every Gate, the unreconciled manifests are read
aloud. Not the dead — the *unreconciled*: the names of people who left and were never confirmed to
have arrived anywhere. It takes between four and nine days depending on the Gate. Attendance is
not compulsory and is universal. Shifts are organised. Children take turns. The reading is done
badly, by ordinary people, in ordinary voices, which is the point.

**Handwriting is a taught discipline.** Terran schooling includes six years of it. The
justification is practical — hardcopy survives what systems do not — and the effect is cultural:
a Terran's hand is a signature of class and training, and Clerk Ames's *none given* in the
Reason Stated box is legible ninety-nine years later.

**The pantry.** Discussed above, and it is genuinely the centre of Terran domestic life. You are
judged by your reserve.

**Precedent as law.** A Terran trial is a search. There is no jury; there is an archivist, and the
question before the court is not *what is just* but *what was decided last time*. This produces
outcomes that are consistent, slow, and occasionally monstrous, and Terrans defend it on the
grounds that the alternative is somebody's opinion.

## The senses

**Sound.** Pneumatic tube systems — Terra never abandoned them, and every Gate has kilometres of
brass tube whacking and hissing behind the walls. Rubber stamps. Trolleys. And in the empty
freight halls, an enormous echoing quiet that the small inhabited districts are always audibly
inside of.

**Smell.** Paper dust first, always. Then hot brass, ink, floor polish, and grain. A Terran
government building smells like a library that is slightly on fire.

**Architecture.** Two scales, jammed together and never reconciled. Outside: brutal poured-concrete
freight infrastructure, unornamented, on a scale that reads as inhuman. Inside: warm wood-panelled
offices, index drawers to the ceiling, brass fittings, green shaded lamps — a nineteenth-century
civil service nesting inside a dead spaceport. Everything is labelled. The labels have labels.

**Food.** Preserved, salted, tinned, pickled, brined. Heavy bread. Strong tea served in a glass.
The national dish is a layered pie of preserved fish and root vegetables that is genuinely good and
that every other race finds baffling, because it is a meal designed to be eaten forty years after
it was made.

## History that still bites

- **BU 900–60, the Concord Centuries.** Terra grows fat on being useful and builds a civil service
  of monstrous sophistication. That civil service is the only reason it still exists.
- **BU 8.** Clerk Ames files the Ancients' letter as *Commercial — Shipping — Advisory*, correctly.
  Twenty minutes to the end of her shift. See `10-the-long-file/01-terran.md`.
- **AU 0–30, the Shortening.** The Registry keeps publishing with no authority to do so, for
  thirty years, and accidentally becomes the government.
- **AU 45.** Shipmaster Vaun's log is filed as instrument error. Titan foreign policy has not moved
  past this and never will.
- **AU 68.** Chart-Warden Ilsa Rell dies with the Long File at eleven testimonies.

## Their place in the Trellis story

Terra holds the letter, the archive, and the count. They are the custodians of a road system that
is gone, and their entire identity is the belief that if you write everything down carefully enough
nothing is truly lost — which is *false*, demonstrably, and they have built a civilisation on it
anyway, and it is the most sympathetic thing about them.

They are also where the four sentences sit, in cabinet forty-one, which makes an unremarkable
temperate planet the most important military objective in the galaxy the moment anybody finds out.
