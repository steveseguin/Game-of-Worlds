# The Long Season — the Bioform Collective

| | |
|---|---|

| **What made it** | Nutrient flow. A hull has to grow somewhere |
| **Explains** | Crystal ×1.2, metal ×0.8, hulls −10%, +2% growth/turn, **orbital locked** |

---

## The world

Ossa's Delta is warm, wet, and biologically ferocious. High oxygen, high humidity, a shallow-sea
world with enormous river systems and a biosphere that has never had a mass extinction to slow it
down. Things grow there at rates that alarm visitors. A cleared field closes in a season.

The Collective's crystal output is 1.2 because biomineralisation is easy here — shell, spicule and
lattice are grown, not refined. Their metal is 0.8 because **they do not mine.** There is no
Bioform mining tradition, no metallurgy worth the name, and no cultural concept of extraction; when
they need metal they trade for it, and they always have.

**Their cities are berths.** A hull is raised in flowing nutrient-rich water over nine years, so
settlement follows hydrology with total precision: river deltas, warm estuaries, shallow tidal
shelves. Every Bioform population centre is where a cruiser will thrive, and the map of their
civilisation is a map of good water. Inland, upland, anywhere dry — nothing. Not poverty. Simply no
reason.

And the geographic fact that explains their tech lock: **nothing on Ossa's Delta is permanent.**
Everything is grown, used, and composted. In a biosphere that closes a field in one season, a
structure that must outlast its usefulness is a structure that must be *defended from the world*,
and the Collective has no engineering tradition for that at all. They have never built anything
intended to stand empty.

This is why they cannot build an orbital turret and will never build a Warp Gate.

## The economy

They grow hulls, and they sell them, and every empire in the galaxy buys them and resents it.

A Bioform-raised hull is the cheapest good vessel available anywhere — 10% under everyone's cost,
and it *improves* with age, which no manufactured hull does. Eight of the eleven other powers have
Bioform stock in service. The Titan Lords do not, on principle. The Mechanicus do not, because a
hull they did not build is a hull they cannot reshape.

**The currency is the season.** Contracts are denominated in growth cycles, delivery is scheduled
against maturity, and the entire commercial culture assumes a nine-year horizon as the minimum unit
of a serious agreement.

**The trade flaw is structural and unfixable, and it is the most important economic fact about
them:** a living hull must be *raised*. You cannot stockpile them and you cannot surge production.
Nine years is nine years. In a long war the Collective's output is a flat line that does not respond
to demand, and every admiralty knows this, and it is why nobody is truly afraid of them.

**Food is the other half of their power.** Ossa's Delta is the galaxy's larder — abundant, varied,
spectacular, and exportable. Three empires have a caloric dependency on Bioform agriculture that
their own populations are not fully aware of. The Collective has never once used this as leverage.
They mention it, gently, when pressed, and then offer you something to eat, and it unnerves people
far more than a threat would.

## The politics

**Custodial authority: you speak for what you tend.** A grower who raises a large line has standing
proportional to it, and the assembly of tenders is weighted by berth. It is agricultural politics,
conservative by temperament, and slow by the honest constraint that nothing can be decided faster
than a growing season.

Two flaws, both live.

**Custodianship cannot be audited.** A tender's judgement about a line is a judgement about
something that will not be verifiable for nine years, and the entire epistemology of the Collective
holds that a grower's feel for a hull is real knowledge. Which it is. It also means that anyone
tending a major line holds enormous power on the basis of a claim nobody can check, and there is no
mechanism for removing a tender who is simply wrong.

**And the fracture: the silence.** Since BU 40 the Collective has known that the deep lanes were
bad — their hulls came back thin, reluctant in the berth — and they rerouted quietly and told
nobody. Every other power lost people because of what the Collective did not say.

There is now a generation that considers that silence a crime. They call themselves the **Tellers**,
their argument is that a feeling withheld is still a fact withheld, and they are gaining. The
counter-argument, which Ossa makes and which is not weak, is that there was no mechanism to tell:
*there is a box on the intake form for Reason Stated, and what would I have written?*

Ossa's testimony to Ilsa Rell was, technically, a Teller act, and Ossa was sixty-eight years old and
did it anyway, and has never described it as courage.

## Culture, faith, and habit

**The Long Season** is a real religion, and the only one in the setting with a doctrine other
civilisations find genuinely offensive.

Its central tenet is that **things should be allowed to complete.** Growth, maturity, decline, and
composting are one process, and interrupting any part of it is the only sin the faith recognises.
Death is not feared and is not mourned in the way other species mourn; a Bioform funeral is a
composting, conducted with real tenderness and no grief, and the dead are genuinely still present in
the biosphere in a way that is not mystical.

The offensive part: they hold that **fixed structures are a category error.** A thing built to last
forever is a thing that refuses to end — and that includes fortifications, and monuments, and
empires, and the Trellis. The Collective does not want the lanes relit. They have said so,
courteously, for seventy-four years, and eleven empires have taken it as obtuseness or sabotage.

They are the only power with no Lamp programme. Not from incapacity. From doctrine.

**Everything is tasted.** Bioform greeting protocol is to offer food, and refusing is a serious
discourtesy, and their diplomatic corps has caused four incidents by offering things that other
species should not eat. They also **taste hulls** — a tender assessing a line will put a hand to it
and then taste her palm, and this is normal, and Terran envoys have never got used to it.

## The senses

**Smell.** Overwhelming, immediately, and permanently: rot and blossom together, in the same breath.
Sweet decay. Every visitor's first report mentions it and most describe it as pleasant for about six
hours and then intolerable for a week and then unnoticeable forever.

**Sound.** Water, insects, and animals — an unbroken wall of biological noise that never drops below
a level other species find loud. And in the berths: hulls, breathing. A nine-year-old cruiser
respires audibly, on a slow cycle, and a berth of forty of them is the strangest sound in the galaxy.

**Architecture.** Grown wood, shell, and cultivated fibre. Nothing straight. Nothing square. Nothing
finished — every Bioform structure is on its way to being something else, and buildings are pruned
rather than repaired. There are no monuments of any kind, anywhere, and no graves.

**Food.** The best in the galaxy by universal agreement. Enormous variety, everything fresh,
everything strongly flavoured, much of it alive shortly before serving. Every visitor eats too much.
Every visitor is also, at some point, taken to see a cruiser being fed, and does not eat again that
day.

## History that still bites

- **BU 40.** The hull-lines stop taking on the deep lanes. Not sickening — *thin*, slow in the
  growth, reluctant in the berth. The Collective reroutes over eleven years and tells nobody. See
  `10-the-long-file/07-bioform.md`.
- **AU 0.** They lose almost nobody, because their ships had not been on those lanes for forty
  years. This is the founding fact of the Teller movement and the reason no other power fully trusts
  them.
- **AU 0–30.** The Collective feeds three empires through the Shortening at cost, unasked, and has
  never mentioned it in a negotiation.
- **AU 49.** Ossa's testimony. *Yours are not asked.*

## Their place in the Trellis story

They knew **forty years early**, by husbandry rather than analysis, and could not tell anyone,
because what they had was not a fact — and they are the only power in the galaxy that does not want
the lanes relit.

When the campaign reveals why they are right, their standing inverts. The people everyone patronised
for seventy-four years turn out to have been the only ones listening to the correct instrument, and
the Tellers turn out to have been arguing on the wrong side of a case they could not have understood.

## Canon anchors

`races.js`: crystal ×1.2, metal ×0.8, hulls −10% cost, all stats 1.0 otherwise,
`all: { growth: 0.02, organic: true }`. `RACE_ACCESS[7]`: `SHIELDS: 2, ORBITAL: 0`, no Carrier.

**Orbital locked** is the load-bearing lock and must never be balanced away: no turrets, no Warp
Gates, ever. It is why they cannot fortify, why they spread rather than hold, and why they alone will
never rebuild a fragment of the Trellis. Per Law 15, growth is the *only* sanctioned free
regeneration besides Mechanicus capitals, its mechanism is stated (nine-year nutrient berths), and
the hard cost is that replacement takes a decade and cannot be surged.
