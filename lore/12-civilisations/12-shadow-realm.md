# The Unlisted — the Shadow Realm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Registry name** | Shadow Realm. Listed as *unverified*, which they enjoy |
| **Homeworld** | No designation has ever been accepted. The Registry files it as *Sable's Dark* |
| **What made it** | A faint star and an atmosphere you cannot see through |
| **Explains** | Stealth signature, crystal ×1.2, durability ×0.8, armour cap 2, no Dreadnought |

---

## The world

Their star is faint — a dim red dwarf — and their world is cold, and its atmosphere is thick,
hazy, and **radio-opaque.**

That last property is the whole civilisation. On every other inhabited world in the galaxy, an
observer in orbit can see the ground. On Sable's Dark, they cannot, and never could, at any
wavelength that matters. **The Unlisted evolved and built in a place where being observed from above
was not a thing that happened**, and so they never developed the assumption of visibility that every
other species treats as a law of nature.

Their crystal is ×1.2 because cold-formed lattices grow well in the deep cold, and their metal is
×0.9 and unremarkable. And their durability is ×0.8 with armour capped at 2 for a reason that is
purely historical: **nothing on their world ever needed to survive being seen and shot at.** There
is no Unlisted armour tradition because there was never an era in which one would have been useful.
Concealment was always cheaper, and always available, and always worked.

**Their cities are underneath.** Sub-surface, under ice sheets and old lava tube systems, and the
surface is deliberately kept clear — no structures, no lights, no emissions. A Shadow city cannot
be counted from orbit. No outsider has ever been given a population figure for Sable's Dark, and
the Registry's estimate carries an error bar of a factor of six, and the Realm has never corrected
it in either direction.

## The economy

The only economy in the galaxy that produces nothing physical.

They sell **information**, and specifically they sell **fragments** — partial holdings, deliberately
incomplete, priced so that no buyer ever accumulates enough to make a whole. This is not caution. It
is pricing, and Sable says so plainly: eleven buyers, eleven fragments, and not one buyer holding
enough to make a sentence out of.

They use other empires' currencies externally. Internally they use something no other power has ever
been able to price: **owed answers.** Debt is denominated in questions you are obliged to answer when
asked, at a time of the creditor's choosing, truthfully. An Unlisted of standing may hold hundreds
of these. They are transferable. They are inheritable. They can be called at a wedding, in a
council chamber, or on a deathbed, and they are always honoured, and the culture that produced this
instrument is the same one that will not answer the first question you ask it — which is not a
contradiction. **An owed answer is precious precisely because a volunteered one does not exist.**

**Food is subterranean**: fungal cultivation, cave fauna, cold-water fermentation, dark-adapted and
unhurried. It is superb — every visitor says so — and no visitor has ever been told what any of it
is. Asking is not refused. It is simply answered with something else.

## The politics

**An unlisted hierarchy.** Nobody outside knows who leads, or how many tiers there are, or whether
the entity that signed a treaty had the standing to. Every Realm signatory has been honoured, which
is the only evidence anyone has that the structure is real.

The flaw is exquisite and it is fatal: **concealment is the state's core competency, so the Realm
cannot demonstrate its own strength.** Its deterrent depends entirely on nobody ever testing it. The
Shadow Realm is a bluff that has never been called at scale, and the day somebody counts them
properly is the day the Realm ends — and their own strategists have written this down, and it is
the founding anxiety of their foreign policy.

**And the internal fight, which is the sharpest in the setting:**

**The Keepers.** Never sell. What we hold is worth more unspent, and the moment it is retailed it
stops being leverage and becomes somebody else's document.

**The Brokers.** Sell fragments. We have no industry, no agriculture worth exporting, no armour, and
no army anyone fears once counted. Information is the only thing we have, and an asset that never
converts is a fantasy.

**The Brokers have been winning for seventy years**, which means the Realm's single irreplaceable
holding — the BU 13 imagery of eleven Ancient vessels working at the relay by the mouth — has been
sold off a frame at a time for three generations. There are eleven fragments in eleven hands. The
Keepers' position is that this was a slow catastrophe conducted for operating expenses, and the
Keepers are right, and the Brokers kept the lights on.

**Sable gave a fragment to Ilsa Rell for nothing.** That is a Broker heresy — a giveaway sets a
price of zero — and Sable did it anyway, chose a good frame deliberately, and told her the honest
reason rather than a clever one: *we cannot read them either.*

## Culture, faith, and habit

**Courtesy as armour.** Unlisted hospitality is elaborate, warm, and genuinely meant, and it is also
the primary defensive technology of the civilisation. The ethic is explicit: **you must never let a
guest know what you know.** Not lie to them — lying is crude, traceable, and beneath a host. You
simply answer a slightly different question, generously, at length, and with evident pleasure.

They never answer the first question. This is taught to children as manners.

**And they have the only positive liturgy about the dark in the galaxy.**

Their faith — and it is a faith — holds that **the unobserved is the only thing that is truly
yours.** What is seen is taken. What is recorded belongs to the recorder. A life lived unwitnessed
is the only life fully owned. From which follows the doctrine that every other power finds
unforgivable: the Dimming is not a catastrophe. It is a **sanctification.** The galaxy was
returned to a proper condition, and the empires clawing to relight it are trying to put themselves
back under observation, and the Unlisted watch this with the specific pity of people who cannot
explain a thing that seems obvious.

They say it out loud. In council. Politely. It is the single largest reason eleven empires believe
they caused it, and it is very nearly the only piece of evidence against them, and it is not
evidence of anything.

**The Unlisted are innocent of the Unarriving and guilty of profiting from it, and they have chosen —
with something that looks a great deal like enjoyment — not to defend themselves.**

## The senses

**Light.** Low, everywhere, always. Sable's Dark is lit by a dim red star through a hazy atmosphere,
and their cities are underground and lit warmly and sparsely. Nothing is bright. Other species find
the light level restful for a day and oppressive by the fourth.

**Sightlines.** This is the defining architectural fact: **you can never see far in a Shadow city.**
No sightline exceeds the length of a single room, anywhere, by design, at every scale. Corridors
turn. Halls have baffles. There are no plazas, no avenues, no vistas, and no windows onto anything
larger than a room. Visitors describe it as either intimate or suffocating and the two reports come
from the same building.

**Sound.** Muffled and close. Everything is soft, layered, hung with textile, and the acoustics are
dead. Combined with the low light, the effect is of a very large building that always feels like a
small one.

**Smell.** Fungus, cold stone, woodsmoke, and spice — heavily spiced, warm, and inviting, and it is
the first thing every envoy mentions and the thing they miss afterwards.

**Food.** Dark, rich, fermented, unexplained, and excellent.

## History that still bites

- **Before contact.** They never developed armour, siege engineering, or fortification, because on
  Sable's Dark you were never found.
- **BU 600.** First contact, made *by* the Realm, on their terms, after an unknown period of
  observing everyone else. This has never been forgiven and is still cited.
- **BU 13.** Eleven Ancient vessels work at the relay by the mouth for some months. The Realm records
  it. Nobody else in the galaxy knows the relay exists.
- **AU 0.** The Dimming. The Realm's strategic position improves in every measurable dimension, which
  is noticed immediately by everyone.
- **AU 0–present.** Universally suspected. Never charged, because there is no forum with jurisdiction
  and no evidence beyond motive.
- **AU 67.** Sable gives Ilsa Rell a fragment, for nothing, and notices that she looks tired. She dies
  within the year.

## Their place in the Trellis story

They hold the **imagery** — Ancient vessels at the relay in BU 13, five years before the four-sentence
letter, which means **the Ancients were building the shutdown before they warned anybody.** That is
the most incriminating fact in the file and it is in eleven pieces in eleven hands, and the people who
broke it up did so for rent.

And they are the setting's best answer to the question of who to blame: everybody's suspect, entirely
innocent, thoroughly compromised, and holding the truth about somebody else that they cannot read. The
day the fragments are assembled is the day the Realm loses the only thing it has — which is why Sable
told Ilsa exactly that, and gave her one anyway.

## Canon anchors

`races.js`: crystal ×1.2, attack ×1.2, speed ×1.2, research ×1.1, metal ×0.9, **durability ×0.8**.
`RACE_ACCESS[12]`: `ARMOR: 2`, ships `[1,2,3,4,5,6,8]` — no Dreadnought, no Carrier.
`unitModifiers`: `scout 0.5`, `intruder 0.8 (+1.5 attack from stealth)`, `all 0.3`.

The stealth signature is the one race ability actually implemented in the engine: it feeds
`getRaceStealthScore()` against enemy detection in `resolveBattle`, and beating their sensors means
they receive a battle summary instead of full telemetry. It hides **composition**, not the fleet.
Durability ×0.8 and `ARMOR: 2` are the geography: nothing on Sable's Dark ever needed to survive being
seen. Note that their stealth is **worthless against the Mechanicus**, who never knew what you had.
