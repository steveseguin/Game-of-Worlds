# Bell — those who never stopped

| | |
|---|---|
| **Registry name** | Void Walkers |
| **Homeworld** | Bell. Thin, dry, poor, and perfectly placed |
| **What made it** | A junction of the longest lanes, and nothing else |
| **Explains** | Speed ×1.5, armour cap 2, economy ×0.9/0.9 |

---

## The world

Bell is a poor planet. Thin atmosphere, low water, weak ore, unremarkable crystal — 0.9 and 0.9,
the flattest economy on the roster, and it is not a design compromise, it is just what Bell has.

What Bell had was **position**. Its star sat at the convergence of the four longest lanes in the
Trellis, and the longest lanes were the most valuable, because on a lit road the only thing that
matters is who can be somewhere first. For eight hundred years the Void Walkers were the fastest
thing on the fastest roads, and Bell got rich on a service rather than a resource.

The geography follows with total logic. **Nobody on Bell lives anywhere they cannot leave from
quickly.** The population is arranged along **launch spines** — nine enormous linear cities, each
one a strip forty kilometres wide and eight hundred long, running dead straight along an
acceleration corridor. Everything is built on the spine. Land off the spine is worthless, and the
interior of Bell — most of the planet — is empty, dry, and has never been settled.

A Void Walker city has a *direction*. Every building faces the way you leave. The spines run
outward from the pads like the spokes of something, and the older the district the closer it is to
the pad, and the richest addresses on Bell are the ones with the shortest walk to a departure.

## The economy

They sold speed, and secrecy, and both were the same product: **you paid a courier because a
courier arrived first and told nobody else.**

That economy is now broken, and it is worth being precise about why. Their hulls are still the
fastest in the galaxy — ×1.5, and nobody is close. But under the Trellis, speed was worth a
fortune because arriving first *meant* something in a galaxy where everyone arrived reliably.
Running dark, everybody's cargo is late and nobody's schedule holds, and being twice as fast as a
freighter that may not arrive at all is a much smaller product than it used to be.

**The currency is the charge** — a courier's fee, and by extension a unit of obligation. A charge
is personal, it attaches to a name rather than an estate, and it is **inherited**. Void Walker debt
passes to your successor along with your routes, and a courier who takes on a dead colleague's
lanes takes on her charges too. This is considered obvious. It also means a Void Walker family can
be four generations deep in someone else's obligation and still flying.

**They are hungry.** This is the fact nobody expects. Bell imported food for eight centuries
because importing was trivial and farming Bell is miserable, and after AU 0 they had to learn
agriculture in one generation on a thin dry world that does not want it. They are not starving.
They are *underfed*, permanently, as a civilisation — thin, fast, and slightly malnourished — and
the fastest people in the galaxy eat worse than anybody except the Nomads.

## The politics

**A guild, not a government.** Standing derives from routes held: a courier who carries forty lanes
outranks one who carries nine, in every practical sense, and the Route-Holders' assembly on Bell
is weighted accordingly.

Because routes are not written down, **political power dies with the person.**

This is the flaw and it is structural and terminal. Every generation, the Void Walkers have a
succession crisis, permanently, because every death redistributes standing unpredictably and there
is no institution to inherit anything. And the arithmetic underneath is worse: the guild is
currently losing roughly **four per cent of its total route knowledge per year** to old age, and
the losses are not replaceable, because the lanes were flown under lit conditions that no longer
exist and cannot be re-surveyed.

They know the number. It is published. It is argued about annually.

And the schism it has produced is the sharpest internal politics in the galaxy:

**The Scriveners** want it written down. All of it, into a Bell archive, before the last
lit-era couriers die. Their position is arithmetically unanswerable.

**The Guild proper** considers this apostasy — not error, *apostasy*, with a theology behind it
(below) — and Scriveners are stripped of standing, refused pads, and in three recorded cases
refused a funeral.

The Scriveners are right. Everybody involved understands that they are right. It has changed
nothing, and that is the most human thing about the Void Walkers.

## Culture, faith, and habit

**Memory is a sacrament, and writing is a sin.**

The theology is coherent, which is what makes it dangerous. What is written can be *taken*. What is
taken is no longer yours to give. A courier's value, her standing, her livelihood and her worth to
her people all rest on being the only copy — so committing a route to paper is not carelessness,
it is a betrayal of everyone who ever paid you for exclusivity, and it is a betrayal of the lane
itself, which was bought with somebody's crew.

**The daily recitation.** A courier speaks her lanes every morning. Out loud, in order, entire.
This takes ten minutes for a young courier and upward of two hours for a Route-Holder, and it is
done alone, before anything else, every day, for a lifetime. It is devotion and it is also
maintenance — an unspoken route decays.

**The funeral is a recitation.** A dying courier speaks her lanes to a chosen successor. It can take
days. If she dies too fast, or in the wrong order, or without a successor present, then those lanes
are simply gone — and everyone in the room knows precisely what was lost, and can name it, and can
say what it was worth.

Wren died in AU 71. She had refused the Scriveners, refused Ilsa Rell three times, and named no
successor for the Anselm lanes. What she saw in that lane died with her, on purpose, and she would
tell you it was hers to keep.

**They eat standing up.** Fast, sour, badly preserved, apologetic. A Void Walker meal is an
interruption. Long dinners are a foreign perversion they associate with the Titan Lords, whom they
regard with total incomprehension.

## The senses

**Sound.** Departures. Constant rolling thunder along the spines, dozens per hour, never stopping,
and the buildings are engineered for it — every window on Bell is triple-glazed and every wall is
mass-damped, and Void Walkers raised on a spine cannot sleep in quiet places and take white-noise
generators with them when they travel.

**Smell.** Dust, ozone, scorched ceramic. Bell's air is dry enough to crack skin, and everything
smells faintly burnt.

**Architecture.** Long, low, and streamlined for no reason at all — Bell's atmosphere is too thin
for aerodynamics to matter, and they build that way anyway, because it looks like leaving.
Everything points the same direction. There are no plazas, no squares, no gathering spaces; the
spine is a corridor and the city is a queue.

**Food.** Thin. Sour flatbread, pickled roots, a fermented drink that outsiders describe as
industrial. Portions are small and eaten fast. The one exception, and it matters: a successor's
meal, taken at a funeral recitation, which is enormous and slow and is the only time a Void Walker
sits down to eat.

## History that still bites

- **BU 800–0.** Eight centuries as the Trellis's couriers. Rich, fast, and utterly specialised.
  Armour was never developed because being hit was a failure of speed and speed was never the
  problem.
- **AU 0.** The Walkers lose a smaller fraction of their people than the Nomads and a larger
  fraction of their *value*. Their entire product evaporated in nine days.
- **AU 0–30.** They learn to farm. Badly. The generation that did it is still alive and is
  bitter.
- **AU 40.** The first Scrivener petition. Refused. Forty-one further petitions since, all refused.
- **AU 71.** Wren dies. The Anselm lanes go with her. The Guild holds a full recitation and does not
  discuss what is missing.

## Their place in the Trellis story

Courier Wren had **one second of sight** — the only living creature ever to look down a Trellis lane
in the instant its Lamp died — and the lane was occupied.

She would not describe it, on professional principle, and she died with it. That is the Void Walkers'
contribution to the story and it is also their indictment: the most valuable fact in the galaxy was
held by a people whose religion forbids recording anything, and they lost it exactly the way they
lose everything, on schedule, predictably, while arguing about it.
