## The world

Ordel Deep is a large, heavy, metal-rich world at about **1.9 standard gravity**, and every single
fact about the Titan Lords descends from that number.

Structural minimums scale with gravity. At 1.9g, the smallest viable load-bearing member is roughly
three times the mass of its equivalent on Terra, which means the smallest thing you can usefully
build is enormous — and after four hundred years of an engineering tradition in which nothing small
was ever *possible*, nothing small is now conceivable either. **The Titan shipwright's art has no
small form.** This is why they cannot build a Frigate, a Destroyer, a Scout or an Intruder: not
doctrine, like the Mechanicus, but a craft tradition with no vocabulary for it.

**There are nine cities on the entire planet.** They sit on the deepest, oldest bedrock, because
anything shallower cannot take the mass, and their positions were surveyed once, four hundred years
ago, and have not changed. Between them: high plains, heavy agriculture, and nothing else. Ordel Deep
is the emptiest inhabited world in the galaxy by settlement count and one of the most populous by
head.

And the propulsion cap comes from the same place. Getting mass off a 1.9g surface is the dominant
engineering problem of their civilisation, and they solved it four centuries ago by **building the
vessel around its launch** — enormous single-stage hulls with the lift architecture integral — rather
than by developing better drives. It worked. It works still. It also meant nobody on Ordel Deep has
had a reason to improve a drive since, and `PROPULSION: 1` is what four hundred years of a solved
problem looks like.

## The economy

**Capital goods and patience.** They build few things, enormous, permanent, and they price them over a
century.

**The currency is the consideration** — a contract denominated in **service life**. A Titan hull sold
in AU 12 is still generating revenue for the house that built it, because the consideration runs for as
long as the vessel runs, and Titan houses hold portfolios of hulls in service across eleven empires
going back three centuries. It is the most stable financial instrument in the galaxy and the least
liquid, and Terran economists study it with visible frustration.

This is why the *Weight of Consideration* is in service still, in her seventy-sixth year — forty-six of them
under Shipmaster Vaun, from her launch in BU 2 until his death in AU 69, and the rest under his
granddaughter. It is not sentiment. It is the business model.

**Food is heavy agriculture** — high-yield, dull, and enormous. The high plains produce grain and root
crops at industrial scale and there is no scarcity and no variety, and Titans eat vast quantities
slowly. Their metal is ×1.4 because the crust is rich and the gravity has concentrated the heavy
elements beautifully, and their crystal is ×0.8 because nothing about Ordel Deep favours lattice
growth.

## The politics

**An aristocracy of shipwrights.** Standing derives from what your house has built and how long it has
lasted. Nine cities, roughly forty houses, and a formal precedence order that is recalculated annually
against the service records of every hull each house has in the water.

It is transparent, it is meritocratic in its own terms, and it is a machine for producing stagnation.

**The flaw: it rewards not changing, and every individual decision is correct.**

A house that innovates and fails loses centuries of accumulated standing, because a failed hull's
service record is permanent and public. A house that repeats a proven design loses nothing. So at every
single decision point, for four hundred years, the rational choice was to build what worked last
time — and forty houses each making the correct choice four hundred times in a row produced a
civilisation with `PROPULSION: 1`, `research ×0.7`, and no small hulls.

Their own historians describe this as **maturity.** They have a word for it that translates roughly as
*settledness*, and it is a compliment.

**The Titan Lords are the roster's argument that a doctrine can be rational at every step and still be
a dead end.** They are not decadent, not corrupt, and not foolish. They are correct, repeatedly, into
extinction, and it is the most frightening kind of decline because there is no point at which anyone
could reasonably have done otherwise.

**And the standing grievance.** In AU 45 Shipmaster Vaun reported the observation of a Lamp relighting
— in writing, on arrival, in the correct form, to three authorities. The Terran Registry recorded it as
instrument error on the grounds that no other vessel corroborated it, which was procedurally correct
and factually impossible, because there was no other vessel.

Titan foreign policy has not moved past this and will not. It is raised at every council. It is the
first item in every bilateral negotiation with Terra. Vance has apologised twice, in writing, and both
apologies were accepted formally and changed nothing, because to the Weight an apology is not a
correction — **the file still says instrument error.**

## Culture, faith, and habit

**The long service.** Reverence for things that last, and it is a genuine faith with a genuine
theology: what endures is what was made correctly, and what was made correctly is the only evidence a
life leaves.

**A Titan funeral honours the works, not the person.** You are remembered for as long as something of
yours is still running. The rite is a recitation of the deceased's hulls and their current service
status, and it is updated — a house will re-read a founder's list every decade to add the years, and
the reading gets *better* as time passes, which is unique among the twelve. Vaun will be commemorated
for as long as the *Weight of Consideration* is in the water, and every year of her service is another
year of him.

The corollary is bleak and they accept it: a shipwright whose works all fail is genuinely forgotten,
deliberately, and there is no consolation offered.

**Formality.** No contractions in speech, ever. Full titles. Hurry is vulgar, and being hurried is an
insult of a specific and actionable kind. A Titan meal takes four hours minimum and business is not
discussed in the first two. Their diplomatic corps is the most difficult in the galaxy to work with
and the most reliable once an agreement exists, because an agreement is a *work* and works are
honoured.

**They do not sit at round tables.** Precedence must be visible.

## The senses

**Physical.** The air at 1.9g is thick, and it carries sound oddly — low frequencies travel and high
ones die, so a Titan city *booms* and never rings. Everything is low. Ceilings are low, thresholds are
low, and every visitor's first physical sensation is the weight in their knees and the second is that
they cannot hear consonants clearly.

**Smell.** Stone dust, heavy machine oil, and cooking fat. Titan cities smell of a quarry and a
kitchen at the same time.

**Architecture.** Megalithic and honest. Load-bearing masses, visible structural logic, nothing
cantilevered, nothing spanning further than it must. There is ornament, but the ornament is always
*structural* — a carved column is still a column, and decorating a member that carries nothing would be
considered a lie about the building. Nine cities, each a few enormous districts, each district built to
outlast the house that built it.

**Food.** Rich, fatty, enormous, and slow. Long tables, fixed seating by precedence, service in
courses over hours. Their bread is dense enough to be sold by mass. Their national dish is a
whole-roasted root vegetable the size of a Terran child, served with rendered fat and salt, and it is
excellent, and eating it takes ninety minutes, and hurrying it is the single rudest thing a guest can
do.

## History that still bites

- **BU 400.** The launch problem is solved by integral lift architecture. It is the last major Titan
  innovation and everyone alive regards it as the founding achievement of the modern era.
- **BU 400–AU 0.** Four centuries of forty houses each correctly choosing the proven design.
- **AU 0, second day.** The *Weight of Consideration* enters the Ordel–Vail lane on an eleven-day
  crossing. Every other vessel in the galaxy either arrived before the nine days began or did not
  arrive at all. She was inside it, at three-fifths of everybody's speed, for the duration, and she came
  out the far end.
- **AU 0, seventh day.** One Lamp comes back on for rather more than six hours, ahead of her, at the
  far end. It is in the log.
- **AU 45.** The log is filed as instrument error. Vaun gives testimony to Ilsa Rell the same year.
- **Present.** She is still in service.

## Their place in the Trellis story

**They hold the keystone**, and they hold it because they are slow.

Their crossing took longer than the Unarriving lasted, so the *Weight of Consideration* is the only
vessel in history to have been inside the transition and come out the other side. Her log is the only
interior record of the event: the Lamps going out ahead one at a time at a stated interval, the sequence
from within — and, at the far end, one Lamp back on, for six hours, in good order.

*It resembles somebody checking a door.*

The worst stat on the roster is the reason the most important document in the galaxy exists. And it was
filed as instrument error, by a competent clerk, for a defensible reason, which is the third time this
file has told that story and it is not the last.
