# The Not-Yet — the Quantum Entities

| | |
|---|---|
| **Registry name** | Quantum Entities. Listed as *unverified* |
| **Homeworld** | Registry designation Osk's Flux, after its first witness. High-flux, close orbit |
| **What made it** | An environment where indeterminacy is usable at macroscopic scale |
| **Explains** | Research ×1.4, crystal ×1.3, hulls +30% cost, armour cap 2, no Dreadnought |

---

## The world

Osk's Flux orbits close to a collapsed companion in a binary system, inside a radiation and particle
environment that no other species could inhabit and that no other species entirely believes in.

The consequence is the whole civilisation: in that environment, **quantum indeterminacy is usable at
a scale you can build with.** Not magic — engineering, in a place where the engineering tolerances of
reality are unusually loose. Their crystal output is 1.3 because the flux grows exotic lattices that
cannot be produced anywhere else, and their research is 1.4 because the physics that took every other
empire four centuries to derive is, on Osk's Flux, *the weather*.

**Their cities have statistical edges.** A Quantum settlement is built deep, shielded, and — this is
the part that matters — **ambiguous**: its extent is a probability distribution rather than a
boundary. There is no line where the city stops. Their census is a distribution with error bars. Their
maps have confidence intervals. Two Registry surveys of the same settlement, eleven years apart,
returned population figures differing by a factor of three, and both surveys were competent.

And the geography produces the tech lock directly: **they cannot build a settlement that is definitely
in one place.** So they have no capital city. Which is why they have no flagship. Which is why the
access table forbids them a Dreadnought.

## The economy

They sell **hedges.** Insurance against being wrong — but not the Silicon Collective's kind. The
Collective sells you the odds. The Not-Yet sell you an *instrument that resolves favourably some of
the time*, and the distinction has kept a great many lawyers employed.

A Quantum-manufactured object is slightly better than specification, unpredictably. A Quantum hull
plate is sometimes stronger than the same plate from the same batch. This is genuinely useful and
nobody enjoys buying it, because you cannot specify it, cannot audit it, and cannot rely on it in the
one instance that matters.

**They use everyone else's currency**, and they are notorious for settling in instruments that
resolve differently than the counterparty expected. Eleven empires have accused them of fraud.
Every investigation has concluded the same thing: it is not fraud, it is physics, and the contracts
were honoured as written. This is much worse, and it is why the Not-Yet are the only power with whom
Terra insists on settlement in stamps, in advance, in full.

**They eat**, and they do not agree on what. Quantum food service is prepared in portions that vary,
and a Not-Yet meal has no fixed menu — the dish is described by its distribution. Guests are
warned.

## The politics

**No decision is final.** Governance is by non-collapsed consensus: a policy exists in several states
simultaneously and is only resolved by being acted upon, at which point the other states are not
overturned so much as *not selected.* They regard this as obviously superior to voting, on the grounds
that voting throws information away.

It works, slowly, for ordinary matters. On one matter it has produced a seventy-four-year
constitutional crisis:

**A meaningful fraction of the Not-Yet has not experienced the Unarriving.**

This is not a metaphor, not a delusion, and not a fringe. It is a bounded physical claim about a
population in a high-flux environment, and it means that any policy premised on the Unarriving having
occurred cannot achieve non-collapsed consensus, because a substantial part of the polity will not
consent to a premise that is not, for them, true yet.

**Osk's faction is not a cult. It is a quorum problem that has lasted three generations.**

The practical effect is total paralysis on the one subject that matters. The Not-Yet cannot fund a Lamp
programme, cannot join a coalition premised on the shutdown, and cannot sign anything that dates the
event — which every other power reads as evasion, and which is in fact the most scrupulous
constitutional behaviour in the galaxy.

And the flaw underneath the flaw: **there is no mechanism by which the Not-Yet fraction can ever be
convinced**, because from their frame there is nothing to be convinced of. They are not waiting for
evidence. They are waiting for the event.

## Culture, faith, and habit

**They do not commemorate.** No monuments, no funerals, no memorials, no lists of the dead, no
graves — and no records of individuals who have ceased.

The reasoning is consistent and every other species finds it monstrous: a dead person is one outcome
among several, and a monument selects one branch and declares it the case, which is both false and
unkind. To carve a name is to insist on the worst version.

The Not-Yet, in turn, find the rest of the galaxy's practices genuinely cruel. They have observed the
Terran Reading of the Names — nine days of reciting the unreconciled — and their formal diplomatic
comment on it, filed in AU 22 and never withdrawn, describes it as *"a civilisation gathering annually
to eliminate its own possibilities."* Terra has never replied.

This is the sharpest cultural collision in the setting and it has no resolution, and it should not be
given one.

**They speak in the conditional and the subjunctive**, constantly, and they are not being coy — the
grammar is doing real work about states they consider unresolved. Keep their lines *short*, per the
roster note, or it becomes unreadable.

## The senses

**Sight.** Difficult, physically. Edges do not resolve. A Not-Yet structure viewed steadily appears to
have a definite boundary, and looked at again does not, and photographs of the same wall differ. Other
species report headaches and mild nausea after a few hours, and Registry postings are limited to four
months, the shortest of any station.

**Sound.** A held note, slightly out of tune with itself, everywhere, always — a beat frequency
produced by the flux interacting with their structures. It is not loud. It cannot be escaped indoors,
and it is the thing envoys dream about afterwards.

**Smell.** Ozone and something sweet, like scorched sugar.

**Architecture.** Shells, interference patterns, curves that do not close. Nothing has a defined
corner. There is no straight line anywhere in a Not-Yet settlement, and no repeated element — a
Quantum building is never symmetrical, because symmetry is a commitment.

**Food.** Served in portions that vary, described by distribution, and genuinely excellent about
seventy per cent of the time.

## History that still bites

- **BU 400.** First contact, by the Void Walkers, who were paid extremely well and refused to go back.
- **BU 200–AU 0.** The Not-Yet are the Concord's most profitable and least trusted counterparty. Four
  fraud actions, all lost by the plaintiffs.
- **AU 0.** The event occurs. Or is scheduled. The polity does not agree, and has not agreed since,
  and this is the founding fact of modern Not-Yet politics.
- **AU 22.** Their comment on the Reading of the Names is filed. It is never withdrawn and never
  answered.
- **AU 55.** Osk gives testimony to Ilsa Rell and subsequently maintains that the interview has not
  taken place. Ilsa filed it anyway, under her own name, with a note: *witness disputes the recording.
  Recorded regardless.*

## Their place in the Trellis story

Osk holds the clearest statement of the quarantine in the entire file, and it is delivered by the least
credible witness in the galaxy to an interviewer who had been sighing:

**The Lamps did not fail.** They are still there, still working. What stopped was the *arriving*. A
road does not become dangerous because the lamps go out; it becomes dangerous because something has
been put across it, and *then* the lamps are turned off so nobody sees what they are driving into.

And the item nobody has ever been able to place: for nine days nothing will arrive anywhere, and then
**one arrival that is not on the schedule.** Pair it with Vaun's log — one Lamp, back on for six
hours, at the far end, like a door being checked. Neither witness knows the other exists.

Per **Law 21**, which is the hard one: positional uncertainty is bounded to **one sector**, cannot
evade a hazard roll, cannot phase past defences into held space, and cannot be used on a hull carrying
anything that must arrive intact. **Armour cap 2 and no Dreadnought** because a thing that definitely
exists in one place can be definitely destroyed — so they never build one. The +30% cost is exotic
manufacturing, not magic.
