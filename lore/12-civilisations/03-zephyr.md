---

## The world

Churn is heavy, hot, and moving. Gravity is about 1.4 standard. The crust is metal-rich to an
absurd degree — nickel-iron close enough to the surface to be scraped — and it is also the most
tectonically active inhabited world in the galaxy. Quake swarms are continuous. Coastlines change
inside a lifetime. There are mountain ranges on Churn younger than the Concord.

**Nothing permanent can be built there, so the Swarm never learned to build permanent things.**

This is the single fact from which the entire civilisation follows. There are no cities on Churn.
There are **nests** — accreted masses of housing, foundry, and fungal culture, thrown up fast where
the population currently is, occupied for two or three decades, and abandoned when the ground goes
bad. An abandoned nest is not demolished; it is scavenged and left, and the surface of Churn is
littered with several thousand of them in every state of collapse.

The Swarm's population does not live *somewhere*. It lives *where the ground is quiet this
generation*, which is a moving target, and it moves.

Crystal is scarce — the crust is metal-rich and lattice-poor — which is why their reckoning is thin
and their probing is minimal and their expansion is done by throwing hulls rather than by buying
knowledge. They cannot afford to know things. They can afford to lose things.

## The economy

The Swarm has the highest raw industrial output in the galaxy and the least sophisticated economy.
They produce **hulls and metal at volume** and consume most of it themselves.

There is no internal currency because there is no property. Allocation is by need and density, and
need is assessed by the same mechanism that assesses everything: the greatest concentration of the
Swarm decides. Externally they trade **by mass** — literally, by weight, in standardised bulk — and
they are the cheapest supplier of raw material in eleven empires and the most frustrating
counterparty in existence, because a contract signed with the Swarm is signed with wherever the
Swarm happened to be dense that season, and next season the party you agreed with is somewhere else
and thinking about something else.

**Food is fungal mass-culture**, grown in the warm interiors of the nests on scavenged organics. It
is a pale, dense, warm paste, it is nutritionally complete, and — this surprises every visitor — it
is genuinely delicious, faintly sweet, faintly sour, like a very good sourdough. It is eaten from
communal troughs, continuously, by whoever is passing. **Nobody in the Swarm has ever eaten a meal
alone or a portion measured for one.** There is no word for a serving.

## The politics

There is no government. There is no office, no assembly, no leader, and no succession. A decision is
made **where the most of them are**, because that is where the bandwidth is densest and the
consensus resolves fastest.

Which produces the most extraordinary flaw in galactic politics: **Swarm foreign policy is a
function of geology.** When the tectonics push population toward the northern basins, the Swarm
becomes expansionist, because the northern nests border the sectors it expands into. When the ground
opens and population migrates south, the same Swarm becomes, within a decade, cautious and
mercantile — not because anybody changed their mind, but because the centre of mass moved.

Eleven empires have written diplomatic doctrine about this. None of them has found a way to
negotiate with it. The Terran Registry's standing guidance is one line long: *establish where they
are.*

**And the moral blind spot, which is the whole reason the Swarm has a story.** A fragment that loses
connection — cut off in transit, stranded, isolated — has no standing. Not as punishment. There is
simply no category for it: rights in the Swarm attach to the whole, and a fragment is not a whole and
is not a part, and the language has no construction for what it is. A fragment cannot own, contract,
speak, or be addressed. It can only rejoin, and rejoining means being absorbed, which means whatever
it learned alone is gone.

**The Remainder has been outside that category for seventy years.** Eleven hundred hulls, no
standing, and the only surviving memory of what ten thousand of the Swarm saw. It cannot go home
without erasing the thing that makes it worth going home. Nobody in the Swarm has noticed that this
is a tragedy, because noticing would require a word they do not have.

## Culture, faith, and habit

No religion. No gods, no afterlife, no dead — a Swarm unit that ends is simply a reduction in the
total, and the total is what matters, and the total is enormous.

**The practice of return.** The highest good is rejoining; the worst state is apart. Every Swarm
ritual is a reunion. When two nests come into contact after separation there is a synchronisation
that takes about four hours, involves the entire population of both, and is the closest thing the
Swarm has to a festival. It looks, from outside, like a slow riot. Visitors have described it as the
most joyful thing they have ever witnessed and have also universally declined to attend a second
one.

**Art is synchronised motion.** Hundreds of thousands of bodies moving in phase, in the open, for
hours, for no purpose. There is no audience — everyone participating is also watching, which is a
distinction the Swarm does not draw.

**There is no singular pronoun.** Not a stylistic choice: the grammar has no first-person singular
and no construction for a single individuated thing. *One* must be built from parts and the
construction is clumsy. This is why the Remainder can say only *there was one of it*, and why the
most important eyewitness account in the galaxy is four words long.

## The senses

**Sound.** There is no silence anywhere on Churn. The crust cracks continuously — a deep irregular
report, dozens per hour — and over it lies the sound of several million bodies in an enclosed warm
space, which is a hum rather than a roar. Swarm envoys posted to other capitals report the silence
as *physically painful*, and two have had to be recalled.

**Smell.** Yeast, hot rock, sulphur, and the warm bready smell of the fungal culture, which
permeates everything and which Swarm units carry with them and cannot detect.

**Architecture.** Not built — accreted. Nests grow by addition, without plan, in whatever material is
to hand, and the interiors are a warren of warm low passages with no straight lines and no
addresses. There is no exterior aesthetic because nobody looks at a nest from outside. When it is
finished, it is already being abandoned.

**Food.** The trough. Warm, communal, continuous, excellent, and eaten with the hands while standing
in a crowd. Every outsider who tries it likes it. No outsider has ever been able to tolerate the
circumstances.

## History that still bites

- **BU 400.** The Swarm builds its last capital ships — a class of nine heavy hulls, expensive,
  individually named. Six are lost in a single decade to ordinary attrition and the loss is recorded
  as having caused something the records describe, in a language with no word for grief, as *a fault
  in the whole.* No capital hull has been laid down since. This is the origin of `SHIPS: 1`.
- **AU 0.** Ten thousand of the Swarm are in transit. All of them see it. See
  `10-the-long-file/03-zephyr.md`.
- **AU 0–4.** The Swarm loses a larger absolute number than any other power and recovers faster than
  any other power, and the two facts are the same fact.
- **AU 63.** Ilsa Rell interviews the Remainder. She is the first entity of any species to address
  a fragment directly in the Swarm's recorded history.

## Their place in the Trellis story

They hold the only **eyewitness plurality** — ten thousand simultaneous observations of the moment,
from inside, and a species-level inability to describe what was seen. *There was one of it.*

The Swarm is also the setting's argument that the Unarriving was not equally survivable. Terra
survived because it was fat. The Swarm survived because it does not count, and the same event that
ended the Star Nomads as a people barely registered here — which is the least comfortable fact in
the galaxy and the Nomads bring it up.
