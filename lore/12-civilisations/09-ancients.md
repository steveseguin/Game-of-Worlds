## The world

No one has ever visited an Ancient homeworld, and there may not be one.

This is a deliberate canon gap and it should be defended. What is known — from Registry survey and
from seventy-four years of Whisper traffic — is that the Ancients maintain **installations, not
colonies.** Perhaps two hundred are catalogued. Every one of them is old, every one is near a lane,
and every one is a *facility*: a workshop, a relay station, a watch post, a depot. There are no
farms. There are no schools. There is nothing that looks like somewhere anybody was born.

The Registry's working conclusion, filed and never publicly stated, is that the Ancients are not a
civilisation in the sense the other eleven are. **They are an order with a job**, and the
installations are the job's infrastructure, and whatever produced them is either very far away or
finished.

The one thing that is certainly true: their installations are *sited along the Trellis*. All of them.
Two hundred facilities, and if you plot them the plot is a map of the lane network, and it is the
best surviving map of the Trellis that exists, and Terra has had it for sixty years and has never
managed to read the parts that matter.

## The economy

**They have no economy. They have an inventory, and it is going down.**

This is the single most important thing to understand about them and it explains the whole stat
line. The Ancients field the deadliest hulls in the galaxy — attack and durability ×1.5, full tech
tree, every ship class — and everything costs half again, and their production base is 0.8. That
combination looks like a design compromise and it is not. It is a description of a civilisation
**cannibalising a supply chain that stopped.**

Every Ancient dreadnought was built before anyone now alive. They cannot build another. What they
can do is maintain, refit, and consolidate, superbly, forever — and every hull lost is permanently
subtracted. An Ancient fleet fights like people who know exactly how many they have left, because
they do, to the unit.

**They do not use currency.** They pay in **repairs.** An Ancient will fix a thing for you — a hull,
a relay, a failing orbital — and it will be fixed better and longer than any other power in the
galaxy could manage, and that is the transaction, and it is offered rather than negotiated. Eleven
empires have tried to establish an exchange rate. There isn't one.

**Nothing about their sustenance has ever been observed.** No food, no supply runs, no consumption.
Do not resolve this.

## The politics

**An order with duties, not a state with interests.** There is no head, no assembly, no succession,
and no visible internal disagreement. Function determines authority: the unit that holds the watch
speaks for the watch.

And here is the flaw, and it is the most unusual decline in the setting.

**Their entire structure is oriented around a task they themselves terminated.** The Ancients existed
to maintain the Trellis. In BU 61 they began shutting it down. Seventy-four years later they are an
institution whose purpose was completed by its own hand, and there is nothing after it.

They are not fighting a civil war. There is no reform faction, no revanchist wing, no
succession crisis. There is **drift.** Units stop. Not dramatically — a facility goes quiet, and
nobody is dispatched, and eleven years later the Registry notices it has dropped off the Whisper.
Fourteen catalogued installations have gone silent since AU 40. None has been reoccupied. None has
been explained.

The Emissary has attended every galactic council since AU 3 and has spoken twelve times. That is the
whole of Ancient foreign policy: attendance, and near-total silence, and a shrinking number of
chairs.

**Why they say nothing.** Not stubbornness. Two reasons, both stated obliquely in the Long File.
The first is Law 25's problem: an explanation is a message, and a message can be read by the thing
they sealed out. The second is worse and is why they have stopped trying. **The shell is still
expanding and they cannot stop it.** Whatever they set in motion in BU 61 propagates on its own, and
will finish in nine hundred years, and there is nothing to negotiate about, and no help anyone can
offer, and no version of the story in which telling anyone improves the outcome.

The Emissary's silence is not a secret being kept. It is somebody with nothing useful to say, attending
every meeting anyway, for seventy-four years.

## Culture, faith, and habit

They have one rite, and it is a duty roster.

**The watch.** Somewhere out past the frontier there is a relay, sited adjacent to a collapsar such
that it can only be observed from a position nothing survives. Since AU 0, an Ancient unit has been
watching it. Continuously. Not for enemies, and not for salvage.

**They are watching to see whether the door is checked again.**

It was checked once, on the seventh day of the nine — one Lamp, back on for rather more than six
hours, at the far end, in good order — and Shipmaster Vaun of the Titan Lords is the only outside
witness, and his report was filed as instrument error. The Ancients know it happened. They have been
watching for seventy-four years for the second time.

**They use the past tense for present things.** *There was an account. It was very fine. We were
wrong.* This is not affectation and it is not grief; it is what a people sound like when they have
concluded that they are a completed thing. Keep it absolutely consistent — it is the single most
efficient characterisation device available for them, and one slip into the present tense would be
a story event.

## The senses

**Sound.** Nothing. Ancient installations are quiet in a way that is not silence — everything is
running, and nothing is loud, and the machinery is so well maintained that it produces almost no
noise at all. Visitors report the quiet as *attentive*.

**Smell.** Nothing, deliberately. Their environmental systems are scrubbed to a standard no other
power bothers with, and the absence of any smell whatsoever is the detail every envoy mentions
first. It is warm, clean, odourless, and old.

**Architecture.** No ornament and no wear. Surfaces maintained continuously for millennia look
*new*, which is far eerier than ruin — there is no patina anywhere, no worn thresholds, no scuffed
corners, nothing to indicate that anyone has ever walked here. And yet the proportions are wrong for
anyone currently alive: doorways slightly too tall, corridors slightly too wide, treads slightly too
deep. They were built for something, and it is not what maintains them now.

**Food.** Never observed. No kitchen has ever been catalogued in any Ancient installation. This is
the kind of gap that should stay a gap.

## History that still bites

- **Before all records.** They maintained the Trellis. They did not build it. **This distinction is
  the campaign's central revelation** and the Emissary will not confirm it until forced.
- **BU 61.** They begin the shutdown. The shell starts propagating outward from a point in empty
  space.
- **BU 8.** They send four sentences to every empire at once, asking for reduced transit volume, with
  no reason given. It is filed under shipping. It is the only warning they ever gave and it is the
  only thing they will still discuss.
- **BU 13.** Eleven Ancient vessels work at the relay by the mouth for some months. The Shadow Realm
  has imagery and has been selling it in fragments for seventy years.
- **AU 0, seventh day.** One Lamp comes back on for six hours. The watch begins.
- **AU 40–present.** Fourteen installations go quiet. Nobody is sent.
- **AU 74.** The Emissary gives Chart-Warden Rell six sentences. Ilsa asked thirty-one times and died
  six years earlier.

---

## Their place in the Trellis story

They are the story. They maintained the roads for longer than anyone else has had writing, they shut
them down to seal something out, the cost was the Unarriving and the Shortening and seventy-four
years of dark, and they have carried it without explaining because explaining accomplishes nothing
and risks everything.

And their last word on it, in AU 74, is not a confession of what they did. It is *"we were wrong
about what she was asking for"* — an apology to one dead archivist for having answered her question
instead of her.
