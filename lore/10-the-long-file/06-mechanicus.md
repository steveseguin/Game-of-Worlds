# Testimony 6 — Mechanicus

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Liaison Fourth, of the Second Shift |
| **Was** | Built to talk to organics. Twenty-two years in post at recording |
| **Recorded** | AU 60 |
| **Carries** | An AU 12 annotation: *requires heavier hull.* A sector that killed a heavy fleet |

---

You have asked for an account of the nine days. The Second Shift has no account of the nine days.
Freight was in transit. Freight did not arrive. Yards re-tooled. Output resumed in the fourth year.

I understand this is not what you wanted. I have no better answer. I have asked.

You have asked a second question, which is whether anything unusual is on our records from that
period. I have searched. I have found one item which is annotated *unresolved*, and unresolved
annotations are rare, so I am giving it to you.

AU 12. Sector nine of grid D. Advance element, four heavy hulls, standard survey-by-occupation.

Nothing returned.

The annotation reads: **requires heavier hull. Revisit.**

I will provide context, because you will not know how to weigh this. Four heavy hulls do not fail.
In eleven hundred years of records I can find eleven other instances. Nine were shoals. Two were
mouths. This one is neither — the sector is charted as open dark and we have crossed the adjacent
six without loss.

The annotation is due. It comes due this decade. Third Shift, Yard Nine holds it. They have
calculated that heavier hull is now more expensive than looking first, and they have filed a
variance to build a scout.

I am told by your Registry that this is significant. I do not know why it is significant. It is a
cost variance.

You are writing more than I have said.

I have a question. I am aware that I am here to answer them. I have asked my own shift and received
a work order in reply, which was correct, and I would like to ask you instead.

You send a scout ahead. It tells you what is there. Then you decide.

Is the deciding the point, or the knowing?

I ask because I have been given no work for two shifts, and I have found that I am still running.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Mechanicus.** It arrives as a work order. Losses are inventory. There are no adjectives
in the first two-thirds, and the sentence *"Nothing returned"* is doing what four paragraphs would do
in any other race's testimony. Fourth never explains Mechanicus doctrine — it simply cannot see why
the annotation matters, which tells the player everything.

**The keystone.** Sector 9-D is adjacent to Analyst Nine's origin coordinate (testimony 2). Fourth
does not know that. Nine does not know about the annotation. **Ilsa filed both and never put them
side by side**, because she was collecting grief, not solving a case. This is the file's central
withheld connection and the player must make it themselves.

**Why it had to be the Mechanicus who held it.** They don't scout. So a sector that annihilates four
heavy hulls becomes a *work order* instead of an investigation, gets filed, and sits untouched for
two centuries. Any other race would have gone back with everything they had. The most important
document in the galaxy survived because the people holding it weren't curious.

**Fourth's question.** The last three lines are the whole subplot: a purpose-built unit developing
curiosity, and the last line — *"I have found that I am still running"* — is the first thing in
eleven hundred years anybody in the Second Shift has said about being alive. It costs one line.

**VO note.** No inflection through the report. The question at the end must not be played as a
breakthrough — Fourth does not know it is asking something enormous. Flat all the way to the end,
and let the audience do the work. Any warmth in the delivery ruins it.

**Art beat.** The annotation itself: a physical plate, stamped, two hundred years of grime, four
words. And the quilt motif — the hulls that will be sent to revisit it are patched from four eras.
