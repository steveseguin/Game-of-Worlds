# Testimony 7 — Bioform Collective

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Tender Ossa of the Long Season |
| **Was** | A grower. Raised hull-lines for sixty years |
| **Recorded** | AU 49 |
| **Carries** | Their hulls **refused** the deep lanes from BU 40 — forty years early |

---

Sit. You have been standing in doorways for thirty years and it shows in your back.

You want to know what we knew. I will tell you, and you will be disappointed, and in some years you
will not be — and I would rather you were disappointed here, where I can offer you something to
drink.

We knew nothing. We are not clever. I want that first, because everyone who comes here wants us to
have been clever.

What happened was that the ships stopped thriving.

BU 40, thereabouts. Our hulls are grown — you know this, everyone knows this, but knowing a thing
and understanding it are different seasons. A hull is not built to a plan. It is raised. You put a
line in a berth and you tend it, and after nine years you have a cruiser, and in those nine years it
develops the way anything develops: some of it from what you give it, most of it from where it has
been.

And the lines that flew the deep lanes stopped taking.

Not sickening. Nothing you could show an inspector. They came back *thin*. Slow in the growth.
Reluctant in the berth. A grower feels it in a hull the way you feel a bad night in a child, and I
could not have told you one measurable thing about it, and I would have bet my orchard on it.

So we rerouted. Quietly. Over eleven years we moved our lines off the deep lanes onto the short ones,
and we told our own people it was a husbandry matter.

It was a husbandry matter.

We did not tell you. You would like to know why.

Because what we had was a feeling, and you do not file a feeling with the Registry. I have seen your
intake form. There is a box for *Reason Stated.* What would I have written? **The ships do not like
it.** Twelve empires would have thanked us and written it down and gone away — and you know they
would, because that is precisely what happened to Sarn of the Standing, for seventy-four years, with
something far more solid than we ever had.

So we moved our ships, and said nothing, and lost almost nobody in the nine days.

I am not proud of it. I have thought about it every season since. But I will not pretend we were
noble, and I will not pretend we were clever, and I will not have it written in your cabinet that the
Long Season saw it coming. We did not see anything. We do not see. We *tend* — and the thing we
tended told us, and we listened, and that is the whole of our great insight.

You should try asking your ships what they want.

Ah. No. Forgive me.

Yours are not asked.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Bioform.** Agricultural register throughout, measures in seasons, opens by noticing the
interviewer's body. Never states the Collective's philosophy — instead it *performs* the philosophy
by treating a fleet as livestock and finding that entirely unremarkable. The kindness is real and it
is also the weapon.

**The fact, and why it's structurally important.** The Bioform knew forty years early. Not by
analysis — by husbandry. They are the earliest warning in the file and the least usable, and they
told nobody, for a reason that is completely sound. This is the testimony that establishes their
"no Lamp programme, no Warp Gates ever" position as *earned* rather than obtuse: they have felt what
is on those lanes and they do not want them relit.

**The last three lines** are the best cruelty in the file, and they are delivered with total
gentleness. *Yours are not asked.* Every other empire in the galaxy builds ships. Ossa is not
scoring a point; Ossa genuinely forgot for a moment, and the apology is sincere, and that is what
makes it land.

**The cross-reference to Sarn** is deliberate and should stay. Ossa has read the file, or has heard
about it — the witnesses know each other exists, because Ilsa has been going door to door for thirty
years. Small, and it makes the collection feel like a real thing that happened in a real place.

**VO note.** Warm, unhurried, faintly amused, no bitterness anywhere. The trap is playing the last
line as a barb. Play it as an apology.

**Art beat.** A berth. A young hull nine years from being a cruiser, tended by hand. And somewhere in
frame, a thin one — a line that flew the deep lanes and came back reluctant.

**This testimony is the earliest point on the file's longest line, and it moves the whole thing forty
years.** The hulls refused those lanes from **BU 40** — four decades before the Lamps went out. Read
beside Sarn's *consideration* (testimony 4) and Keth's unmarked hull (testimony 8), the implication is
not that something arrived in AU 0. It was already there, already attentive, and already behaving
carefully, and the only ones who noticed were the people whose ships are alive and who could not file
a feeling. See `../28-through-lines.md` T1.

Which is also the quietest cruelty in the collection: the Bioform were right first, by forty years,
and being right took the form of a reluctance nobody could write down.
