# Testimony 1 — Terran Empire

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Clerk Ames, retired, Registry intake |
| **Was** | The clerk who filed the Ancients' letter |
| **Recorded** | AU 41, aged ninety-four |
| **Carries** | The four-sentence letter was real, arrived simultaneously to all twelve empires, and was filed correctly |

---

You will want to know about the letter. Everyone wants to know about the letter now.

I filed it on the twelfth of the eighth month, BU 8. I can give you the date because I can give
you every date. I filed eleven thousand documents that year, and the Registry keeps a hand-log of
who filed what, and mine is in cabinet forty-one, and you may go and read it.

It came in on the commercial wire, which is where their correspondence always came in, because in
eleven hundred years they had never sent us anything that was not a shipping matter. Four
sentences. No letterhead. No addressee — it went to all twelve of us at once, which I noticed,
and which I should have queried, because simultaneous multi-empire correspondence goes to Third
Reading whether or not it appears trivial.

That is the rule. I knew the rule. I taught the rule, later, for nineteen years.

It asked us to reduce transit volume. It did not say why. There is a box on the intake form for
*Reason Stated*, and I wrote **none given** in it, in my own hand, and that is also in cabinet
forty-one.

I classified it Commercial — Shipping — Advisory. I want to be clear with you that the
classification was correct. Given what the document said, and given what it did not say, it was
correct, and I would classify it the same way this afternoon.

It was twenty minutes to the end of my shift.

I have had forty-nine years to think about those twenty minutes and I have reached a conclusion,
and I would like you to write it down exactly as I give it to you, because people put words in my
mouth now.

It would have made no difference.

Third Reading refers to the Transit Board. The Transit Board met in the spring. By the spring we
had two more years of it in hand, and nobody was going to reduce volume across twelve economies on
four sentences from a people who had never once asked us for anything, because we could not have
afforded to, and because we would have wanted a reason, and there was no reason in the box.

I did not end the galaxy, Warden. I filed a document accurately.

But I would like it recorded that I read it twice.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Terran.** Her defence is procedural correctness and her grief is that she followed the
rules. She cites a cabinet number twice. She does not once describe how she feels — the feeling is
entirely in *"I read it twice."*

**The best thing in it** is that she is right. The letter genuinely could not have been acted on.
The galaxy ended and nobody was negligent, which is far worse than a villain and much rarer in
this genre.

**VO note.** Flat, precise, slightly deaf, no tremor. Direct the performer away from frailty — Ames
is not a sad old woman, she is a formidable retired civil servant defending a filing decision, and
she has clearly done it many times. The last line is the only one with air around it.

**Art beat.** Cabinet forty-one. A wall of index drawers, one pulled out. The intake form with
*none given* in a ninety-four-year-old's handwriting. This is the Terran clipboard motif doing its
job.

**Campaign use.** Mission 8, "Four Sentences." Ames is dead by AU 74; the testimony is how the
player learns the letter exists, which is better than a briefing telling them.
