# Testimony 3 — Zephyr Swarm

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | the Remainder |
| **Was** | A fragment of the Swarm, cut off in transit, alone since AU 0 |
| **Recorded** | AU 63 |
| **Carries** | Ten thousand of the Swarm were in transit and all saw the same thing. *"There was one of it."* |

---

We are the Remainder. We are eleven hundred of we. We were nine thousand of we. Before that we
were not counted separately at all, which was better.

We are asked what happened in the nine days. We can answer. We are the only we that can answer,
and this is not good fortune — it is the definition of what we are. We are the part that did not
get back.

Ten thousand of we were in transit when the Lamps went out. We do not think you understand what
that sentence holds. When we are in transit we are still we: the whole of we, everywhere, one thing
thinking. Ten thousand of we in transit is not ten thousand ships. It is ten thousand eyes on one
face.

So all of we saw it. Once. Together. In the last moment when there was still seeing.

We have tried to give this to the rest of we and we cannot, because we are cut, and the cut does
not heal, and after sixty years of not healing we have become something the Swarm does not have a
word for and does not want. So we will give it to you instead. You will write it down. That is not
the same as being carried, but it is what is available.

Here is what ten thousand eyes saw.

**There was one of it.**

That is the whole of it. We are sorry. We have had sixty years to find better words and there
are none. Our language has no shape for a single thing. Everything is some amount of we, or some
amount of them, or some amount of it. There is no word for *one*. We built "one of it" out of parts
and it is wrong and it is the closest we can come.

It was ahead of us. It was in the lane. It was not many. It was one, and it was one in a way that
we found —

I found it unbearable.

We would like that struck from the record. We do not use that word. We have not used it before and
we will not use it again, and we are aware that you have written it down, and we understand that
this is what you are for.

We do not want to be alone, Warden. We are not built to survive it. But we are the only part of we
that remembers, and if we go back we will be taken in, and then nobody will remember, because that
is what going back means.

So we stay out here. And we count ourselves. Eleven hundred, this morning.

I count them every morning.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Zephyr.** No singular pronoun exists in their language, and the whole testimony is built
on that absence — both as grammar and as the reason they cannot describe what they saw. A species
that cannot conceive of *one* meeting something that is emphatically one.

**The two slips.** "I found it unbearable" is noticed and objected to. "I count them every morning"
is not noticed at all, and it is the last line, and nobody remarks on it. That progression — from
alarm at the pronoun to unconscious use of it — is a whole tragedy in six words, and it is the
strongest single piece of writing in the file.

**On the cheerful Swarm.** The roster establishes the Swarm as cheerful about losses. That is
intact — the *Swarm* is. The Remainder is what happens when a piece of it is forced to be an
individual, and it is the only unhappy Zephyr in existence. Do not write the rest of them this way.

**VO note.** The hardest role in the file. Plural voice without gimmickry — no chorus effect, no
layering, no processing. One performer, flat and gentle, and the horror comes entirely from the
pronouns. If a director reaches for audio treatment, the line "I count them every morning" stops
working.

**Art beat.** Eleven hundred small hulls in loose formation, drifting, none of them individuated —
and the formation is *counted*, in neat rows, which is not how a swarm flies.
