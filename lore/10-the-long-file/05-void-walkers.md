# Testimony 5 — Void Walkers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Courier Wren |
| **Was** | Point courier, Bell to Anselm's Reach. The only person alive who was inside the transition |
| **Recorded** | AU 52. Refused twice before |
| **Carries** | She had one second of sight, and the lane ahead was **occupied** |

---

Forty minutes, Bell to Anselm's Reach. Longest lane in the galaxy. I flew it two hundred and six
times. I could fly it now.

I will not.

You want the nine days. Fine. It is on the record elsewhere in pieces and you may as well have it
in one place.

I was point. Convoy of nine, me in front. Courier work — I carried the manifests, they carried the
freight. That is how it is done.

Was done.

The Lamp ahead of me went out while I was looking at it.

Understand what that is. On a lit lane you can *see*. That is the entire function of a Lamp; that
is the entire reason we existed as a profession. So I had sight, and I was watching the Lamp, and it
stopped — and for about one second I still had the sight it had already given me.

One second. At that speed, one second is a long look.

**The lane was occupied.**

No. I am not going to describe it. Do not lean forward at me.

I decelerated. Everything I have ever been taught says do not decelerate in a lane, do not
decelerate blind, deceleration is what slow people do instead of arriving. And I stood on it, and I
turned, and I came out sideways into open dark at a quarter of the speed I went in at, and I did not
arrive anywhere for eleven days.

The other eight did not decelerate.

They were doing it correctly.

You want to know why I will not describe what I saw. I will tell you, because it is not what you
think, and I am tired of the assumption.

It is not that I cannot. It is not too terrible for words. I am a courier. Route knowledge is not
written down — that is our whole profession, that is why we are paid, that is why I am the only copy
of two hundred lanes and why my mother was the only copy of hers and died of it.

What is in that lane is route knowledge.

It is the most valuable route knowledge in the galaxy.

So: no, Warden. Not into your cabinet. Not for nothing.

Ask me again in twenty years, when you have something to trade and I am old enough to be
sentimental.

Forty minutes, Bell to Anselm's Reach. It is a very fine lane. Somebody should have it.

---

## Notes

**Why it's a Void Walker.** States a transit time in the first line and again in the last. Clipped
sentences, no interiority, visibly impatient with being interviewed. Refuses to write anything down,
on professional principle, in the middle of an archive interview — which is the entire Void Walker
tragedy performed rather than described.

**The gut-punch.** *"The other eight did not decelerate. They were doing it correctly."* Nine words,
and it contains her survival, her guilt, and her doctrine's fatal flaw. Do not add anything around
it.

**The withholding is not a mystery-box.** She has a stated, in-character, mercenary reason, and it
is consistent with everything else about her people. The player will find it maddening, which is
correct. **She never tells anyone.** She dies in AU 71 — three years before the campaign — and the
route knowledge dies with her, which is the exact thing the Void Walkers do to themselves.

**Ilsa asked three times.** Recorded on the third. Note the pattern building across the file: Ames
answered once, Sarn twice, Wren three times, the Ancients thirty-one and refused. The collector's
persistence is characterised entirely through metadata.

**VO note.** Fast. Faster than anyone else in the file. No pauses except before "They were doing it
correctly." Direct the performer to be *irritated* rather than haunted — the haunting leaks out
anyway, and it lands much harder when she is trying to get the interview over with.

**Art beat.** A single fast hull, coming out of a lane sideways, wrong. And the visual restraint that
matters: we never show what was in the lane. Not now, not ever.

**Wren is the only bet in either book that paid — see `../28-through-lines.md` T7.** She did the one
thing her doctrine forbids, blind, at speed, and it bought her a second of sight and her life. Every
other wager in this folder cost somebody: Halloway's corridor, Sten's inherited lane, four Mechanicus
hulls at 9-D. The setting's exchange rate on knowledge is always bad, and Wren is the exception that
makes the rule visible.

**And she is the front half of Account 5, twenty-two years later.** Sten breaks off in the sixth hour
exactly as Wren did — that account says so. What it cannot say, because Sten does not know it, is the
symmetry: **Wren broke a rule to save herself, and Sten will have to break one to save somebody she
will never meet** (plant P2). One deceleration and one refusal, the same doctrine, opposite
directions, and the second one is the more expensive.

Which is also why Wren refuses to describe what she saw and is *irritated* rather than haunted. She
has spent thirty-four years being the person whose survival everybody wants a lesson from, and there
isn't one. She got lucky at speed. She knows exactly how thin that is.
