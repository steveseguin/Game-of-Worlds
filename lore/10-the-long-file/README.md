# Registry Series 9 — The Long File

Status: **PROPOSED**. Twelve testimonies, one per race. The single most useful body of writing in
this folder.

---

## What it is

Officially: *Registry Series 9, Collected Testimony Concerning the Unarriving.* Everyone calls it
**the Long File.**

It is a Terran archive series containing first-person accounts from all twelve species, gathered
over forty years by one Chart-Warden who could not stop. It is exactly the artefact the Terran
Empire would produce — their doctrine is *write everything down*, and this is what that doctrine
looks like when it is applied to grief.

**Why this frame rather than twelve character profiles:**

1. **It lets every race speak in its own voice without breaking the one-voice VO rule.** These are
   documents. Rell reads from them. A transcript quoted aloud is not a second performer.
2. **It demonstrates instead of explaining.** No testimony states its race's doctrine. Each one
   *enacts* it, which is rule 3 in `03-themes.md` and the difference between good and terrible
   faction writing.
3. **It gives the collection a reason to exist and a person who made it.** A codex nobody in the
   world wrote is furniture. A codex somebody spent forty years on is a character.
4. **It ships in pieces.** Twelve independent 400-word documents. Each one is a finished
   deliverable on its own — a codex entry, a loading-screen read, a VO block.

---

## Who collected it

**Chart-Warden Ilsa Rell.** Our Rell's grandmother.

She lost her husband and both her brothers in the nine days — three names on three manifests that
were never reconciled, because the count was kept by the Trellis. She read arrival schedules aloud
for forty years hoping to find them. She never did.

Somewhere around AU 28 she started collecting other people's accounts instead, and the reason is
the saddest thing in this folder: **her own account had no ending, so she went looking for someone
else's that did.** None of them had one either. She kept going anyway, for forty years, across
eleven species, and she died in AU 68 with the file unfinished.

She never got the Ancients to speak to her. She asked, formally, thirty-one times.

Her grandchild inherited the file. **In AU 74 — the campaign's present — Rell gets the twelfth
testimony.** That is the last entry, and it is six sentences long.

## The structural payoff: the file *is* the mystery

Each of the twelve testimonies contains one concrete fact. Alone, each is meaningless — an old
woman's filing anecdote, a statistical footnote, a merchant's ghost story. Read together, they
spell out the quarantine, and no character in the world has ever read them together.

| # | Race | Witness | The fact they carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Terran | Clerk Ames | The four-sentence letter was real, arrived simultaneously to all twelve, and was filed correctly |
| 2 | Silicon | Analyst Nine | The Lamps failed in a **sequence**, propagating outward in a shell from one origin — and the origin is empty space |
| 3 | Zephyr | the Remainder | Ten thousand of the Swarm were in transit and all saw the same thing. "There was one of it." |
| 4 | Crystalline | Elder Sarn | It did not feel like breaking. It felt like being **set down** — carefully, by someone |
| 5 | Void Walkers | Courier Wren | She was inside the transition, had one second of sight, and the lane ahead was **occupied** |
| 6 | Mechanicus | Liaison Fourth | An AU 12 annotation: *requires heavier hull*. A sector that killed a heavy fleet |
| 7 | Bioform | Tender Ossa | Their hulls **refused** certain lanes from BU 40 — forty years early |
| 8 | Star Nomads | Keth | A ship that did not arrive in AU 0 **arrived in AU 31.** Empty, undamaged, decelerating normally |
| 9 | Ancients | the Emissary | Six sentences. A confession, if you can read it |
| 10 | Quantum | Osk | Describes the Unarriving in future tense — including **one arrival that is not on the schedule** |
| 11 | Titan Lords | Shipmaster Vaun | The ship's log from inside the transition. At the far end, for some hours, **one Lamp came back on** |
| 12 | Shadow Realm | Sable | Imagery of Ancient vessels at the relay in BU 13 — and they have been selling it in fragments for seventy years |

**And here is the thing that makes this a designed set rather than twelve nice pieces of writing:**
each race could only notice its own fact *because of its doctrine.*

Silicon found the sequence because they compute rather than see. Crystalline felt it because they
are made of the same substance. The Titans hold the keystone log because they are slow — their
crossing took longer than nine days, so their ship was *inside* the transition and came out the
far side. Mechanicus has the annotation because they don't scout, so a sector that kills a fleet
becomes a work order instead of an investigation. Wren survived because she decelerated, which is
the one thing her doctrine forbids. Bioform knew first and told nobody, because what they had was
a feeling and you cannot file a feeling with the Registry.

Every race's answer to the dark determined what truth it was able to hold. That is what the
roster was for.

## The collector, characterised entirely through metadata

A device worth protecting, because it costs nothing and does an enormous amount of work. Every
testimony's header records how many times Ilsa had to ask:

| Witness | Asked | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Clerk Ames | once | She had been waiting forty-nine years for someone to ask |
| Elder Sarn | twice, ten years apart | *"You are the first one to ask me twice."* |
| Courier Wren | three times | Refused twice. Gave it, kept the important part |
| Sable | once, after thirty years | *"You have finally come to us."* |
| the Ancients | **thirty-one times** | Refused every time. Answered six years after she died |

Ilsa never speaks in the file. Not one of her questions is printed. She is built entirely out of
recording dates, refusal counts, and the things witnesses say *about* her — Sarn noticing she came
back, Keth's *"you are the only one who ever comes back,"* Sable's *"you look tired."*

That is the whole technique of the collection: **the person the file is about is the only person
not in it.**

## Reading it in the right order

The file is filed chronologically by recording date, which is *not* the order that makes sense —
and that is correct, because an archive is filed by when it arrived, not by when it becomes
useful. A player who reads it in file order gets Ames, then Sarn, then Vaun's log, then Nine's
analysis, and has to do the assembling themselves.

**Nobody in the world has put Nine's origin coordinate next to Fourth's annotation.** They are
adjacent. That is the reader's job, and the campaign should let a player who did it feel it.

## Craft rules for these twelve

Enforced, and worth checking every draft against:

1. **No testimony explains its own race.** If a witness describes their civilisation's philosophy,
   cut it and let them behave instead.
2. **Grammatical signature over vocabulary.** A race is identifiable by tense, person and sentence
   length, not by invented nouns. The Ancients' past tense; the Quantum conditional; the Swarm's
   missing singular.
3. **One physical detail each.** A cabinet number. A transponder still reporting. Twenty minutes
   to the end of a shift. The abstract does not survive being read aloud.
4. **The interviewer is audible only through the gaps.** Ilsa's questions are never printed. You
   can tell what she asked from what was answered — and from what wasn't, which is how Sable's
   testimony works entirely.
5. **350–500 words.** A testimony is not a chapter. Short also means recordable, readable in-game,
   and revisable.
6. **Nobody is lying.** All twelve are telling the truth as they have it. The mystery is
   distribution, not deception. This is much harder and much better than an unreliable narrator.

## How it reaches the player

- **Codex:** one entry per race, unlocked on first contact. Zero new systems — it is text in a
  panel.
- **Rell reads excerpts** at story beats, from their grandmother's file, which is why they are
  quoting rather than briefing. Small tonal shift the player will feel before they can name it.
- **Loading screens.** Four hundred words is too long; a *sentence* of it is perfect.
- **Late campaign:** Rell takes the twelfth testimony themselves, and files it, and says so. Ilsa
  asked thirty-one times and was refused. That is the payoff for a subplot that costs nothing but
  a line here and there.
