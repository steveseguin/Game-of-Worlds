# Testimony 11 — Titan Lords

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Shipmaster Vaun, of the *Weight of Consideration* |
| **Was** | In command of the only vessel that crossed a lit lane *during* the nine days |
| **Recorded** | AU 45 |
| **Carries** | The log from inside the transition. At the far end, for some hours, **one Lamp came back on** |

---

You will forgive me if I begin some distance before the part you want. I am told that other witnesses
have obliged you with brevity. I am not able to, and I would observe that brevity has not yet
produced you an answer.

The vessel was the *Weight of Consideration.* Heavy freight. I had her for forty-six years and she is
still in service, which will tell you something about how we build and something about how we sell.

We were crossing from Ordel to the Vail approaches. Under the Concord, this was a crossing of eleven
days.

I would like you to sit with that figure, because every person I have told this to has hurried past
it, and it is the entire reason we are speaking.

Eleven days. Your couriers ran it in six hours. Our fastest hull would have managed three. The
*Weight of Consideration* took eleven days, because she massed what she massed, and we do not build
for haste, and we have never apologised for it, and I do not intend to begin now.

We entered the lane on the second day of your nine.

We were inside it. All of it. Beginning to end. Every other vessel in this galaxy either arrived
before it began or did not arrive at all. We were in the middle, at three-fifths of everybody's speed,
for the duration.

There is a log.

The log records the Lamps ahead of us going out one at a time, in order, at a stated interval. I will
give you the interval; your Silicon analysts have it to four figures and mine agrees with theirs to
three, which I mention because they and I have never corresponded.

They went out ahead of us, and they went out behind us, and we continued, because there was nothing
else available to do, and by the sixth day we had no sight at all and were running on the last plot
and the assumption that a lane swept in the morning is swept in the evening.

She came out the far end.

I do not offer that as seamanship. I offer it as mass. Anything smaller would be part of the Vail
shoal.

Now the part you came for. I will give it plainly, and then I will tell you what was done with it.

On the seventh day, at the far end, ahead of us — **one Lamp came back on.**

For rather more than six hours. Then it went out again.

I reported it in writing, on arrival, in the correct form, to your Registry, to my own, and to a
Transit Board that no longer existed.

Your Registry recorded it as instrument error.

The reason given was that no other vessel had corroborated the observation. Warden: no other vessel
*could* have corroborated it. There was no other vessel. That was rather the point. I said so. I was
thanked.

One Lamp. On, and then off. In the middle of the dark, at the far end, in good order.

I have had seventy-four years to consider what such a thing resembles. I have arrived at one answer
and I dislike it.

It resembles somebody checking a door.

---

## Notes

**Why it's a Titan.** Long, formal sentences, no contractions anywhere, open contempt for haste, and
the first third is spent defending a design philosophy nobody attacked. He will not be hurried even
into his own testimony — and being unhurried is precisely why he has the document.

**The keystone, and the best structural payoff in the project.** `RACE_ACCESS[11]` caps propulsion at
1 and sets speed ×0.6. That weakness is why a Titan hull was still in transit after nine days, which
is why it was inside the transition, which is why it holds the only interior record of the Unarriving
in existence. **Their worst stat is the reason they own the most important fact in the galaxy.**

**Why nobody acted on it.** Filed as instrument error for a defensible reason — no corroboration —
which could not exist by definition. Same failure mode as Ames's classification and Nine's paper: not
one villain in the chain, just competent people applying correct procedure to an unprecedented thing.
The file keeps making this argument and it should.

**"Somebody checking a door."** Pairs with Osk's *one arrival that is not on the schedule* (testimony
10). Between them: the quarantine was tested from the far side, once, and something may already be
through. Neither witness knows the other exists.

**VO note.** Slow, patrician, entirely unhurried — and *not* pompous. Vaun is a working shipmaster
with forty-six years in one hull, and the formality is professional rather than aristocratic. The last
line must be delivered as a conclusion he has resisted, not a revelation he enjoys.

**Art beat.** The log itself — a physical volume, seven days of entries, the handwriting unchanged
throughout. And the *Weight of Consideration*, in service still, in her seventy-sixth year.
