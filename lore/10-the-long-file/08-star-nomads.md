# Testimony 8 — Star Nomads

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Keth of the Fleet, trace-broker |
| **Was** | Great-grandchild of the *Ninefold Patience*'s manifest |
| **Recorded** | AU 66 |
| **Carries** | A ship that did not arrive in AU 0 **arrived in AU 31.** Empty, undamaged, decelerating normally |

---

Sit down, sit down. You are the Rell woman.

My mother's cousin mentioned you — no, that is a lie, I looked you up. Thirty years at this and you
have never bought anything. That is either integrity or bad manners and I have not decided which.

You want the nine days. Everybody wants the nine days. I will give you those for nothing, because
everybody has them. Then I will give you the thing you actually came for, and we will discuss what
it costs.

Nine days: we lost everyone.

You have the figure. No — you have *a* figure. You have the Registry's figure. Let me tell you about
the Registry's figure.

My people had no home to be at. That is not poetry, Warden, that is a fact about berthing rights.
Home was a schedule. When the schedule stopped, my great-grandmother's entire world was in transit —
and the Registry counted the hulls it held manifests for, and it held manifests for the ones that
filed with Terran ports.

We filed with each other.

So the number is wrong. It is wrong downward. It has been quietly corrected downward six times in
sixty years by people who each had a defensible reason, and I do not believe anybody lied. I believe
a great many careful people each made one small decision.

That is worse, and you know it is worse, and you are the only one of yours who has ever said so out
loud in front of me.

Now. The thing you came for.

The *Ninefold Patience* left Kettering on the fourth day of the nine. Freight hull, crew of
thirty-one. My great-grandmother's sister among them. It did not arrive. It went onto the list.

In AU 31 it arrived.

Thirty-one years late. Correct lane. Correct approach. Decelerating normally. Transponder reporting
cheerfully. Hull undamaged — undamaged, Warden, write that down. Not a scratch. No shoal scoring, no
debris pitting. Thirty-one years in a dark that eats everything, and there is not a mark on the
paint.

Empty.

No crew. No remains. Nothing disturbed. Thirty-one meals in the galley in thirty-one stages of
nothing at all, because nothing out there rots.

We have it. We have had it forty-three years, in a berth I will not name, and we have never told your
Registry. I will tell you exactly why. Because the Registry would take it. Not maliciously —
*procedurally.* It would be catalogued, and it would be correct, and it would stop being ours.

Thirty-one names. That is what I am holding.

You are holding several million that nobody will ever name.

So. What have you got?

…

I thought so. Write it down anyway. Somebody should have it, and you are the only one who ever comes
back.

## Notes

**Why it's a Nomad.** Warm, mercantile, sentimental about routes, and the whole testimony is
structured as a *transaction* — free goods first, then the real item, then a price. He asks for
payment, is offered nothing, and gives it away regardless. That last move is the entire faction: they
sell traces for a living and they cannot actually refuse someone who came back.

**The fact.** The *Ninefold Patience* is the file's one physical object, and it is the best
campaign asset in this folder — a real hull, in a real berth, that the player can go and stand
inside. Undamaged is the detail that matters: it did not survive the dark, it was *unaffected* by it.
Something brought it home.

**On the corrected count.** This is the Terran story hook from `01-terran-empire.md` paid off from the
other side. Note that Keth explicitly refuses to call it a lie — *"a great many careful people each
made one small decision"* — which is a much more damning and much more adult account of institutional
failure than a conspiracy would be.

**The single best line about Ilsa** in the whole file is Keth's last one, and it is about her rather
than by her: *you are the only one who ever comes back.* Thirty years of persistence, characterised
in nine words by somebody else.

**VO note.** Fast, warm, generous, a salesman's rhythm. The ellipsis is a real pause — he waits for
an offer and receives silence, and the recovery must be entirely without resentment.

**Art beat.** The berth. A clean freight hull among working ships, in perfect condition, obviously
not in service. And the galley: thirty-one places set.
