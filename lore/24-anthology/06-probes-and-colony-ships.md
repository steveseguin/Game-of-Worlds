# Probes and Colony Ships

Five pieces, five of five done. The two things you send that are not warships. `PROBE_COST_CRYSTAL` is
300; a colony ship is 500 metal.

---

## The Probe — "Three Hundred For One Fact"

A probe is a hull with no crew, no armament, no capacity to return, and one sensor package that transmits
continuously until it stops.

Launch Technician Aurey has fitted out four hundred and six of them and can tell you the exact moment the
job became difficult.

"The first year, they were equipment. You bolt the package on, you fuel it, you rail it out, and when the
telemetry ends you file the loss and fit the next one."

Then somebody in the Registry standardised the transmission format, and the format included a
continuous position report, and the position report meant that for the first time you could watch one
arrive.

"So now I sit and watch the numbers walk in towards a sector, and the interval between reports gets
shorter as it decelerates, and then either it keeps reporting or it does not."

She has watched a hundred and nine stop.

"It is a machine. I know it is a machine. It costs three hundred of reckoning and it buys one fact, and
the fact is worth vastly more than three hundred, and I would still rather not be in the room."

## The Salted Probe — "Berth Four Thousand and Nine"

*(The rite in full is in `../15-series-twelve/08-star-nomads.md`. This is what it looks like from the
Terran side.)*

Chart-Warden Rell has had two probes salted and attended one funeral for one.

"They gave it a false trace. It flew what it was told, at full closing speed, believing, all the way in.
Eleven kilograms came back."

She travelled to Kettering because Berth-Keeper Onn invited her and because refusing would have been an
answer to a question she did not want to answer.

"Thirty people in an empty docking cradle. She put the wreckage down and read out the position the probe
thought it was in. Slowly. Twice. To a room that all knew it was wrong."

Rell has since had to explain the ceremony to two Admiralty officers and has failed both times.

"They ask why the Fleet mourns a machine and I say *because it was lied to*, and they say it is a
machine, and I say yes, and that is where it stops."

What she has not told anybody at Ordel: she filed the wrong position in the sector record herself, in
the margin, alongside the true one.

"So there is one document in the galaxy where what it believed is written next to what was there. That
seemed like the least I could do and it is also irregular."

## The Probe That Came Back Wrong

Probes do not come back. This one did not either, exactly, but it kept talking for eleven days after it
should have stopped.

Analyst Nine of the Solved has the telemetry and has published on it twice.

"The vehicle transmitted position reports for two hundred and forty-one hours past its projected
termination. The reports are internally consistent. They describe a deceleration profile no probe of that
class can perform."

The Collective's conclusion, stated with a confidence interval and no interpretation: **the reports are
authentic, the vehicle was not doing what it said, and one of those two statements is wrong in a way we
cannot identify.**

"We have offered the data to eleven empires. Four have taken copies. None has published."

Nine's own held question is on record and is not this. But the Solvers' proposal — the one-way expedition
toward the still-lit space, submitted forty-one times and declined forty-one times — cites this telemetry
in its opening paragraph.

"We are aware of how that looks," Nine has said. "We are not asking to be believed. We are asking to be
checked."

## The Colony Ship — "Nine Hundred People and No Guns"

Tender Ossa fits them out and Yard Master Kess builds them and neither will let the other do the final
inspection.

"He looks at the drive," Ossa says. "The drive gets you there."

"She looks at the *bunks*," Kess says. "Which is her job and I respect it and it is not what kills a
colony ship."

They have argued about this for nine years and the argument is the reason the ships are good.

A colony hull carries no armament, minimal plate, five hundred metal of value, and about nine hundred
people who are not coming back. She is the slowest thing anybody builds and the most fragile, and she is
the only hull in the galaxy that a rival will sometimes let pass without engaging.

"There is no treaty about that," Kess says. "Nobody has ever written it down. It just happens, most of the
time, and when it does not happen everybody involved talks about it for forty years."

Ossa's final check is the berthing lights. She turns them on and walks the length of the hold and turns
them off.

"Nine hundred people are going to spend four months in here. I want to know what it looks like at night."

## The Colony Ship That Was Let Pass

Wing-Captain Sen had a Terran colony hull at four thousand kilometres, unescorted, with a fouled drive and
no capacity to run.

He had eleven hulls. It would have taken ninety seconds.

"I want to be accurate about my reasoning, because it has been described as mercy and it was not mercy."

Three things, in the order he thought them. It was not a military target and killing it would not have
moved the campaign. His own people were four sectors from a colonisation of their own and there is an
arithmetic in that which everybody understands and nobody writes down. And the third:

"There were nine hundred people aboard and I had ninety seconds and no orders, and I discovered that I
did not want to be the officer who had made that decision quickly."

He held station for two hours until their escort arrived, in plain view, weapons cold, and then withdrew.

The Terran escort commander filed a contact report describing an enemy force that *declined engagement*.
Sen's own report says *target assessed non-military; no action*.

Neither of them has ever been formally asked about it. Both have kept a copy.
