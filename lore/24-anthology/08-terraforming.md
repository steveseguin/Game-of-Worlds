# Terraforming

Five pieces, five of five done. `TERRAFORMING` has five levels in `tech.js`. Per Law 16 it takes
**generations** — the ladder makes a world *reachable*, not habitable, and every level is somebody's
grandchildren.

---

## Level 1 — "The Cheapest Lie On The Form"

Terraforming level one is the difference between a world you cannot stand on and a world you can stand on
in a suit for six hours.

Assessor Tanu has certified forty-one of them and hates the word.

"*Terraforming.* It sounds like we are making a world. We are making a *tolerance*. Level one is a
seal-and-scrub programme — pressure domes, a scrubber chain, water cracked out of whatever ice is
available, and a hard limit on how long a person can be outside."

The form asks for an estimated habitability date. She has been leaving it blank for nineteen years and
being queried for it every time.

"Because the honest answer is *ask my successor's successor* and there is no box that accepts that."

Her one concession: in the margin, on every certification, she writes the same four words.

**Habitable for whose grandchildren.**

Eleven of her forty-one files have come back with the margin note struck through by a different hand. She
writes it again on the next one.

## Level 3 — "The Generation That Does Not See It"

Ovin's father laid the first scrubber chain. Ovin extended it. Ovin's daughter maintains it and will not
see it finished.

"Three of us. Sixty-one years. And the assessment moved from a two to a three."

He describes level three as the point at which the world stops actively trying and starts merely
declining to help. Outdoor work in a mask rather than a suit. Crops in frames rather than tanks. Rain,
occasionally, which the first generation had never seen and which the third finds unremarkable.

"That is the thing nobody prepares you for. My daughter was born into a three. She has never worn a suit.
When I tell her about the domes she is polite about it."

He has stopped resenting this and can say why.

"It is the *point*. If she had to care about the domes we would have failed. The measure of the work is
that the next one finds it boring."

The scrubber chain has a plate on it now. Three names.

## Level 5 — "Nobody Alive"

There are four level-five worlds within reach of anybody and every one of them was finished before the
Lamps went out.

Academician Sarn has visited two. She describes the experience as "professionally humiliating."

"You walk out of the lander without a mask. There is weather that arrived on its own. There is soil
containing things nobody put there. And you are standing on nine hundred years of continuous, funded,
uninterrupted work by people who never once expected to see the end of it."

The Registry holds the Concord terraforming schedules for both worlds. Forty-one volumes. A funding line
that ran, without interruption, across four centuries and eleven changes of government.

"That is what defeats us. Not the technique — we have most of the technique. The *institution*. They
maintained a project for four hundred years and we cannot maintain a survey office through a change of
Registrar."

Her submission on the subject, filed and shelved, closes with one line.

**"A level five is not an engineering achievement. It is evidence that somebody's politics worked for
four hundred years, and that is the technology we have actually lost."**

## The Bioform Objection — "We Do Not Terraform"

The Collective's terraforming access is capped, and Tender Ossa is tired of being asked whether this is a
limitation.

"You put a dome on a world and pump the air you want into it. That is not making a place. That is
*arguing* with a place, forever, and the moment you stop arguing it goes back."

What the Collective does instead takes longer and is not on the tech tree.

"We put something in and we let it decide. Not what we want — what will *take*. In nine seasons you know.
In ninety you have soil. In nine hundred there is a world and nobody is arguing with anything and nobody
has to keep paying."

Asked about the obvious objection — that nobody has nine hundred years.

"Nobody has four hundred either, and you have level fives, so somebody did. You simply gave the work to
an institution instead of to a biosphere." She offers food, which is how these conversations end. "We
gave ours to something that does not need funding."

## The Star Nomad Refusal — "Three Motions Since AU 30"

The Fleet's terraforming cap is a *vote*, not a technology, and Convoy-Master Hallam can name the three
people it cost.

"AU 34, AU 51, AU 66. Three motions to fund a serious terraforming programme. All three failed. Two of
the three sponsors lost their berths."

She has read all three submissions. She thinks all three were correct.

"That is the awkward part. The arithmetic was good every time. We are the largest population in the galaxy
and we have no ground and no standing and no vote in any forum, and a world would fix a great deal of
that."

The counter-argument has never been written down, because it does not survive being written down. It is
delivered in council, verbally, by somebody old, and it is always the same.

*Home was a schedule. We believed a timetable and eleven thousand berths are empty. Wanting a place to be
is what killed everyone we knew.*

"And then the motion fails," Hallam says, "by a wide margin, from people crying. I have voted against it
twice myself and I could not tell you today whether I was right."
