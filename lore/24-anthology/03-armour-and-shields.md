# Armour and Shields

Eight pieces, eight of eight done. Levels from `tech.js`: Reinforced Hulls (5), Reactive Armor (5),
Adaptive Plating (3), Deflector Shields (5), Phase Shields (4).

**Canon constraint that shapes every piece here.** Per Law 4, **kinetic hazard cannot be shielded**. A
shoal at closing speed does not care about deflectors, and the best-shielded hull in the galaxy rolls the
same odds as a frigate. Armour and shields are for what people aim at you. Nothing here may imply
otherwise.

---

## Reinforced Hulls · Lv1 — "Two Centimetres"

The difference between Reinforced Hulls 1 and no armour research at all is about two centimetres of
laminate over the crew spaces, and Rating Deshan is alive because of it.

He was in the forward mess of the destroyer *Diligence of Ordel* — no relation to the cutter class, and
the yard has been asked to stop — when a plasma bolt came through the outer plate at an angle.

It did not come through the laminate. It came *most* of the way through the laminate. Deshan was
eating, and there was a bang, and a section of bulkhead in front of him turned the colour of a stove
element and stayed that colour for six minutes while forty people watched it and nobody said anything.

Then it cooled and it was still a bulkhead.

The damage report lists it as *outer plate breached, inner intact, no casualties.* One line. Level one
armour, the first thing anybody researches, the cheapest entry in the branch.

Deshan requested a transfer to damage control the following week and got it. He has been doing that job
for six years now. When he trains ratings he takes them to the forward mess of whatever ship they are on
and makes them put a hand on the inner bulkhead.

"That," he says. "That is the research."

## Reinforced Hulls · Lv5 — "The Argument at Yard Four"

Yard Four spent two years arguing about the fifth level, because the fifth level is where it stops being
free.

The first four are laminate and geometry. The fifth is *mass*, and mass is speed, and speed is arrival,
and Sweep-Admiral Ito wanted the tonnage in drives instead.

Chief Constructor Vail's submission ran to four pages and the operative sentence was one line: **"a ship
that arrives faster and does not come back has not arrived."**

She won. Twenty-six hulls were refitted over eighteen months, each losing about four per cent of its
speed.

Six of those twenty-six have since been in engagements they would not have survived at level four. Vail
knows the number because she asked for it, formally, and the Admiralty provided it, formally, with a note
observing that the counterfactual is not measurable.

She keeps the note. She also keeps the six ships' names on a card in her desk, which is sentimental, and
which she would deny.

## Reactive Armor · Lv3 — "Ablative"

Reactive plate works by being destroyed on purpose. Gunner Kess had this explained to her four times
before she understood that it was not a euphemism.

"It goes off," said the constructor. "Outward. Into the thing that hit you."

At Vail Margin the cruiser *Assurance* took forty-one hits in six minutes and lost forty-one panels of
reactive plate, each one detonating outward a fraction of a second before the incoming round arrived,
each one turning a penetrating hit into a scorch.

Kess spent the engagement in a gun mount listening to the ship dismantle itself protectively.

"You hear them go. Bang, bang, bang, all down the flank, like somebody running a stick along railings.
And every one of those is a hole that is not in you."

They came home with the flank stripped to bare frame and no hull breach anywhere. The refit took three
weeks and cost more than the engagement was worth.

"People say the armour saved us," Kess says. "The armour did not save us. The armour *left*. In pieces.
On purpose. That is a different thing and I want the difference respected."

## Adaptive Plating · Lv3 — "The Second Shot" ↔

*(Pairs with `04-weapons.md`, "Antimatter Warheads · Lv3 — Butter".)*

Adaptive plating learns. Not quickly, and not much, and only about one thing at a time — but a hull that
has been hit by a particular energy signature is measurably harder to hit with it again, and the
Crystalline Entity have been growing plate that does this for four hundred years.

At Anselm's Reach the Standing hull *Terrace of Long Consideration* took an antimatter warhead amidships
and did not break.

It should have broken. Everybody on both sides expected it to break.

The second warhead, forty seconds later, went into the same quarter and did about a third of the damage
of the first, because by then the plate had *changed* — reorganised, at some cellular level nobody
outside the Standing can describe, into something that had already met this once.

The third did almost nothing.

Elder Tessen's comment, in a communication to the Terran Registry that took two years to arrive: **"We
were surprised twice. The first time by the weapon, and the second time by how quickly we stopped being
surprised. You should consider what that means about the rest of us."**

## Deflector Shields · Lv1 — "Missing"

A deflector does not stop a shot. It makes the shot miss.

Sub-Lieutenant Aurey found this genuinely upsetting for the first six months of her service, because it
means the shield either works completely or does nothing at all, and there is no way to tell which one is
about to happen.

"Armour is a promise. Shields are a *coin*. I can tell you the thickness of my plate. I cannot tell you
whether the next round will find us."

At Kettering approach the frigate *Diligence* — the cutter, this time — took fourteen aimed shots from a
raider and was hit twice.

Aurey did the arithmetic afterwards and could not stop doing it. Twelve misses. At level one deflection,
perhaps three of those were the shield and nine were the enemy shooting badly.

"So I owe my life to the shield about three-twelfths. I have tried to feel three-twelfths of grateful and
it turns out not to be a quantity a person can feel."

## Phase Shields · Lv4 — "The Sound It Makes"

Nobody warns you about the noise.

Phase deflection at the fourth level does something to incoming energy that a hull registers as a
*tone* — a rising note, about a second long, every time a shot resolves as a miss. In a heavy engagement
the note never stops. It just changes pitch as the geometry changes.

Signals Rating Ombra stood eight hours of it at the Vail Shoal action.

"After the first hour you are not hearing shots. You are hearing a chord. It goes up when they close and
down when they open, and every single note in it is somebody trying to kill you and failing, and you
cannot make yourself hear it as good news."

Four hundred and six shots. Eleven hits.

Ombra transferred to a Mechanicus liaison posting the following year, on a ship with no shields at all
and plate a metre thick, and slept better.

"I would rather be hit," she said, "than listen to not being hit."

## Shields versus a Shoal — "The Thing Deflectors Cannot Do"

Captain Sarn of the Standing hull *Long Terrace* had the best shields in the cluster and lost four hulls
of six crossing 8-F.

This surprised her escort commander, a young Terran officer who had read the specifications and drawn a
reasonable conclusion.

Sarn's explanation, delivered at Crystalline speed over about forty minutes, reduces to this:

"A deflector persuades an aimed shot to go elsewhere. It is an argument with a gunner. Gravel is not
aiming. Gravel has no intention to redirect. At crossing speed a stone the size of your thumb arrives
carrying more energy than any weapon on either of our ships and it arrives without having decided
anything."

The escort commander asked what does help.

"Mass. Luck. Owning the sector."

"And of those?"

"Owning the sector," said Sarn. "The other two are not strategies."

## Armour versus Doctrine — "The Second Shift Will Not Discuss Deflectors"

Liaison Fourth was asked, at a Registry technical exchange, why the Mechanicus cap shields at level one.

The transcript is short.

**Q:** Deflectors are demonstrably effective. Your own engagement data shows it.

**A:** Yes.

**Q:** Then why is the branch capped?

**A:** A deflector stops a shot without touching it. Plate stops a shot by being there.

**Q:** That is the same outcome.

**A:** It is the same outcome and it is not the same *fact*. We would rather be hit through good plate
than missed by good fortune. I am aware this is not a technical argument. I have asked three shifts for a
technical argument and been given work orders.

**Q:** So it is superstition.

**A:** It is eleven hundred years of hulls that came back. I do not have a better word for that than the
one you have used, and I would like the record to show that I did not object to it.
