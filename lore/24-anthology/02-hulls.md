# Hulls

Nine pieces, nine of nine done. Ship type ids from `combat.js`; shipyard requirements from `tech.js`.

---

## 1 · Frigate — "What A Frigate Is For"

Nobody writes about frigates, so Ordnance Mate Sesse wrote this in the back of a maintenance log and
somebody found it later.

*A frigate is the cheapest thing that can shoot back. That is the entire specification and everything
else about her follows from it.*

*She has one gun that matters and about forty per cent of the armour anyone would want. Her berthing is
nine bunks in a space designed for six. She has no medical bay; she has a locker with a red cross painted
on it by hand, and the paint is from 8-F, and I did the painting.*

*In four years I have watched frigates do the following. Screen a colony ship through a shoal by going
first. Hold a sector alone for nine turns because there was nothing else to send. Tow a dreadnought that
had lost steering, at four per cent of the dreadnought's speed, for three weeks, successfully.*

*None of that is in the specification.*

*The Registry lists us at Shipyards level nought, which means we are what you can build before you know
anything. I want it in a log somewhere that the ships you build before you know anything are the ones
that are there when it goes wrong.*

## 2 · Destroyer — "Middleweight"

Lieutenant-Commander Ito — no relation to the Sweep-Admiral, and tired of saying so — has commanded
destroyers for eleven years and has a theory about why.

"A frigate is a thing you spend. A cruiser is a thing you commit. A destroyer is neither, and that is
why they give them to officers they have not decided about yet."

The *Ganges Reach* is a destroyer. She has two mounts, real armour, a spine that will take a hit at an
angle, and no capability that is best in class at anything. She can escort, screen, raid, hold, and lose
gracefully, which is five things done adequately by one hull.

In AU 72 she was the only warship inside four sectors when a Swarm element came through 4-C. Ito did not
win. She was not going to win; there were nine hundred of them and one of her.

She held the crossing for eleven turns, retreating one sector at a time, making them pay for each,
until the relief arrived — and then she was towed home with her spine cracked in two places and eleven
of her thirty-one crew unaccounted for.

The Admiralty called it a delaying action. Ito has never used that phrase. She calls it "eleven turns,"
and everyone who was there knows what she means.

## 3 · Scout — "The Cheapest Fact"

A scout has no armour worth naming, one gun for form's sake, and the best sensors any hull carries. It
exists to find out and come back, in that order, and the second part is negotiable.

Survey Officer Wren — a Terran, no relation to the courier, and she has heard the joke — has flown eleven
of them and lost four.

"People think it is a brave posting. It is not. It is an *arithmetic* posting. There are two of us and
three hundred of them on the line behind us, and the sum only works if you accept that the two are worth
less than the three hundred."

She says the hardest part is not the crossings. It is the moment on arrival when the sensor board fills
and she has perhaps forty seconds to decide what matters, because a scout that comes home with
everything is a scout that came home late.

"Shoal, mouth, world, fleet. Four things. Everything else can wait for somebody with a bigger ship and
more time."

The Mechanicus do not build scouts. Wren has read the reason and finds it, in her words, "the single
most expensive economy in the galaxy."

## 4 · Cruiser — "The Ship You Commit"

Captain Vey of the *Forbearance* — a Bioform hull, grown, eleven years old and getting better — did not
want a cruiser and asked twice to be put back on destroyers.

"A cruiser is where it stops being a manoeuvre and starts being a decision. If I take her somewhere, that
is the fleet's weight going there. You cannot make a gesture with a cruiser."

The *Forbearance* is eleven years into a life that may run forty. She breathes on a slow cycle; her
plating is shell rather than plate and it thickens where she has been hit, which means her hull is a
record of every engagement she has survived and Vey can read it like a chart.

"There is a ridge along her port quarter from 8-F. There is a patch under the bow from a Titan mount at
Ordel that should have ended us and did not. She is *better* than she was, which no other navy's ships
do, and it means I cannot replace her. If I lose her I do not get another one for nine years."

Vey has stopped asking to go back to destroyers.

"I did not understand what they had given me," she says. "They had not given me a ship. They had given me
something that will outlive me if I am careful."

## 5 · Battleship — "Line of Battle"

Shipmaster Ordry Vaun does not think battleships are for fighting.

"A battleship is for making a question unnecessary. If she is in the sector, there is no engagement,
because nobody sensible starts one. The best service she does is the war that does not happen in the
sector she is sitting in."

House Vaun builds them heavy, slow and permanent. Nine mounts, plate that would embarrass a fortress,
and a service life specified in centuries rather than years. Shipyards level two, and the Titans regard
level two as the point at which shipbuilding becomes serious.

"The Registry files her under capital. We file her under *works*. She will be in the water when my
granddaughter reads my list aloud, and every year she is running is another year of me, and I would
like that noted."

Ordry has fought one fleet action in a battleship, at Ordel in AU 71. It lasted nine minutes.

"They came in fast and they were very good, and they left. That is the whole engagement. Nobody died. I
have been asked whether I was disappointed."

She was not.

## 6 · Colony Ship — "Five Hundred Metal and Everybody's Grandchildren"

The most expensive hull in the game that cannot shoot.

Tender Ossa has fitted out nine of them and she inspects the berthing before she inspects the drive.

"Everyone looks at the drive. The drive gets you there. The berthing decides whether there is anybody
alive in year four."

A colony ship carries no armament, minimal plating, and about nine hundred people who will not be coming
back. Her hold is seed stock, hydroponic frames, a foundry small enough to be lifted and large enough to
make its own replacements, and eleven months of food, because eleven months is the Terran superstition
and everybody has quietly adopted it.

She is slow. She is fragile. She is the only hull in the galaxy that a rival empire will sometimes let
pass, and nobody has ever put that in a treaty.

"Five hundred metal," Ossa says. "People say it like it is expensive. Five hundred metal is nine
hundred people and everything they will ever build and everybody they will ever have, and it is the
cheapest thing in the catalogue by a distance that is frankly obscene."

## 7 · Dreadnought — "The Thing You Cannot Replace"

There are eleven dreadnoughts in the cluster and Chart-Warden Rell knows where nine of them are at all
times, because it is her job and because losing track of one is how wars start.

Shipyards level three. Twelve mounts. Adaptive plating. A crew of four hundred and eleven. Three thousand
two hundred metal, four hundred and fifty crystal, and a slip with the capacity to take all of that in one
turn — which means a tier-four port, and there are two of those in the cluster.

"A dreadnought does not win battles," Rell says. "A dreadnought *ends* them. She arrives and the
arithmetic changes and somebody withdraws. That is the service. The gunnery is almost incidental."

She has filed one dreadnought loss. The *Perseverance*, at 9-D, in AU 69, to a mouth.

"Four hundred and eleven people and eleven per cent of the fleet's tonnage, and the fourth field on the
form asks for a reason, and the reason is that nobody knew what was there."

She has the manifest in the top drawer rather than the cabinet, which is irregular, and which nobody has
raised with her.

## 8 · Intruder — "Nobody's Favourite"

The Shadow Realm builds the best intruders and will not discuss them, so this is from a Terran who
captured one.

Prize Officer Onn spent four months going over an Unlisted intruder at Ordel Gate with eleven
specialists and a very poor mood.

"There is no armour. There is nowhere for armour to go; the whole interior is signature suppression and
the crew live in what is left, which is not much, and they live there for months."

What unnerved her was the absence of markings. No hull number, no yard plate, no serial on any
component, no name anywhere on eleven thousand tonnes of warship. Somebody built this and declined to
sign it.

"We asked their liaison for the class designation. He said it was a very good question, and then fed us
extremely well for two days, and we came home with the class designation *unknown*, and I have never
enjoyed being obstructed so much in my life."

The captured hull is at Ordel still. It is used for training. Its file is four hundred pages long and
the field for *name* is empty.

## 9 · Carrier — "The Ship That Does Nothing"

A carrier has almost no guns. Ask a gunner what she thinks of carriers and she will tell you they are a
target with a hangar attached.

Ask Flight Officer Pell.

"Nine hulls in the bay. That is what she is. She goes nowhere near the engagement; she sits eleven
minutes out and she opens, and then for two hours she is the only reason anybody comes back, because a
frigate with a cracked spine cannot reach home and it *can* reach me."

Shipyards level three, and the Zephyr and the Bioform cannot build one at all, for reasons that are
about how each of them thinks about the individual hull.

Pell has recovered four hundred and nine ships in eleven years and lost sixty-one.

"Nobody writes about it. There is no medal for retrieval. The battle reports say *sector held, losses
eleven* — and eleven is the number after I got there, and nobody has ever asked me what the number was
before."

She keeps her own tally. It does not go in any file.
