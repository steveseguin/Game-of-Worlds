# The Twelve Wonders, in Myth

Twelve pieces, twelve of twelve done. Shorter than the rest of the anthology on purpose — these are not
scenes, they are the stories each people tells about the thing it is building, in the register that people
tells them in.

**Canon note.** Nobody has completed one. Every myth below is about an object that does not exist yet,
which is what makes them myths rather than histories. Mechanics in `13-wonders/`.

---

## 1 · Terran Empire — The Concord Lamp

*Told at Ordel Gate, to apprentices, on the first day.*

There is a drawer in cabinet forty-one and in the drawer there is a requisition form.

It asks for eleven parts. Nine of them we can make. One of them we can make badly. One of them nobody
alive knows the name of, and the form does not describe it, because the person who filled in the form in
BU 400 knew what it was and did not think to say.

That is the whole of the Lamp programme. Not genius. Not vision. **A form, filled in correctly, by
somebody who assumed we would still be able to read it.**

We can read it. That is the miracle and nobody thinks it is one.

## 2 · Silicon Collective — The Solved Sky

*A changelog entry, appended without ceremony.*

We do not have a myth. We have a projection with a confidence interval and we are aware that this is the
part organics find insufficient.

The engine will not look at the galaxy. It will *derive* it — every observation any of us has ever made,
four centuries of Concord survey data, and the statistical shape of what a system's history permits, until
the map is not seen but concluded.

It will be right.

The part we do not publish, because it is not a result: when it is finished, we will point it at the origin,
and it will answer, and it will publish, because publishing is what it is for.

We have modelled whether we should. We have not been able to agree on the objective.

## 3 · Zephyr Swarm — The Ten Thousand Mouths

*Recited in the nests, in unison, by everyone.*

We do not want the roads back.

The roads were for the ones who count. We have never counted. When the lights went out the others
starved in front of full warehouses because they had one each and could not reach the others, and we did
not starve, because we were already many and already everywhere and already going.

So we will dig a place that makes more of we, faster than the dark can take we, forever.

That is not a Wonder. That is a *trough*.

There will be no ceremony when it is finished. There is nothing to celebrate. There will simply be more of
we tomorrow than there is today, and that has always been the only thing we wanted.

## 4 · Crystalline Entity — The First Thickness

*Grown into a terrace at Sarn's World, to be read in ten thousand years.*

You will want to know who is inside it.

Someone old. Someone who agreed. That is all that is required and all that will ever be recorded, because
the agreeing is the whole of the act and a name would make it a sacrifice, and it is not a sacrifice.

We are made of what the roads were made of. This is not a secret; it is simply a thing nobody has been
willing to say in a room with us in it.

So one of us will be *given*, and will remain — aware, and slow, and useful — and the dark around it will
stop being dark for a distance you would consider small.

And every empire that flies through that light will finally understand what they have been buying from us
by the tonne for six hundred years.

## 5 · Void Walkers — The Whole Recitation

*Not told. Argued about, at every funeral, for forty years.*

The oldest law we have is that what is written can be taken.

The Scriveners say: and what is unwritten is *lost*, and we are losing four in a hundred every year, and
in a lifetime there will be nothing left to take.

The Guild says: then it will be gone honestly, held by people who paid for it, instead of copied by
somebody who did not.

Route-Holder Ash has sixty-one lanes and agrees with the Scriveners and has never said so, because saying
so would split us in a decade and the slower loss is the one she has chosen.

When it is built it will be the greatest archive in the galaxy and the end of what we are.

Both of those. In the same building.

## 6 · Mechanicus — The Endless Yard

*There is no myth. This is the work order.*

**ORDER — no completion condition.**

Extend the plant outward. Build the yards that build the yards. Occupy, fortify, annotate, continue.

Filed as a cost variance by Third Shift, Yard Nine, on the calculation that continuous advance is cheaper
than repeated assessment.

Accepted at the fourth shift. No objection raised at any level.

**There is no instruction to stop.** There is no office that could issue one. When asked, three shifts have
returned work orders.

*(Appended, unauthorised, in a hand nobody has identified: "I have read this eleven times. I would like
somebody to tell me where it ends.")*

## 7 · Bioform Collective — The Mother Grove

*Told slowly, in a berth-house, usually while somebody is eating.*

They will tell you we do not want the roads relit. That is true and it is not the interesting part.

The interesting part is that we do not need them.

Put a thing in the ground at a shoal. Not a fortress — a *seed*. Come back in nine seasons and the gravel
is bound, and in ninety it is soil, and in nine hundred somebody's grandchildren live there and nobody
remembers that it used to kill people.

We have started. We started forty years before the Lamps went out, when the ships stopped taking on the
deep lanes and we rerouted and said nothing.

It will take centuries. Everyone else has a Wonder that takes a decade.

That is why they will win, and why we will be right.

## 8 · Star Nomads — The Reconciled Schedule

*Read aloud in berth four thousand and nine, annually, by whoever is in port.*

Eleven thousand berths, swept and lit, holding nothing, for seventy-four years.

The Registry counted the hulls it had manifests for. We filed with each other, proudly, for nine
centuries, because filing with their ports meant accepting their courts.

So our dead are not in anybody's number.

We are going to close the ledger. Every manifest, every berth assignment, every convoy log, from every
empire, matched against each other until the count is *right* — and it will be a memorial, and it will
also be the most complete map of routes that has ever existed, because a manifest is a record of where
something went.

Terra will not survive us publishing it.

We have thirty-one names in a galley on this rock. We have decided we can live with that.

---

## 9 · The Ancients — The Closing

*Six lines. There is no more.*

There was a road. There was a thing using it.

We closed it, badly, in a hurry, and it cost more than we can say and we have not said it.

It is still closing. It will finish in nine hundred years and we cannot stop it and we cannot speed it.

We could finish it now. Properly. Everywhere. Forever.

It would take everything we have left, and there is not much, and afterwards nobody would ever leave what
they had swept.

We have been deciding for seventy-four years. That is not indecision. That is the arithmetic being what it
is.

## 10 · Quantum Entities — The Unresolved Lane

*Stated, then partly withdrawn, as everything is.*

We would build a road that has not decided whether it is there.

You would use it. Nobody else would find it, because there would be nothing to find until you were on it,
and afterwards there would be nothing to have found.

It would be cheap. It would be invisible.

It would not be *safe* — please write that down, everybody omits it — because gravel does not care where
you probably are, and we have never claimed otherwise and are tired of being told we have.

And one day it would resolve, once, in one direction, and whatever was put across the road would resolve
with us inside it.

We have published that outcome. It is in the set. We would rather not be present for it.

We may not be.

## 11 · Titan Lords — The Weight of Ages

*Read at the annual precedence, by the senior house, standing.*

Forty houses. One drawing. The first time in four hundred years that we have all built the same thing.

She is specified for a thousand years of service. Nobody who signs the plate will see her decommissioned;
nobody who signs the plate expects to. That is the correct relationship between a person and their work
and we will not be lectured about it.

A shoal will be weather to her. A fleet moving in her shadow will not lose a hull. She will arrive late to
every war and there will be no further question of any sector she arrives in.

And when the last of us is forgotten, some Vaun or some Kell will read a list aloud on a Tuesday morning
and she will still be on it.

That is the whole of what we want. We have never wanted anything else.

---

## 12 · Shadow Realm — The Assembled Frame

*Told over an excellent meal, to somebody who will not remember being told.*

Eleven frames. Eleven buyers. Seventy years.

We have been selling the same afternoon in pieces since before your grandmother was born, and nobody holds
enough of it to make a sentence, and that was not caution — that was *pricing*.

Now we are buying it back. All of it. From eleven empires who each know exactly what they have, and who have
all become suddenly very interested in the set.

And when it is whole we will finally be able to read what the Ancients were doing at that relay five years
before they warned anybody.

Do have some more of the bread.

You understand what it costs us, I think. The moment we can read it, so can everybody. And the only thing
that has ever kept us alive is that nobody has counted us.

We are going to do it anyway. I have never been able to explain why, and I have stopped trying, and I
notice that you have not asked.
