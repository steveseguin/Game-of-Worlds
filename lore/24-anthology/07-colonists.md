# Colonists

Six pieces, six of six done. The people the colony ship carries, and what happens after the feed says
*somebody will be born there*.

---

## Year One — "The Manifest Says Nine Hundred"

The manifest said nine hundred and four. Sanne counted eight hundred and eighty-one off the ramp and
spent her first night on the new world trying to find out about the rest.

Twenty-three. Not deaths — the crossing had been clean, they had been lucky, everyone said so. Twenty-three
people who had signed the manifest at Ordel and had not boarded, and nobody at either end had a list of
which twenty-three.

She was the settlement's registrar because she had been a clerk in a bonded warehouse and that was the
nearest qualification anybody had. So she made the list herself, over four months, by asking eight hundred
and eighty-one people who they had expected to see.

Some had changed their minds. Some had been reassigned. One had died in the port two days before lift and
his family had come anyway and nobody had told them there was a form for that.

The list is on the wall of the first structure they built, which is an equipment shed, because they built
the shed before they built anything for themselves.

Twenty-three names of people who are not here and are not dead. Sanne has been asked to take it down
twice.

## Year Two — "Water"

The survey said the water was good. The survey was right. The survey did not mention that the water was
forty kilometres away and downhill.

Ovin had been a pipefitter on Terra, in a Gate, where water arrived because it arrived. He spent his
second year on the new world laying eleven kilometres of pipe uphill with forty people and a winch, and
he will talk about it for the rest of his life.

"You cannot pump water forty kilometres uphill with a colony foundry. You can pump it eleven kilometres
four times, with a tank at each stage, and you can build the tanks out of the crating the seed stock came
in. That is what we did. It took fourteen months."

The fourth tank leaked for a year and a half before they found it.

"People think the hard part of a colony is the danger. It is not. The danger is a fortnight at the start
and then it is *plumbing*, for a decade, and nobody puts plumbing in a story."

The pipe is still there. It has been replaced in sections eleven times and the original stage-two tank is
still the original, and Ovin has requested in writing that it not be replaced while he is alive.

## Year Three — "The Problem Nobody Found"

Every eight-grade world hides something, and Tanu found theirs in the third spring.

The dust. Not toxic — nobody was sick, the assays were clean, and for two years everybody had breathed it
and been fine. But it was fine silicate, and it got into everything with a bearing in it, and in the third
spring the settlement's bearings all failed at once.

Winches. Pumps. The foundry's own lathe. The ventilation.

"Two years of everything working," Tanu says. "And then a fortnight where nothing turned."

They stripped the ventilation to make bearings for the pumps, and stripped the winches to make bearings
for the ventilation, and lost the harvest because there was nothing left to run the irrigation with. Four
hundred people on eleven months of stores that were already six months down.

Nobody died. She wants that said, because the story sounds like people died.

"We ate badly for a year and we sealed every bearing in the settlement in wax by hand and we sent a
survey amendment to the Registry with one line in it. *Fine silicate atmosphere. Seal everything.
Everything.*"

She has since been told the amendment saved a colony at Vail. She has not been told which one.

## Year Six — "The First One Born"

Her name is Alis and she is the reason the feed line exists.

Chart-Warden Rell's copy reads *colony confirmed; somebody will be born there,* and it is the only
sentimental line in the whole event script, and it fires on a database write. Six years later, at the
place that write referred to, Alis was born in an equipment shed with a wall of twenty-three names on it.

Sanne registered her. First entry in a new ledger, and she wrote it twice — once in the settlement book
and once in a letter to the Registry, because a birth off-world has to be filed at the Gate that
authorised the colony or the child has no standing anywhere.

The letter took four months to reach Ordel and eleven to be acknowledged.

The acknowledgement is a printed form with a stamp on it. Sanne framed it. Alis, who is eleven now and
finds her mother embarrassing, has pointed out that it is a receipt.

"Yes," Sanne said. "It is a receipt. It is a receipt from an empire for a person. Do you know how many
people in this galaxy do not have one?"

## Year Eleven — "What We Do Not Tell The Next Ship"

The second colony ship arrives in the eleventh year, and the settlement has four months to decide what
to put in the handover.

Ovin argued for everything. The dust, the bearings, the fourth tank, the harvest they lost, the eighteen
months when the pipe was a rumour. All of it, in detail, so the next nine hundred do not repeat it.

Tanu argued against about a third of it.

"Not because it is untrue. Because they are coming anyway, and they cannot turn round, and there is a
difference between *preparing* somebody and *frightening* them for four months of transit with nothing
to do."

The handover they filed is nine pages. It contains the dust, the bearings, and the pipe. It does not
contain the fortnight when nothing turned, or the second winter, or the argument about the shed.

Ovin signed it. He has never fully agreed with it.

"They will find out," he says. "They will find out in the third spring like we did, and somebody will
ask why it was not in the handover, and the answer will be *because we wanted you to get here first.*"

## Year Forty — "Terraforming Requirement Two"

The Registry file for the world lists it as terraform requirement two, which is a number that means
nothing to anybody who has not lived it.

Alis is forty-five and she can tell you exactly what it means.

"It means the air is survivable and the water is somewhere else and nothing here wants us. Requirement
two is not a hostile world. Requirement two is a world that is *indifferent*, and indifference takes
three generations, because you cannot fight it, you can only outlast it."

She is the settlement's engineer now. Her grandmother's name is on the equipment shed wall, in the column
of twenty-three, because she never boarded — Sanne's mother, who changed her mind at Ordel Gate and never
said why, and whose granddaughter has therefore been born on a world she declined to come to.

"We got the water up. We sealed the bearings. There are eleven thousand of us and the dust is a nuisance
rather than a crisis and last year we exported ore for the first time."

She looks at the shed.

"Forty years to turn a two into a two that works. My daughter might see a three. Nobody alive will see a
nine. That is what the number is. It is not a difficulty rating. It is a *length*."
