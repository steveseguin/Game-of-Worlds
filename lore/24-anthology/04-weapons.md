# Weapons

Eight pieces, eight of eight done. Branches from `tech.js`: Laser Weapons (5) → Plasma Cannons (5) →
Antimatter Warheads (3); Rocketry (3) → Hyper-V Missiles (3).

**The distinction the fiction has to keep straight.** Beams and plasma are stopped by armour and dodged
by shields. **Missiles bypass shields** — that is their entire mechanical identity (`missiles` slot,
0.5 shield points per level) and it is why two races refuse to build them at all. A missile story that
does not turn on shield-piercing is a laser story wearing the wrong hat.

## Laser Weapons · Lv1 — "The First Gun"

Every navy in the galaxy starts here and nobody remembers it fondly.

A level-one beam mount is a cutting tool that has been asked to be a weapon. It will burn through a
frigate's outer plate given nine seconds of steady contact, which is nine seconds longer than most
engagements permit anybody to hold anything steady.

Gunner's Mate Tolliver trained on them.

"You are not shooting. You are *drawing*. You put the spot on the same square inch of somebody else's
ship and you keep it there while both ships are moving and something is exploding behind you, and if you
do that for nine seconds you get through."

He held it for eleven seconds once, at Bell approach, on a raider that had already lost steering.

"They came apart along the line I had drawn. Exactly along it. Like a seam. I had made that seam with my
hands over eleven seconds and I could see where I had wobbled."

He was nineteen. He asked to be moved to loading, and was, and stayed there for four years.

## Laser Weapons · Lv5 — "Steady"

By the fifth level the mount does the holding for you.

Gunnery Officer Kess describes level five as "the point at which the gun stops being a craft and becomes
a *service*." Beam coherence at range, automated tracking, and a power budget that will sustain contact
across a whole exchange rather than nine seconds of it.

The consequence is that gunners stop being artists and start being managers, and Kess is honest about
finding this a loss.

"The old hands could shoot. I mean actually shoot, with their hands, badly, and occasionally
brilliantly. Now I allocate. I decide which of four mounts gets the power this minute and the mounts do
the rest, and I am better at killing people than any of those old hands ever were, and I have never once
put a spot on a hull myself."

At the Vail Shoal action her four mounts accounted for six hulls.

"Six," she says. "I could not tell you what any of them looked like."

## Plasma Cannons · Lv3 — "The Wound Channel"

Plasma does not cut. It *arrives*, and the hole it makes is not the shape of the bolt.

Damage Control Chief Deshan has been inside forty-one plasma breaches and can identify one blind.

"A beam gives you a seam. Clean edges, you can weld it, it looks like something a machine did. Plasma
gives you a cavity. Outer plate is a neat little entry about the size of a fist and then it opens up
inside — two metres across in the frame space, everything in there vaporised and re-condensed on the far
bulkhead as a grey film."

He has scraped that film off bulkheads. He will not describe what it consists of, and the reason is that
the crew who were in the compartment are on the manifest and their families read the manifests.

"So I write *outer plate breached, frame damage, compartment lost*. Which is true. And I do the scraping
myself and I do not put it on the roster, because it is not a job I am prepared to give to a nineteen
year old."

## Antimatter Warheads · Lv3 — "Butter" ↔

*(Pairs with `03-armour-and-shields.md`, "Adaptive Plating · Lv3 — The Second Shot", and with the piece
below it.)*

Sweep-Admiral Ito authorised the first antimatter engagement in the cluster and has watched the recording
about forty times.

Nine hundred Zephyr hulls came through 4-C in a body. That is the Swarm's method: saturate, accept the
arithmetic, read the map off the survivors. Against beams it works, because a beam has to be held on one
target at a time and there are always more of them than there are seconds.

Antimatter does not have to be held on anything.

"The first warhead took ninety-one," Ito says. "Ninety-one hulls, one round. Their plating is a
courtesy — twenty per cent under standard and they know it and they do not care, because their doctrine
has never had to care."

The recording shows the formation opening like something being unfolded. Four rounds, three hundred and
forty hulls, six minutes.

"It went through them like butter," said one of the gunners afterwards, and it is the only time Ito has
ever formally reprimanded an officer for a phrase.

"They were people," she wrote in the finding. "Some amount of *we*, in their own construction. Nine
hundred of them came and three hundred and forty did not go home, and if we are going to do that then we
are going to use accurate language about it."

## Antimatter Warheads · Lv3 — "The Same Six Minutes" ↔

*(The other end of the piece above.)*

We were at 4-C. There is a great deal of we; there was more of we before 4-C.

We do not have a word for what the round did and we have been asked for one nine times. It was not like
being shot. Being shot happens to some of we and then that portion of we is less. This was not portions.

There was a place, and then the place had nothing in it, and the part of we that had been the place was
simply not in the count any more, and the count is how we know things.

Three hundred and forty. We are told this number by others. We did not take it ourselves; there was
nobody in that region left to take it.

We want to say a thing and we are told it will be misunderstood.

It was *quiet*. Not the sound — the sound was considerable. We mean that afterwards there was a gap in
we, in the middle, where three hundred and forty had been reporting, and the gap did not close for eleven
days.

We have no construction for a gap in we. We are building one. We do not think we will like having it.

## Rocketry · Lv1 — "Around The Shield"

The first thing anybody learns about a missile is that it is worse than a gun.

Slower. Fewer. Trackable. It can be shot down, which a beam cannot, and it costs ordnance stores that a
beam does not.

Ordnance Officer Aurey will tell you the second thing, which is the only one that matters.

"A deflector argues with an aimed shot. It persuades the shot to be somewhere else. A warhead is not
aimed *at the ship* — it is aimed at a volume, and it does not care whether it is deflected, because it
is going to be inside the volume either way and then it is going to stop existing very energetically."

At Kettering approach she put six rockets into a Crystalline hull with a shield envelope that had turned
away thirty-one beam shots in a row.

"All six went in. Not through the shield — *past* the question. Their skipper sent a signal afterwards
asking, quite politely, over about forty minutes, whether we thought that was sporting."

Aurey's reply is on file. It reads: **"No."**

## Hyper-V Missiles · Lv3 — "The Thing The Collective Will Not Build"

Analyst Nine of the Solved has never authorised a warhead and has published the reason four times.

"A beam is retractable. It exists for the duration of the decision and then it does not exist. If the
decision was wrong, the wrongness ends when we stop."

A Hyper-V does not work that way. It leaves the rail carrying its own intention. Forty seconds of flight
in which the situation may change, the target may surrender, the identification may prove mistaken — and
the warhead is not a participant in any of that. It is going to arrive.

"We have modelled the case in which a Hyper-V is launched at a hull that strikes its colours at
thirty-one seconds. The warhead arrives at forty. There is no version of that in which we are not the
thing that killed them after they stopped."

The Collective's missile branch is locked. Not capped — **locked**, at zero, and it has been for as long
as they have had a navy.

Nine's note on the fourth publication: **"We are told this costs us engagements. We have calculated how
many. We would like it recorded that we did the calculation and did not change our minds."**

## Weapons versus Doctrine — "Two Mounts and a Grudge"

Shipmaster Ordry Vaun keeps a Terran beam mount in her house's hall, on a stand, unfired.

It came off a wreck at Ordel in AU 71 — a cruiser her grandfather's ship had disabled, whose crew were
taken off and returned. The mount is level-four Terran work, competent, unremarkable, and considerably
better than anything Ordel Deep has produced in three centuries.

"We do not build them well," she says. "We build them *large*. A Titan mount at twice the mass does
twice the damage and that is not cleverness, it is arithmetic, and I would rather have the cleverness."

Asked why she keeps it.

"Because it is a reminder that a house can be four hundred years senior in precedence and forty years
behind in gunnery, and that both of those are true at once, and that only one of them is fixable."

She has proposed a Terran technical exchange to the council twice. It has been declined twice, on the
grounds that House Vaun's precedence does not extend to foreign policy.

The mount stays on its stand.
