# Leadership

Seven pieces, seven of seven done. Four from the top, three from inside the mass — and the pairs are the
point. **↔** marks the same event seen from both ends.

---

## From the top · "The Number She Signs" ↔

Registrar Vance signs a mobilisation order for eleven thousand people and it takes her about four
seconds.

She has thought about the four seconds. She thought about them for a whole year, early on, and then
stopped, because thinking about them did not improve the orders and did make her slower, and slower is
how sectors are lost.

What she does instead is this: before she signs, she reads one manifest at random from the last
engagement. Not a summary — an actual list, with names and berths and next of kin. It takes about eleven
minutes and it is the only part of her day that is not on the schedule.

"It does not make me a better Registrar," she has said, exactly once, to exactly one person. "It makes me
a Registrar who has recently read a list. Those are not the same and I am aware of the difference. I do
it anyway."

Then she signs, in four seconds, and the eleven thousand go.

Sweep-Admiral Ito thinks the manifest reading is self-indulgent and has said so to her face. She agreed
with him and did not stop.

## From the ranks · "The Number They Are" ↔

*(The other end of the piece above.)*

Rating Deshan has been mobilised four times and has never once wondered who signed it.

"People ask that. Officers ask that, mostly — *do you resent the people who send you*. And the honest
answer is that there is no *people* in it. There is a posting. The posting arrives on a Tuesday and it
says a sector and a date and a berth, and then you are in the berth."

He has read that the Registrar reads a manifest before signing. He has an opinion about it and it is not
the expected one.

"Good. She should. Not for us — it does nothing for us, we are already in the berth. For *her*, so that
the next one is not easier than the last one. The day it gets easy for her is the day the postings stop
making sense."

Asked whether he would like to meet her.

"No. What would we say? She would want to tell me it was necessary and I already think it was necessary.
That is a very boring conversation for two busy people."

He has kept every posting slip. Four of them, in a tin.

## From the top · "Ito Loses The Argument"

Sweep-Admiral Ito has been proposing the same trade for nine years and has lost the vote nine times.

Archive access to the Star Nomads, in exchange for four centuries of route knowledge. The arithmetic is
not disputed by anybody, including Vance. It would roughly double Terran reach inside a decade.

It would also end the Registry's monopoly on certification, which is the only real power Terra has.

"I am not wrong," he says. "I want that understood. I have never been wrong about the trade. I have been
*outvoted*, nine times, by people who are also not wrong, about a different thing."

What he does not say publicly, and what his staff know: after each vote he writes to the Nomad council
himself, personally, to tell them it failed and why.

He is not required to. It costs him standing every time — it reads as disloyalty to some of the
Gates — and he does it because Keth's people have had seventy-four years of being decided about by an
office that does not explain itself, and Ito has decided that they will at least hear it from a person.

"Losing well is a competence," he says. "Nobody teaches it. It is most of what command is."

## From the ranks · "The Officer Who Was Wrong"

Gunner's Mate Tolliver served under a commander who made a bad call at 4-C and he will not name her.

"She took us through a crossing on a trace that was eight years old. She had a fresher one available and
it cost crystal and she was under a schedule. Four hulls went in and two came out and I was on one of the
two."

He has been asked, in three separate hearings, to characterise the decision.

"It was wrong. I have said that every time. It was also *the decision that was in front of her*, and I
have watched four other officers make it since, and two of them got away with it, and nobody convened a
hearing about those two."

What he minds is not the call.

"She never said anything. Not to us. There was a finding and a note in her file and she was moved to
another command and we got a new commander, and nobody ever stood in front of the surviving forty of us
and said *I did that*."

He is a Chief now, with ratings of his own. He has made one bad call so far, at Bell approach, costing a
hull and no lives.

He stood in front of them the same evening and said it.

## From the top · "The Second Shift Cannot Lead"

There is no Mechanicus leadership piece, because there is no Mechanicus leader. This is the nearest thing.

Liaison Fourth was asked by a Terran delegation who it should address regarding a border question. The
exchange is preserved because the delegation's secretary was thorough.

**Delegation:** We would like to speak to whoever holds authority for the northern frontier.

**Fourth:** The shift holding the task holds the authority. The task is survey-by-occupation. It is held
by Fourth Shift, Yard Eleven.

**Delegation:** Then we will address Fourth Shift.

**Fourth:** The task completes in nine weeks. The authority completes with it.

**Delegation:** And then who holds it?

**Fourth:** Whoever is issued the next task. It has not been issued. It will be issued when the previous
one completes.

**Delegation:** So there is nobody to make a treaty with.

**Fourth:** There is nobody to make a treaty with. There is somebody to give a job to. I have found that
organics experience these as very different and I have never understood why. A job is honoured. I am told
treaties frequently are not.

## From the ranks · "Twelve Thousand of We"

We are asked about leadership and we do not have the word, so we will answer the question we think is
underneath it.

Where the most of we are, that is where the deciding happens. Not because it is chosen. Because that is
where the signal is thickest and the answer resolves fastest. This season we are thickest in the northern
basins, so this season we are expansionist, and next decade the ground will open and we will be thickest
in the south and we will be careful, and nobody will have changed a mind.

Others find this frightening. We have been told it is frightening nine times, always by people who have
one of them each.

Here is what we do not understand about you.

You have a person who decides. That person can be wrong, and can be tired, and can be replaced by a
different person who decides differently, and the whole of we would go where that one told it.

We have geology. Geology is not clever. Geology has never once been *tired*.

We think you would like ours better and we think you would not be able to bear it.

## From the top and the ranks at once · "Rell Files The Reprimand"

*(This is the Act One debrief from `../22-act-one.md`, in the form the anthology uses, because it is the
only piece in the folder where both ends are in the room.)*

The navigator decelerated inside an unswept crossing without authority and brought back three of four
where the plot said two.

Chart-Warden Rell wrote the commendation in section six and the reprimand in section four, on the same
form, in the same hand, in about eleven minutes.

Both are true. That is not a compromise; it is the whole of the job. Section six says what she saw and
section four says what she did about it without asking, and an institution that records only one of those
is an institution that will get the next navigator killed.

Sesse asked, at the end, why Rell looked pleased.

"I do not look pleased. I look like somebody who has just been handed a fact for the price of one hull
instead of two."

The reprimand followed Sesse for the rest of her service, which was four months.

The commendation is in the same file. Nobody has ever read it, because nobody reads section six of a
form belonging to somebody who is dead.

Rell knows that. She wrote it anyway. She has written eleven.
