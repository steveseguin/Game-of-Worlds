## Metal Extractor — "Fifty Metal and Twenty Crystal"

The cheapest thing you can build, and the reason there is anything else.

Foreman Ovin has stood up nineteen of them on eleven worlds and the procedure has not changed in his
lifetime: find the seam, sink the shaft, bolt the head down, walk away. Four days if the ground is
cooperative. Nine if it is not.

"People upgrade past it and stop seeing it. There is an extractor on every world I have ever worked and
nobody photographs one."

What he wants understood is the second sentence of the specification, which nobody reads: an extractor
is designed to be repaired by whoever happens to be standing next to it.

"No trained crew. No parts you cannot make in a colony foundry. Every fastener on the head is the same
size, which is a decision somebody made in the Concord and which has probably saved more colonies than
any warship ever built."

He has one at home. Not a working one — a head plate off the first he ever raised, on a wall, with the
serial visible.

"My daughter asks why. I tell her it is because everything else we have is downstream of it, and she
does not believe me, and she is eleven."

## Crystal Refinery — "What Reckoning Is"

A refinery does one thing: it turns crystal into the substance you burn to know where you are.

Refiner Tanu has explained this to visitors about four hundred times and has settled on a demonstration
rather than a description. She takes them to the output floor and shows them a canister.

"That is a probe. Not the parts of a probe — *that* is a probe, the whole cost of it, three hundred of
this. And that" — a second canister, smaller — "is one fleet moving one sector."

Then she tells them where it comes from, which is the part that changes the room.

"Highest grade in the galaxy comes from Sarn's World. It is quarried out of regions the Standing have
determined to be inert. Determined. By elders. Slowly."

Somebody always asks the question and she always gives the same answer.

"I do not know whether they are right. Neither do they. Elder Tessen made a determination in AU 31 and has
spent forty years unable to find out, and every canister on this floor is downstream of somebody's
decision about whether a place was still a person."

She keeps refining. Eleven empires cannot navigate without her floor.

## Research Academy — "Sixty Metal, Forty Crystal, and Four Hundred Years"

An Academy does not discover anything. Analyst Nine has made this point in four submissions and nobody
enjoys it.

"You are not producing knowledge. You are *recovering* it. Every technology in the tree was standard
under the Concord. There is no branch on it that somebody was not routinely using while your
great-grandmother was alive."

Academician Sarn — Terran, no relation to the Elder, and she has heard it — agrees and finds it
motivating rather than deflating.

"Nine is correct and Nine is also missing something. We are not rebuilding it from nothing. We are
rebuilding it from *documents*, and the documents assume a reader who knows things we do not, and closing
that gap is real work even if the answer is already written down."

Her Academy at Vail Gate holds four thousand Concord technical papers. Eleven of them are fully
understood.

"Eleven. In seventy-four years. And every one of those eleven is a technology on the tree that an empire
can now build, and the other three thousand nine hundred and eighty-nine are sitting there being
legible and incomprehensible at the same time."

She has requested funding for forty more researchers nine times.

## Spaceport — "Tier Four"

Twelve hulls at tier one. Forty-eight at tier four. That is the whole progression and every yard master
in the galaxy will tell you the number that matters is not the capacity.

Yard Master Kess runs a tier-four port at Ordel Gate.

"Capacity is what the Registry measures. What actually changes at tier four is that you can build two
different things at once without one of them waiting. At tier one a colony ship stops your entire
military programme for a turn."

The port cost two thousand eight hundred and fifty metal and nine hundred crystal to reach tier four — the
original build plus three upgrade steps, over nine years, each step gated on a Shipyards research level
first.

"So it is not a building. It is a *decade*. And the thing nobody tells you is that every increment took
the port offline for part of a turn while we cut the new slip, so four times in nine years we could not
build anything at all, and each time somebody senior asked me whether it was worth it."

It is the largest working yard in the cluster and it has never once been ahead of demand.

## Orbital Turret — "Eighty Metal, Sixty Crystal, and the Argument"

A turret cannot move, cannot pursue, and will fire in anger perhaps twice in its service life.

Battery Officer Deshan has commanded eleven of them and considers this the finest posting in the service,
which almost nobody agrees with.

"A warship is a decision. It goes where it is sent. A turret is a *promise* — it is the empire saying to
nine hundred colonists that somebody thought about them when there was no fleet nearby."

His battery at 6-B fired once, in AU 71, at a Shadow raider that had come for the reckoning stores. Four
shots. It hit nothing.

The raider left.

"That is the engagement. Four shots, no hits, sector held. There is no battle report because there was no
battle, and the Admiralty's assessment of my battery's effectiveness that year was *nil*."

The Bioform Collective cannot build turrets at all — the orbital branch is locked to them, permanently,
by doctrine. Deshan has thought about this a great deal.

"They think a fixed emplacement is a kind of death. I think it is the only apology an empire ever makes
in advance."

*Deshan is right, and `15-they-could-not-be-moved.md` is the morning he is right about — told by the
costing deputy who wrote **no demonstrated engagement value** about the northern chain in AU 63 and had
to watch it work. Read this piece first. It is the argument; that one is the bill.*

## Warp Gate — "Two Hundred Metal, a Hundred and Fifty Crystal, and a Confession"

The most expensive structure in the catalogue and the only one that is an admission.

Chief Constructor Vail has raised three and describes the third the way people describe a difficult
relative.

"It is a single span. One end here, one end there, and between them a corridor that behaves — a little,
badly, over a short distance — the way a lane behaved when the Lamps were lit."

She has read the Concord maintenance schedules. Everybody in orbital engineering has.

"A Trellis lane ran for nine hundred years, unattended, across the whole galaxy, and nobody who used it
could tell you how. My gate goes two sectors, needs a technician on each end, and consumes reckoning at a
rate that would fund a small navy."

At the commissioning of the third one, somebody from the Registry asked her for a comment for the file.

"I said: *this is the best we can do, and it is a hundredth of what we inherited, and I would like the
record to show that we are not proud, we are relieved.*"

They did not print it. She has a copy.
