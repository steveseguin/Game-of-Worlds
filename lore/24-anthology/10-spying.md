# Spying

Five pieces, five of five done. The Intel branch: Espionage Networks (8 levels), Counter-Intelligence (8).
Per Law 8 this is **human** — agents, bribes, bought charts. Never clairvoyance. You are stealing maps.

---

## Espionage · Lv2 — "What It Actually Is"

Intelligence Officer Onn has run networks for nine years and spends most of her briefings correcting the
same assumption.

"They think it is listening. It is not listening. There is nothing to listen to — the Whisper is
encrypted where it matters and open where it does not, and nobody transmits a fleet position."

What it is: eleven people on the payroll at a rival's port, of whom four are clerks, three are
longshoremen, two are in a customs office, one is a chandler's bookkeeper, and one is a woman who cleans a
yard office at night.

"The bookkeeper is worth more than the rest combined. Fleet movements do not appear in orders; they appear
in *provisioning*. Nobody sends a fleet anywhere without buying four hundred tonnes of preserved fish
first."

Her single best product in nine years came from the cleaner, who noticed that a wall chart in a yard
office had been changed.

"She could not read it. She did not need to. She told me which sectors had been *rubbed out*, and rubbed
out means reassigned, and reassigned means a fleet is going somewhere else."

## Espionage · Lv6 — "Reading Their Charts"

At the higher levels you stop buying gossip and start buying paper, and Onn describes the transition as the
point where the work becomes morally uncomfortable rather than merely illegal.

"A chart is a survival. Somebody flew that crossing and paid for it in hulls and wrote down what it cost.
When I buy a copy, I am taking a thing that a stranger's crew died for and I am getting it for money."

She has acquired four hundred and eleven traces in nine years. She can name the engagement that produced
about forty of them, because rival navies file loss reports and she reads those too.

"There is a Void Walker lane in my cabinet that cost nine of theirs. I know the hull names. I did not pay
their families. I paid a chandler's bookkeeper eleven hundred and got a copy."

Her recommendation, submitted twice and declined twice, is that acquired traces be *credited* in the
Registry — the source engagement noted alongside the route.

"Not for their benefit. They will never see it. For ours. So that when somebody in ninety years crosses
safely, the file says who bought it."

## Counter-Intelligence · Lv4 — "Salting"

At sufficient advantage you stop intercepting probes and start *feeding* them, and Counter-Intelligence
Officer Pell wants the distinction understood precisely because his own service keeps getting it wrong.

"We do not shoot them down. We could not; a probe is small and fast and cheap and there are always more.
What we do is much easier. We make sure the chart it is flying is one we wrote."

The craft is in the plausibility. A false trace has to be *good* — internally consistent, matching real
survey data at the edges, filed where a rival's intelligence will find it and trust it.

"Mine take about six weeks each. I have drawn forty-one and had eleven taken."

Then the probe flies what it was told, at closing speed, believing, into a shoal that is exactly where the
chart says there is a corridor.

"It is legal. Every admiralty in the galaxy has accepted that. I have the finding on my wall."

He also has, on the same wall, a copy of a Star Nomad funeral order, obtained privately, for a probe he
salted in AU 71.

"They buried it. Read out the position it thought it was in, twice, to thirty people. I have not been able
to throw that away and I have not been able to explain why to my own service."

## Counter-Intelligence — "Against The Second Shift"

The Shadow Realm's entire intelligence apparatus is inert against the Mechanicus and Pell has the transcript
that proves it.

He approached Liaison Fourth with a prepared package: a false trace, a plausible provenance, and a channel
by which the Second Shift would "discover" it.

**Fourth:** I have received this. What is it for?

**Pell:** It is survey data for the northern margin. We thought it might be of use.

**Fourth:** We do not survey. There is no shift holding a survey task and no pattern for a survey hull.

**Pell:** Then perhaps for routing.

**Fourth:** The advance does not route. It proceeds. The order does not contain a preference between
sectors.

**Pell:** *(after a pause)* Do you understand what I have just tried to do?

**Fourth:** No. I have asked eleven empires what a scout is for and I understand that this is related. I
would like you to explain it, if you are able. Nobody so far has been.

Pell's own note on the file: **"Six weeks' work, delivered to a civilisation with no capacity to be
deceived because it has no capacity to be informed. I am filing this under lessons and I would like it
read."**

## Being Spied Upon — "The Bookkeeper"

Deputy Warden Halloway found the leak in eleven days and it took her four years to stop being angry about
who it was.

The chandler's bookkeeper at Ordel Gate. Forty-one years in the post. A man she had signed provisioning
forms with, personally, several hundred times.

"He was not ideological. He was not blackmailed. He was paid eleven hundred a quarter, for nine years, and
what he sold was not secrets — he sold *fish orders*. Four hundred tonnes of preserved fish, delivered to a
yard, on a date."

From which a competent analyst derives a fleet's departure, size and probable destination, and Halloway has
since read the analyses and describes them as "better than our own."

He was not prosecuted. There was no statute that fitted, and the finding recommended a change to
provisioning confidentiality that was adopted across four Gates.

"So he improved our security. That is the outcome. He is retired, at Ordel, on a pension we pay, and I
walk past his house."

Asked whether she has spoken to him.

"Once. I asked him whether he had understood what the orders meant. He said no, and I believed him, and
that is the part I cannot put down. It was not betrayal. It was *bookkeeping*, and it cost us a cruiser at
Vail, and there is nowhere on any form to write that down."
