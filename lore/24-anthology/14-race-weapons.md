# The Signature Weapons, in Myth

Twelve pieces, twelve of twelve done. Each race's exclusive weapon or hull from `13-wonders/`, told the way
that people tells it about itself. Shorter than the anthology's usual form; these are boasts, warnings and
liturgy rather than scenes.

**All are marked DESIGN PROPOSAL** — the mechanics behind them are unbuilt and uncosted. The fiction is
free; the balance is not.

---

## 1 · Terran Empire — the Chart

They will tell you we have no weapon. Let them.

Every other empire's strength lives in a hull. Ours lives in cabinet forty-one, and it is this: **we have
never lost the same crossing twice.** A Walker's route dies with her. A Swarm learns it again every
generation. We wrote it down, and the writing outlived the writer, and there are four hundred survivals in
that cabinet belonging to people none of us met.

The *Assurance*-class carries a copy. Not a gun — a **copy**. If everything else in the sector dies, the
chart comes home.

That is the weapon. It has never killed anybody and it has decided four wars.

---

## 2 · Silicon Collective — Predictive Deflection

We are worst in our first engagement with you and we would like you to notice how that sentence ends.

The second is harder. The fourth is very hard. By the sixth we are not deflecting your shots, we are
**anticipating your habits** — you have three, we have modelled two, and the third will not last the
afternoon.

There is no cleverness in this. You are a person, and people are consistent, and consistency is a
distribution, and a distribution is a firing solution.

We publish our projected losses before every engagement, including this one, so you may check us
afterwards. You will find we were accurate.

We would prefer you found that reassuring. Most do not.

---

## 3 · Zephyr Swarm — Saturation

Each of we is not much. There are a great deal of we.

Your gun holds one of us at a time for nine seconds. We are not nine. We are ninety thousand, and the
arithmetic is the whole of the weapon: your gunner is very good and there is one of her.

The Mote costs almost nothing and dies to almost anything and **counts**. Four hundred Motes into a shoal
is the cheapest chart in the galaxy. Four hundred Motes into your line is not a tactic. It is a *rate*.

We are told this is horrifying. We have asked which part.

Nobody has been able to say.

---

## 4 · Crystalline Entity — the Ringer

Strike our world and it holds the note for hours.

We built nothing to do that. It is simply what we are, and it took four hundred years to understand that a
thing which rings can be *aimed*.

A Ringer's shot does little on arrival. That is not the shot. The shot is the tone, going into your hull
and staying there, and doing on the fourth round what it did not do on the first, and on the ninth what it
did not do on the fourth.

You will withdraw and it will continue.

We are very slow and we have very few guns and we cannot improve them. We do not need to improve a thing
that does not stop.

---

## 5 · Void Walkers — Deceleration

Everything I was taught says do not slow down inside a crossing.

Everything I was taught is why I am the only one who flew the Anselm lane and came back.

Two seconds. That is what slowing buys — two seconds of borrowed sight in a place where nobody has any, and
two seconds at that speed is a *long look*. The Guild calls it a hazard to the formation and they are
right, and they have written up nine of us for it, and every one of those nine came home.

The Bell–Anselm carries no gun at all. Her weapon is that she is already gone.

Ask me again in twenty years when you have something to trade.

---

## 6 · Mechanicus — the Plate Barge

Pattern Four goes first.

Not bravely. Bravery is not a specification. She goes first because she is the largest and the shoal takes
what it takes and there is more of her to take it from, and the hulls behind her arrive.

She loses plate on every crossing. Plate is filed as scheduled labour. Twelve hulls returned for reshaping
is not a casualty report; it is an inventory movement, and the material continues.

Your fleets carry screens to warn you what is ahead.

We carry a screen to be hit by it.

Continue.

---

## 7 · Bioform Collective — the Elder

There is one. There is only ever one.

She was a thin one, in a berth, eleven years ago, and somebody put her there by hand and tended her every
season, and she is now the best animal in the eastern fleet and she is getting better.

Your veterans are old machines. Ours is an old *creature*. Her plating thickens where she has been struck,
so her hull is the record of every engagement she has survived, and any tender can read it.

Kill her and we do not build another. We *raise* one, and it takes nine years, and there is no shipyard in
the galaxy that can shorten that by a day.

So we are careful with her. You should be careful of her. Those are different sentences and we mean both.

---

## 8 · Star Nomads — the Slow Consort

She is the least efficient hull in the Fleet and no convoy will sail without one.

She carries the wall of names. That is her cargo — engraved, in the main passage, where everybody has to
walk past it.

And this is the weapon, and nobody outside understands it as one: while she is in the sector, **what we lose
there is written down.** Not mourned. *Recorded.* Every hull that does not come home becomes a fact about
that crossing, and a fact about a crossing is a trace, and a trace is the most valuable object in this
galaxy.

Every other empire pays for its dead twice — once in the dying and once in learning nothing.

We only pay once.

---

## 9 · The Ancients — the Precursor

There is one. There was never going to be more than one.

She was built before anyone now living, by people we maintained and did not know, and we cannot make
another, and we have never claimed we could.

She will end any engagement she attends. That is not a boast; it is the reason we do not attend them. Every
shot she fires is subtracted from a total that only goes down, and we know the total, to the unit, and it is
smaller than you think.

We have not lost her.

If we lose her there will be no announcement. You will simply notice, after some years, that she has
stopped appearing at councils, and you will not be told why, and you will be correct in what you conclude.

---

## 10 · Quantum Entities — Perhaps

We would tell you what she does, if it had resolved.

She is in the engagement. She may not be in this round. Next round she may have been in this one after all,
which is not the same claim and we are aware of how that sounds.

Her expected damage is excellent. Her actual damage in any given battle is a coin sequence, and we plan
against the expectation, which every other admiralty considers a form of madness and which is simply
arithmetic done honestly.

And when she is destroyed she will not have been carrying what your telemetry recorded, so your next
engagement will be planned on a fleet that never existed.

Please do not sigh. You have sighed.

---

## 11 · Titan Lords — the Deep Keel

Anything smaller would be part of that shoal.

That is the whole of the doctrine and I have never found it necessary to elaborate. The Forbearance class
masses what she masses. Gravel at closing speed is ordnance to a frigate and it is *weather* to her, and
she crosses what other fleets route around, at three-fifths of everybody's speed, arriving after the
question has been asked.

There will be no further question of the sector.

You will forgive us for not attending in the season you would prefer. We will arrive when we arrive. She is
specified for a thousand years and she will be read aloud from a list on a Tuesday morning long after every
hull in your navy has been broken up for frame stock.

---

## 12 · Shadow Realm — Courtesy

When we win, we send you a report.

It is beautifully formatted. It has our losses and yours, the sector, the duration, the hull classes
engaged. Your staff will file it. Your analysts will build on it. Your admiral will plan the next
engagement from it.

It is wrong.

Not a lie — we do not lie, it is crude and it is traceable and it is beneath a host. It is *a different
question, answered generously, at length.*

You have never known how many of us there are. Your Chart-Warden writes *between eleven and thirty-one* and
everybody above her reads the top of it, and that estimate is the only armour we have, and it is made
entirely out of your own imagination.

Do have some more of the bread.
