# Battle

Seven pieces, seven of seven done. **↔** marks a matched pair: the same engagement from both ends.

---

## "Six Minutes at Vail Margin" ↔ *(the winning side)*

Captain Aurey had twenty-two hulls and the enemy had eighteen, and she has never described the engagement
as a victory in conversation, only in writing.

It went the way they mostly go. Two minutes of closing during which nothing happens and everybody is
extremely busy. Ninety seconds of exchange. Then a period of about three minutes in which the outcome is
already decided and nobody involved knows it yet, and people continue to die in a battle that has finished.

"That is the part," she says. "Not the shooting. The three minutes afterwards, before the withdrawal
order reaches them, where their gunners are still working and my gunners are still working and it is over
and it does not stop."

Six minutes. Enemy losses seven, hers three.

The feed reported *sector held; their losses exceed ours*. She read it in her cabin with both manifests
open, which is a thing Chart-Wardens do and captains are not supposed to.

"Seven and three. I have signed off on worse arithmetic and slept fine. What I cannot get past is that
three of theirs died in the last ninety seconds, after their own admiral had decided to leave."

---

## "Six Minutes at Vail Margin" ↔ *(the other end)*

Wing-Captain Sen ordered the withdrawal at four minutes and it reached his rearmost squadron at five and
a half.

He knows exactly what that ninety seconds cost, because it is the only part of the engagement he could
have changed and he has had two years to think about it.

"Signal lag inside a fleet action is not a technical problem. It is *attention*. My rear squadron was in
contact and a ship in contact does not read traffic. So the order sat in a queue for ninety seconds
behind gunnery solutions."

He lost three hulls in that window. Seven total.

The finding cleared him. The finding was correct; there is no procedure he violated and no doctrine he
ignored, and the recommendation coming out of it was a change to signal priority that has since been
adopted by four navies.

"So I have improved fleet communications across the cluster," he says, "and it cost three hulls, and
their names are Vantage, Ordry's Promise and Sixth Careful, and I would rather have the ships."

He has read the Terran captain's account. They have never met.

"She is troubled by the same ninety seconds. I find that I mind that less than I expected to."

## "The Engagement That Did Not Happen"

Shipmaster Ordry Vaun's battleship sat in 7-B for nine days and nothing occurred, and she counts it as the
best service she has done.

A Void Walker raiding force of fourteen hulls came within two sectors on the second day, held, observed
for eleven hours, and left.

"They were quite right to leave. Fourteen fast hulls against one battleship is a good engagement for them
in every respect except that they cannot *hurt* me, and they worked that out in eleven hours, which is
eleven hours faster than most people."

There is no battle report. There is nothing to file. In the Registry's records, 7-B in that period is
blank.

"Which is the problem," Vaun says. "An empire measures its admirals by engagements and I have prevented
one, and there is no field on any form for a war that did not take place. My house's precedence is
calculated from service records and nine days of nothing does not appear in a service record."

Asked what she did during the eleven hours.

"I read. I was extremely visible. That was the entire operation and it worked."

## "What The Stealth Report Looks Like" ↔ *(from outside)*

The battle report from 3-D reads: *sector lost. Own losses six. Enemy composition not established.*

Chart-Warden Rell has read four hundred battle reports and that last clause appears in eleven of them,
all against the Shadow Realm.

"Their signature beats our sensors, so what comes back is an outcome and not a telemetry. I know six of
ours died. I do not know to *what*. I cannot tell the Admiralty whether we lost to forty light hulls or
to nine heavy ones, and those two facts imply completely different wars."

She has tried to derive it from the damage. Damage control gives her breach profiles; breach profiles
give her weapon classes; weapon classes give her a range of hull types.

"I get to *between eleven and thirty-one*. That is not an intelligence product. That is a shrug with
arithmetic on it."

The line she wrote at the bottom of the file, which was queried and which she defended:

**"We do not know how many they have. We have never known. It is possible that this is the entire
capability and the rest is our own arithmetic frightening us, and I would like somebody senior to consider
that before we commit a fleet."**

---

## "What The Stealth Report Looks Like" ↔ *(from inside)*

We had nine hulls at 3-D.

Nine. Against their fourteen, and we took the sector, and their report will say *composition not
established*, and somewhere a careful woman will write down a range of eleven to thirty-one and everybody
above her will read the top of it.

That is the whole of it. That is the Realm.

I have commanded at 3-D and at Vail and at Kettering approach and I have never once had numbers, and I
have never once been *counted*, and the day somebody counts us properly is the day this stops working. Our
own strategists have written that down. It is not a secret; it is in the training.

So: nine hulls, and a reputation, and a great deal of extremely good hospitality for anybody who might
otherwise start adding.

What nobody outside understands is that this is exhausting. A bluff is not a comfortable way to live. Every
engagement I win makes the estimate of us larger and the estimate is the only armour we have and it is made
of other people's imaginations, and I cannot maintain it, I can only decline to correct it.

Their Chart-Warden's note is the most dangerous document in the cluster and nobody senior has read it.

We have. We have had it for a year. We are not going to do anything about it, because there is nothing to
do about somebody being right.

## "Boarding" — the thing the battle log never records

Battles in this galaxy are decided at range and everybody knows it, which is why nobody prepares for the
other thing.

Prize Officer Onn has taken four hulls by boarding in eleven years and describes it as "a plumbing job with
a moral dimension."

"Nobody surrenders a warship. There is no procedure — the Concord had one and it lapsed. So what happens
is a ship loses steering and power and cannot leave, and it is still a warship with a crew in it, and
somebody has to go across."

Four hulls. Two came quietly. One was empty — genuinely empty, crew evacuated, and they spent six hours
clearing a ship that had nobody on it. One did not come quietly and Onn will not discuss it beyond
confirming that it is the reason she now sends a signal first and waits eleven minutes.

"The battle report says *hull captured*. Two words. There is a whole hour in those two words and it does
not fit in a form, and I have stopped trying to make it fit."

The captured hulls are at Ordel. One is a training aid. Three are back in service under Terran numbers
and their old names have been struck from the plating, which Onn thinks is a mistake and has said so.

## "Attrition" — the Mechanicus way, from the far side

You do not lose a battle to the Second Shift. You lose the fourth round.

Wing-Captain Sen has fought them twice and the account is the same both times.

"Round one you are winning. Their gunnery is adequate and their shields are nothing and you are faster and
you hit them more. Round two you are still winning and the arithmetic on your board is good."

"Round three you notice that the ships you damaged in round one are still there."

Self-repairing capitals. Field fabrication. Plate a metre thick over a hull that does not have to be
elegant because it does not have to be fast.

"By round four your damage has accumulated and theirs has not, and there was never a moment where it
turned, and you cannot point at a mistake. You did everything correctly and lost slowly."

Sen withdrew both times. Both times the Mechanicus did not pursue.

"They never pursue. They are not fighting *you*. You were in the advance path and now you are not, and the
advance continues, and it was never about you at all. I have found that harder to get over than losing."
