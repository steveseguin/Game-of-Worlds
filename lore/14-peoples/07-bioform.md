# The Long Season

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/07-bioform.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: on the way to being something else.** Nothing in Bioform design is finished, because
finishing implies stopping. Grown wood, shell, cultivated fibre; nothing straight, nothing square,
nothing with a right angle anywhere. Buildings are **pruned** rather than repaired, and a structure that
has stopped changing is a structure somebody has stopped tending.

**There are no monuments of any kind, anywhere, and no graves.**

**Palette:** green in forty registers, bone, wet brown, and blossom colours that are startling and
brief. Their hulls are not painted; the colour is the animal.

**Materials:** all grown. A Bioform hull is raised in a nutrient berth over nine years, and the visual
consequence is that a fleet looks like a **herd of different ages** — juveniles thin and pale, veterans
enormous, dark, and marked.

**Insignia:** none, and it would not help. A hull is recognised by its **line** — its lineage — the way
you recognise a breed, and any tender can name a hull's line at a glance and will tell you who raised it
and in which delta.

**People:** minimal dress, plant fibre, and they wear seeds — small, live, in pouches, planted eventually
somewhere they have been. And the detail every envoy remembers: **a tender assessing a hull will put a
hand to it and then taste her palm.** This is normal. Terran envoys have never got used to it.

## Personality

Kind, unhurried, tactile, and unsettling — and the unsettling part is a direct product of the kindness.

A Bioform will notice your body before your rank. Ossa opened her testimony with *"sit, you have been
standing in doorways for thirty years and it shows in your back,"* which is characteristic: they read
physical condition the way other people read insignia, and they mention it, and they are always right.

They will offer you food within a minute of meeting you and refusing is a serious discourtesy.

**Their humour** is gentle, agricultural, and occasionally devastating without any change of tone. The
best line in the Long File is Ossa's, and it is delivered as an apology: *you should try asking your ships
what they want. Ah. No. Forgive me. Yours are not asked.*

**Their virtue:** patience that is not passivity. They fed three empires through the Shortening at cost,
unasked, and have never mentioned it in a negotiation in seventy-four years.

**Their vice:** **they withhold what they cannot formalise.** They knew the deep lanes were bad in BU 40
— their hulls came back thin, reluctant in the berth — and they rerouted quietly over eleven years and
told nobody, and every other power lost people because of the silence. Ossa's defence is sound and it is
still a defence: *there is a box on the intake form for Reason Stated. What would I have written? The
ships do not like it.*

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**Custodial authority: you speak for what you tend.** The assembly of tenders is weighted by berth, and
nothing can be decided faster than a growing season.

**Tender Ossa.** Ninety-three, still tending, and the moral authority of the Collective rather than its
leader. She gave testimony to Ilsa Rell in AU 49 at sixty-eight, which was technically a violation, and
she has never described it as courage. She has outlived every tender who disapproved.

**Tender Vey.** Young, holds a major line, and leads **the Tellers** — the generation that considers the
BU 40 silence a crime. Vey's argument is one sentence: *a feeling withheld is still a fact withheld.*
She is winning, and she has never once attacked Ossa personally, and the two of them are frequently seen
eating together, which is how Bioform politics is conducted.

**How you rise:** you raise something well, for a long time, and people notice. Custodianship cannot be
audited — a tender's judgement about a line will not be verifiable for nine years — and their entire
epistemology holds that a grower's feel for a hull is real knowledge. Which it is. It also means anyone
tending a major line holds enormous power on a claim nobody can check.

**How you fall:** a line fails under you. There is no mechanism for removing a tender who is merely
wrong, and there is no forgiveness at all for one whose berth goes bad, and the distinction between
those two is the whole of Bioform legal argument.

## Beliefs

**On death — composting, and it is not feared.** A Bioform funeral is a composting, conducted with real
tenderness and no grief, and the dead are genuinely still present in the biosphere in a way that is not
mystical. There is no afterlife because there is no *after*; there is only the rest of the process.

**On the Unarriving:** an interruption. A very large one. The Collective lost almost nobody, because
their ships had not been on those lanes for forty years, and they are quietly aware that this is the
reason no other power fully trusts them.

**On themselves — the Long Season.** A real faith, cyclical, whose central tenet is that **things should
be allowed to complete.** Growth, maturity, decline and composting are one process, and interrupting any
part of it is the only sin the faith recognises.

**On the future, and this is the offensive part:** they hold that **fixed structures are a category
error.** A thing built to last forever is a thing that refuses to end — and that includes fortifications,
and monuments, and empires, and **the Trellis**. They do not want the lanes relit. They have said so,
courteously, for seventy-four years, and eleven empires have taken it as obtuseness or sabotage.

They do not know why they are right. That matters: it is instinct, not knowledge. Nobody in the galaxy
has the answer except the Ancients, and a faith that turns out to be correct is far more moving than
smugness.

## Rituals

**Daily — the tasting.** Everything is tasted: food, water, soil, hulls. Greeting protocol is to offer
food. Their diplomatic corps has caused four incidents by offering things other species should not eat,
and the incidents are still described, in Bioform records, as *misjudgements of hospitality*.

**Coming of age — the first berth.** A young Bioform is given a line to tend, alone, and it takes nine
years, and they are adult when the hull is grown. Not when it succeeds — when it *finishes*. A juvenile
whose first line dies at year seven is not adult and must begin again, and this is considered neither
harsh nor arbitrary.

**Death — the composting.** The body goes into a berth. A hull is grown in it. Nobody records which hull,
deliberately, because knowing would make the hull a monument, and monuments refuse to end.

**War — the thinning.** Before a Collective fleet commits, the tenders of every line present speak the
age of each hull aloud. Not its strength. Its **age**. Because a ten-year veteran cannot be replaced
inside a decade, and the whole point of saying it out loud is that everyone in the room hears exactly
what is being spent.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Terran Empire** — Careful, decent, and drowning in paper. Quietly the Collective's favourite, and both
would deny it. Two custodial peoples who think in generations. We have stopped arguing with them about
the roads.

**Silicon Collective** — They asked how we knew in BU 40. We said the ships did not like it. They did not
laugh, and they wrote it down properly, and Nine called it *an unrepeatable result*, and Vey keeps that
in a pocket.

**Zephyr Swarm** — They cannot be spoken to about hulls. We have tried. It is like discussing bereavement
with weather.

**Crystalline Entity** — The only other people who think in generations, and we like them enormously, and
we cannot coordinate with them at all. Their season is ten thousand years. Ours is nine.

**Void Walkers** — Thin, hungry, and proud about it. We sent grain in AU 12 and did not invoice it. Ash
has instructed that no Walker ever charge us for carriage. Neither people has ever mentioned either
arrangement aloud.

**Mechanicus** — The only power that will not buy from us, and they have never said why, and Ossa worked
it out unaided: a hull they cannot melt is a hull they consider lost. She finds this touching. Nobody
agrees with her.

**Star Nomads** — They remember AU 12 and AU 14 and AU 19, and they bring it up, and we would rather they
did not. Our best customers and our most uncomfortable friends.

**The Ancients** — They took the roads down and we think they were right and we cannot say so without
being asked how we know, and we do not know. We have never requested an audience.

**Quantum Entities** — They will not commemorate because a monument selects one branch. We do not
commemorate because a monument refuses to end. Two peoples arriving at no graves from opposite
directions, and finding each other restful.

**Titan Lords** — They build for a thousand years, which is the precise error our faith is about, and
they are the most courteous people in the galaxy about it. Vey has called Ordel Deep *a beautiful
argument against us.*

**Shadow Realm** — They have never asked us for anything except food, which they pay for promptly, and
they are the only power that has never once asked how we knew.
