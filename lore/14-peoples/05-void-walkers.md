# Those Who Never Stopped

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/05-void-walkers.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: leaving.** Long, low, tapered, and streamlined for absolutely no reason — Bell's
atmosphere is too thin for aerodynamics to matter, and they build that way anyway, because it *looks like
departure*. Every structure on the nine launch spines faces the same direction. There are no plazas, no
squares, and no gathering spaces; the spine is a corridor and the city is a queue.

**Palette:** dust, bleached bone, sun-faded grey, everything slightly abraded by a dry atmosphere that
cracks skin. And then **one bright marking per hull** — a route number, in a single saturated colour,
large. The hull is anonymous. The route is not.

**Materials:** thin. Light alloys, ceramic, tensioned fabric. A Void Walker hull is the least material
per unit of capability in the galaxy, and it shows, and it is one reason they die easily.

**Insignia:** the route. A courier is identified by what she carries, and a Route-Holder's standing is
legible at a glance to anyone who knows the numbering.

**People:** thin, fast, and visibly underfed. Practical clothing, worn hard, and — the detail worth
keeping — **a courier wears her lanes.** Tally marks, literally, stitched or scored into the jacket
across the shoulders: one per lane held, added at the funeral where it was given to her. Route-Holder Ash
has sixty-one. You can count someone's worth from across a room, and everyone does.

## Personality

Impatient, proud, brave, and completely mercenary about knowledge.

A Void Walker enters a conversation already wanting it to be over. They state transit times rather than
distances, they will not repeat themselves, and they regard meetings as a Terran disease. *Six-B is forty
minutes. Was forty minutes. Get out of my way.*

They are also, and this is easy to miss, the bravest people in the galaxy on a per-head basis. Somebody
flew every lane on every chart for the first time, blind, and a disproportionate number of those
somebodies were couriers who did it for a fee.

**Their humour** is fast, cruel, and affectionate — insult as intimacy. They are the only people who
mock each other's dead, at the funerals, and mean it kindly.

**Their virtue:** they keep their word absolutely. A charge is honoured, and it is inherited, and a
Walker family four generations deep in somebody else's obligation is still flying it off.

**Their vice, and it is destroying them:** **they would rather lose a thing than share it.** Wren had the
most valuable observation in galactic history, refused Ilsa Rell three times on professional principle,
named no successor for the Anselm lanes, and died in AU 71 with it — and she would have told you, sharply,
that it was hers to keep. The Guild loses about four per cent of all route knowledge every year to old
age. They publish the number. They argue about it annually.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**A guild, not a government.** Standing is routes held. The Route-Holders' assembly on Bell is weighted
by lanes carried, and a courier with forty lanes outranks one with nine in every practical sense.

**Route-Holder Ash.** Sixty-one lanes — the most of anyone alive, and eleven of them are lit-era routes
she flew before AU 0 as a young woman. She is the single largest concentration of irreplaceable knowledge
in the galaxy, she is old, and every admiralty including Terra's has a contingency file on the day she
dies. She knows this. She finds it flattering.

Ash is not a reactionary. She is worse: she agrees with the Scriveners in private and has calculated
that saying so publicly would split the Guild in a decade, and has chosen the slower loss on purpose.

**Scrivener Pell.** Leader of the reform faction, stripped of standing, refused pads at three spines,
and arithmetically unanswerable. Pell's case is one page long and has been submitted forty-one times.
Pell is thirty-four and will outlive Ash and knows it.

**How you rise:** you are given lanes. Only at a funeral, only by the dying, and only by name. There is
no examination and no purchase.

**How you fall:** you write something down. Scriveners are stripped of standing, refused pads, and in
three recorded cases refused a funeral — which means their lanes died with them unrecited, which is
precisely the outcome they were trying to prevent, and nobody in the Guild has been willing to say that
out loud.

## Beliefs

**On death:** the recitation either succeeds or it does not. A courier who dies with a successor present,
in order, at length, has *arrived*. One who dies too fast has not, and the lanes are simply gone, and
everyone in the room knows exactly what was lost and can name it and can say what it was worth.

**On the Unarriving:** the day the product died. Bell lost a smaller fraction of its people than the
Nomads and a larger fraction of its *value*, and Void Walkers are honest about which of those two they
grieve more, which nobody else in the galaxy is.

**On themselves — the only copy.** This is the theology and it is coherent, which is what makes it
dangerous. What is written can be taken. What is taken is no longer yours to give. A courier's worth is
that she is the sole holder, so committing a route to paper betrays everyone who ever paid for
exclusivity, and betrays the lane itself, which was bought with somebody's crew.

**On the future:** shrinking. They are the only people in the galaxy with a published, agreed, annual
figure for how much of themselves they are losing, and no plan.

## Rituals

**Daily — the recitation.** A courier speaks her lanes every morning, aloud, in order, entire. Ten
minutes for a young courier; over two hours for Ash. Alone, before anything else, every day, for a
lifetime. It is devotion and it is also maintenance: **an unspoken route decays.**

**Coming of age — the first solo.** A courier's first lane flown alone, and it is always a short one, and
it is always one somebody died on. You are given the route and told who bought it. The tally mark goes on
that evening, and it is the only one you receive from the living.

**Death — the funeral recitation.** The dying courier speaks her lanes to a chosen successor. It can take
days. Attendance is by invitation and the mockery is affectionate and constant and stops the instant the
recitation begins. The successor's meal afterward is enormous and slow, and it is the only time a Void
Walker sits down to eat.

**War — the naming.** Before a Walker squadron departs, each pilot states the route aloud, to the others,
in full. Not for coordination — they all know it. It is so that if she does not come back somebody in the
formation was the last to hear it, and can say so, and can say what it was.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Terran Empire** — Filing cabinets with an admiralty attached. They want everything written down, which
is why we will not deal with them beyond freight, and they were the only people who came to Wren three
times, and she respected that, and refused them anyway.

**Silicon Collective** — They calculated our loss rate and published it and handed Pell his best weapon,
and they did it without malice, which somehow makes it worse. We use their underwriting. We do not
discuss the paper.

**Zephyr Swarm** — They lose ten thousand and call it a good price. We lose one and lose forty lanes.
There is nothing to say to them.

**Crystalline Entity** — Tessen called us the deliberate murderers of meaning. Ash's reply, on the
record, was *"we have not got ten thousand years, Elder. We have got Tuesday."*

**Mechanicus** — They walk into the dark at three-quarters speed on purpose. We do not understand it and
we cannot look away from it, and their hulls come back, and ours are faster and do not.

**Bioform Collective** — Slow, kind, and the only people who have ever fed Bell for nothing. They sent
grain in AU 12 and never invoiced it and have never mentioned it since. Ash has instructed that no
Walker ever charge them for carriage. It is the standing order nobody argues with.

**Star Nomads** — Our only real friends and our direct competitors, which is an uncomfortable thing to
be. They sell routes. We *are* routes. They think we are romantics; we think they are shopkeepers; both
of us are right and neither of us will sit at the other's table hungry.

**The Ancients** — They took the roads. Every Walker alive believes this and none can prove it, and the
Guild's position is that whoever did it owes Bell four centuries of revenue.

**Quantum Entities** — We were paid extremely well for first contact in BU 400 and refused to go back.
That remains the policy.

**Titan Lords** — They took eleven days to cross what we did in six hours, and because of it they hold
the only record of the thing Wren saw. Ash has read Vaun's log nineteen times. She has never said so
publicly.

**Shadow Realm** — They would give a great deal for our archive, if we had one, which is the best
argument the Guild has ever made against building one.
