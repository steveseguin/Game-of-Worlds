# The Swarm

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/03-zephyr.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: quantity.** The Swarm does not have a design aesthetic in the sense the other eleven
do, because an aesthetic is a claim about an object and the Swarm does not make objects it cares about.
Hulls are identical, unnamed, unnumbered, produced in tens of thousands, and visually interchangeable —
and the *effect*, seen from outside, is genuinely one of the most striking sights in the galaxy. A
Zephyr fleet is not a formation of ships. It is a **texture**.

**Palette:** ochre, rust, dull warm iron. Nothing is painted. Nothing is marked. There is no livery,
because livery distinguishes, and nothing needs distinguishing.

**Materials:** scraped nickel-iron and accreted stone. Nests are grown by addition without plan, in
whatever is to hand, and the interiors are warm low warrens with no straight lines and no addresses.
There is no exterior aesthetic, because nobody looks at a nest from outside.

**Insignia:** none. There has never been one. The concept does not translate.

**People:** small, chitinous, and many. A single Zephyr body is unimpressive and nobody has ever been
asked to be impressed by one. What is remarkable is that a hundred thousand of them move *in phase*,
and their only art form is exactly this: **synchronised motion**, in the open, for hours, for no
purpose, with no audience — because everyone participating is also watching, and the Swarm does not
draw that distinction.

Visitors have described it as the most joyful thing they have ever witnessed, and have universally
declined to attend a second one.

## Personality

Cheerful, incurious, warm, and horrifying — in that order, and all at once.

A Zephyr delegation is *friendly*. Genuinely, unaffectedly pleased to be talking to you, entirely
without agenda, and it will describe a catastrophe in the same register it describes weather. *Some of
we did not come back from 6-B. Now we know 6-B. It was a good price.* Nothing about that is bravado
and nothing about it is trauma. It is arithmetic delivered kindly.

**Their humour** is present and very hard to translate, because most of it is about scale. The Swarm
find *smallness* funny — the idea of a fleet of nine, of a named ship, of a person having a room.

**Their virtue:** they are the least cruel people in the galaxy. There is no Zephyr history of torture,
slavery, or reprisal, and there never has been, because all three require a concept of the individual
victim.

**Their vice, which is the same fact:** they cannot perceive individual suffering. Not that they
disregard it — they cannot *resolve* it. A fragment cut off in transit has no legal category, no rights,
and no word describing what it is, and the Swarm has never noticed that this is a tragedy, because
noticing would require the vocabulary the absence of which is the tragedy.

**And this is a contradiction, not merely a limitation** — the distinction matters, because a race that
is only *missing* something is a concept, and a race whose strength manufactures its own wound is a
character. The Swarm's saturation method is the most efficient survey instrument in the galaxy
*because* nothing in it is individuated. Which means the more successfully it works, the more often a
piece of the Swarm is severed at range — and every severed piece becomes the one thing the Swarm has no
apparatus to notice.

**They produce their own casualties in a category they cannot see, at a rate proportional to how well
they are doing.** The Remainder is not an accident that befell them; it is an output of the doctrine,
and there will be more, and there is no mechanism by which the Swarm could ever count them. A player
watches this happen live in `../22-act-one.md`, mission 3, forty turns before they ever read the
Remainder's testimony.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**There is no government, no office, no leader, and no succession.** A decision is made **where the most
of them are**, because that is where the bandwidth is densest and consensus resolves fastest.

**The Thick.** The current locus of density has a name, and it changes. In AU 74 the Thick is in the
**Vantage basin**, in the northern hemisphere, and has been for eleven years, which is a long time and
is why the Swarm has read as expansionist for a decade — the Vantage nests border the sectors it expands
into.

When the tectonics open and the population migrates south, the same Swarm will become cautious and
mercantile inside a decade, and nobody will have changed their mind. **Swarm foreign policy is a
function of geology.** Terra's entire standing guidance on the matter is one line: *establish where they
are.*

**How you rise:** you do not. There is no standing to hold and nothing to be promoted into.

**How you fall:** you are cut. Losing connection is the only status change available in Swarm society,
and it is not a punishment and cannot be appealed and does not heal.

**The Remainder.** Eleven hundred hulls, cut off in transit in AU 0, outside every category for seventy
years, holding the only surviving memory of what ten thousand of the Swarm saw. It cannot go home
without being absorbed, and being absorbed erases the thing that makes going home worth it. It counts
itself every morning. See `../10-the-long-file/03-zephyr.md`.

## Beliefs

**On death:** there is none, in the sense other peoples mean. A unit that ends is a reduction in the
total, and the total is enormous, and the total is what matters. No gods, no afterlife, no dead. They do
not find this bleak; they find other peoples' graveyards baffling and slightly wasteful.

**On the Unarriving:** a measurement. The largest one ever taken. Ten thousand of we went into the dark
and did not come back and now we know something about the dark. This is not denial. It is their
epistemology working correctly on an input it was not built for.

**On themselves:** the whole. There is no Swarm concept of a Swarm *identity* to be proud of or ashamed
of. There is only more or less of we.

**On the future:** more. Sincerely, and without ambition attached. The Swarm does not want to rule the
galaxy. It wants to be in more of it, which is not the same thing and is much harder to negotiate with.

## Rituals

The Swarm has almost none, and the absence is the characterisation. Ritual marks transitions in the life
of an individual, and there are no individuals, so there is nothing to mark.

**Daily — the trough.** Warm fungal paste, communal, continuous, eaten by whoever is passing, standing,
with the hands. **Nobody in the Swarm has ever eaten a portion measured for one**; there is no word for
a serving. It is nutritionally complete and, to every visitor's surprise, genuinely delicious — faintly
sweet, faintly sour, like a very good sourdough. No outsider has ever been able to tolerate the
circumstances.

**Coming of age — there is none.** A new mass is counted, not named. You were always we.

**Death — there is none.** A subtraction is read into the total. It is a number, and it is accurate, and
it is not read *to* anyone.

**Reunion — the synchronisation.** The only true rite the Swarm has, and it is enormous. Two nests
coming into contact after separation synchronise over about four hours, involving the entire population
of both, and it looks from outside like a slow riot and is the closest thing they have to a festival.
The highest good is rejoining. The worst state is apart. Every Swarm ceremony is this ceremony.

**And one more, which is not theirs.** Every morning, eleven hundred hulls in loose formation somewhere
past the frontier are counted, in neat rows, which is not how a swarm flies. It is the only ritual in
Zephyr history invented by an individual, and the Swarm does not know it exists, and it will end when
the Remainder does.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

The Swarm does not really have foreign policy. It has *proximity*. What follows is closer to how each
race registers.

**Terran Empire** — They write down what we did. We do not know why. They are welcome to it.

**Silicon Collective** — They asked eleven times to look at our loss data. We do not understand what
they want it for. We said yes each time.

**Crystalline Entity** — Slow. Very slow. We are fond of them in the way you are fond of a hill.

**Void Walkers** — Fast, and there are hardly any of them, and each one matters to them enormously. We
find this frightening, which we do not say.

**Mechanicus** — The only other people who go into the dark without asking first. There is a mutual
regard here that neither party has words for and both parties feel. **The Swarm and the Second Shift
have never fought.**

**Bioform Collective** — They grow ships for nine years and then are sad when one dies. We have tried to
understand this and cannot.

**Star Nomads** — They are angry with us. We know they are angry with us. It is because the same nine
days barely dented we and ended them, and we do not know how to make that untrue, and they bring it up,
and we let them.

**The Ancients** — There are very few of them and they are always at the meetings. We do not attend
meetings.

**Quantum Entities** — They say it has not happened. We were there. We do not argue; there is no
argument to have. But we saw it, and there was one of it, and we would like them to stop saying that.

**Titan Lords** — Enormous, and there is one of each, and they name them. Everything about the Titans is
funny to the Swarm and the Swarm is too polite to explain why.

**Shadow Realm** — They ask what our strength is. We tell them. They do not believe us. We tell them
again.
