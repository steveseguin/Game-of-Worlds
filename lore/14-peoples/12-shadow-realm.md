# The Unlisted

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/12-shadow-realm.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: no sightline longer than a room.** This is the defining fact and it holds at every scale.
Corridors turn. Halls have baffles. There are no plazas, no avenues, no vistas, and no window onto anything
larger than a room. **You can never see far in a Shadow city, anywhere, by design** — and visitors describe
the same building as either intimate or suffocating depending entirely on the visitor.

**Palette:** warm dark. Deep browns, dim reds, black, and light at candle scale — sparse, low, and placed
so that you are always in the lit part and cannot see the rest. Sable's Dark is lit by a faint red dwarf
through a hazy sky and their cities are underneath it, and nothing on the surface is built at all.

**Materials:** textile, layered stone, fungus-leather, and a great deal of hanging fabric. The acoustics
are dead. Everything is soft. Combined with the low light, the effect is of a very large building that
always feels like a small one.

**These are the most comfortable rooms in the galaxy.** Every envoy says so. That is not an accident and it
is not hospitality for its own sake — comfort is the delivery mechanism.

**Insignia: none, ever.** Not concealed, not abstracted, not privately held. There has never been one. No
Unlisted hull, uniform, document or building carries a mark of any kind, and the Registry's file on their
heraldry consists of a single line noting that repeated enquiries were answered warmly and at length
without addressing the question.

**People:** dark-adapted, layered, quiet-moving. Adornment is real but never legible — jewellery that does
not indicate rank, cloth that does not indicate house. You cannot read an Unlisted's standing from
anything, and nobody has ever been given a population figure for their homeworld, and the Registry's
estimate carries an error bar of a factor of six.

## Personality

Delighted, hospitable, and never answering the question asked.

An Unlisted is genuinely pleased to see you. That is the disconcerting part — there is no coldness to push
against, no evasiveness to catch, just warmth and food and a conversation that goes somewhere adjacent to
where you were steering it and stays there.

**They never answer the first question.** This is taught to children as manners.

And they do not lie, because lying is crude, traceable, and beneath a host. They answer a slightly
different question, generously, at length, and with evident pleasure — and if you notice, and rephrase,
they will compliment you on it. *No — forgive me. You asked something slightly different that time. I
noticed. It was well done.*

**Their humour** is the most sophisticated of the twelve: courteous, layered, and often at their own
expense. Sable opening a hostile interview by observing that the file *"contains no defendant at all — that
is either the most honest archive in the galaxy or the most useless one, and I have not decided"* is doing
four things at once and enjoying all of them.

**Their virtue:** they have never joined an accusation against anybody. Not once. In a galaxy where they
are universally suspected, the Realm has never returned the favour to any other power, on any question.

**Their vice: they are enjoying this.** The suspicion, the reputation, the unprovable innocence — they
find it funny, and they have chosen with something that looks a great deal like relish not to defend
themselves, and it has cost them seventy-four years of allies they might have had.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**An unlisted hierarchy.** Nobody outside knows who leads, or how many tiers there are, or whether the
entity that signed a treaty had the standing to. Every Realm signatory has been honoured, which is the
only evidence anyone has that the structure is real.

**Sable.** A Broker. Gave Ilsa Rell a fragment in AU 67 for nothing, chose a good frame deliberately, told
her the honest reason instead of a clever one — *we cannot read them either* — and noticed that she looked
tired one year before she died. Giving something away sets a price of zero, which is Broker heresy, and
Sable did it anyway and has never explained.

**The Third Question.** The senior Keeper voice, and a **title rather than a name**, which is
characteristic and also practical: three individuals have held it since AU 0 and no outside power has
noticed the transitions. The Keeper position: what we hold is worth more unspent, and the moment it is
retailed it stops being leverage and becomes somebody else's document.

**The Brokers have been winning for seventy years**, which means the Realm's one irreplaceable asset — the
BU 13 imagery of eleven Ancient vessels working at the relay — has been sold off a frame at a time for three
generations, to eleven buyers, none holding enough to make a sentence. The Keepers call it a slow
catastrophe conducted for operating expenses. They are right, and the Brokers kept the lights on.

**How you rise:** by holding answers other people need. **How you fall:** by being counted. There is no
formal disgrace and no exile; you simply become a person whose extent is known, and in the Realm that is
the end of a career.

## Beliefs

**On death:** the unobserved is the only thing that is truly yours, so **an unwitnessed death is a good
one.** Unlisted die alone by preference, and arranging it is considered a kindness performed by the family
rather than a neglect. There are no graves and no markers, and the location is not recorded, and this is
the most loving thing their culture does.

**On the Unarriving — and this is why everyone thinks they did it.** Their faith holds that what is seen is
taken and what is recorded belongs to the recorder, and therefore the Dimming is **not a catastrophe. It is
a sanctification.** The galaxy was returned to a proper condition. The empires clawing to relight the lanes
are trying to put themselves back under observation, and the Unlisted watch this with the specific pity of
people who cannot explain something that seems obvious.

They say it out loud. In council. Politely. It is very nearly the only evidence against them and it is not
evidence of anything.

**On themselves — a bluff.** Their own strategists have written it down: concealment is the state's core
competency, so the Realm cannot demonstrate its own strength, and the deterrent depends entirely on nobody
testing it. **The day somebody counts them properly is the day the Realm ends.** This is the founding
anxiety of their foreign policy and it is discussed openly among themselves.

**On the future:** keep not being counted.

**And the truth, which is worse than the accusation:** they had nothing to do with the Unarriving, they have
worked out roughly what did, they cannot read their own evidence, and they are holding it as leverage while
selling it for rent.

## Rituals

**Daily — the first question.** Every Unlisted conversation opens with a question that will not be
answered, asked by the guest and deflected by the host, and both parties know the form. It is not a game and
it is not hostile; it is the equivalent of a handshake, and an Unlisted who answers a first question has
either made an extraordinary declaration of trust or made a mistake.

**Coming of age — the unlisting.** An Unlisted child **is** recorded: named, entered in a family register,
counted. At majority they are **removed from it.** The entry is struck, the name is not carried forward, and
they are thereafter uncounted for life.

Adulthood is being erased from the record. It is a celebration. There is food.

**Death — the unwitnessed.** As above. Arranged by the family, unattended by preference, unrecorded, and the
place is never revisited. What is said afterward, once, is *they were not seen.*

**War — the offer.** Before an engagement, the Realm extends hospitality to the opposing commander:
genuinely, specifically, with terms, and it is honoured if accepted. Three have accepted in seventy-four
years and all three were returned unharmed with their fleets intact and their intelligence assessments
politely corrected in the wrong direction. It is the most effective weapon they have and it is also sincere,
which is the thing nobody believes.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Terran Empire** — They have bought four fragments and have never worked out that there are eleven. Vance
is decent and Ito is buyable and we have told neither of them anything they could use.

**Silicon Collective** — They could read our imagery in an afternoon. We have not found terms and it is not
because of price. It is that afterward they would *publish*, because publishing is what they are for, and
we would have nothing.

**Zephyr Swarm** — We ask their strength. They tell us. We do not believe them. They tell us again. It is the
only relationship in the galaxy in which we are the ones being lied to and we are wrong about it.

**Crystalline Entity** — We have never asked them for anything in six hundred years, which everybody finds
sinister and Sarn finds restful, and Sarn is correct: we simply have no use for them and see no reason to
pretend otherwise.

**Void Walkers** — They keep everything in their heads and write nothing down, which makes them the only
people in the galaxy we cannot steal from, and we admire them extravagantly and have said so, and they do
not care.

**Mechanicus** — Our entire toolkit is inert against them. Fourth told us so, plainly, without malice, and
we did not enjoy it. **You cannot hide composition from somebody who never knew what you had.**

**Bioform Collective** — We buy food from them, promptly, at their price, and we are the only power that
has never once asked how they knew in BU 40. They have noticed. Neither side has mentioned it.

**Star Nomads** — Sable gave a fragment to somebody who could not pay. The Fleet found out, has never told
anyone, and has never joined an accusation against us. We are aware of what that is worth and we have never
tried to spend it.

**The Ancients** — We watched them at the relay in BU 13 and we have been retailing it for seventy years and
we cannot read it. They have never attempted to stop us. We find that far more frightening than
interference would be.

**Quantum Entities** — Neither of us can be counted. They made a constitution of it; we made a virtue of it.
There is a professional respect here that neither party will confirm.

**Titan Lords** — We offered House Kell a fragment. They declined on the grounds that a fragment is not a
work, which is the best refusal we have ever received, and we have quoted it since.
