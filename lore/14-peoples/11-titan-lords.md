# The Weight

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/11-titan-lords.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: honest load.** Megalithic, load-bearing, with the structural logic visible in every
member. Nothing cantilevers. Nothing spans further than it must. **Ornament exists and is always
structural** — a carved column is still a column, and decorating a member that carries nothing would be
considered a *lie about the building*, which is the strongest term in Titan architectural criticism.

**Everything is low.** At 1.9 gravity, ceilings are low, thresholds are low, seating is low, and the whole
built environment presses down in a way visitors feel in their knees within an hour.

**Palette:** stone, bronze, deep oxblood red, and gold leaf applied **only to load-bearing members** —
which means the gilding in a Titan hall is a diagram of the forces holding it up.

**Insignia:** a house mark, carved into a structural member of everything the house builds. Not painted, not
riveted. Cut into something that is holding the ship together, so that removing the mark would weaken the
vessel.

**People:** heavy, formal, layered dress, and **precedence is visible in the cut** — the number of pleats,
the depth of a collar, the position of a house mark. A Titan can read the entire room's standing on
entering it, which is the point, and it is why they do not sit at round tables.

## Personality

Formal, generous, slow, and openly contemptuous of hurry.

No contractions in speech, ever. Full titles. Long sentences that arrive where they intended to. A Titan
will not be rushed even into their own testimony — Shipmaster Vaun spent the first third of the most
important account in galactic history defending a design philosophy nobody had attacked.

Being hurried is an insult of a specific and actionable kind. A Titan meal takes four hours minimum and
business is not discussed in the first two, and their diplomatic corps is the most difficult in the galaxy
to work with and the most reliable once an agreement exists, because an agreement is a **work** and works
are honoured.

**Their humour** is heavy, deliberate, and much warmer than outsiders expect — mostly enormous
understatement. *We will arrive when we arrive, and when we arrive there will be no further question of
six-B.*

**Their virtue:** they build things that outlast everybody, and they mean it as a gift.

**Their vice:** **they will be correct into extinction.** A house that innovates and fails loses centuries
of standing, because a hull's service record is permanent and public. A house that repeats a proven design
loses nothing. Forty houses, each making the rational choice four hundred times in a row, produced a
civilisation that has got heavier, slower and less curious for four centuries — and their own historians
call this **settledness**, and it is a compliment.

There is no point at which anybody could reasonably have chosen otherwise. That is what makes it the most
frightening decline in the setting.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**An aristocracy of shipwrights.** Nine cities, roughly forty houses, and a formal precedence order
recalculated **annually** against the service records of every hull each house has in the water. It is
transparent, meritocratic in its own terms, and a machine for producing stagnation.

**House Kell.** Senior house for the past nineteen years, holding precedence on the strength of a line of
heavy freight hulls of which thirty-one are still in service and the oldest is four hundred and six years
old. Kell has laid down no new design since AU 30. Kell does not need to.

**Shipmaster Ordry Vaun.** Granddaughter of Shipmaster Vaun, who died in AU 69, and current master of the
*Weight of Consideration* — still in service at seventy-six years, which is the business model and not
sentiment. House Vaun's precedence rests almost entirely on that one hull and on the log inside it, and
Ordry has raised the AU 45 filing at every council she has attended, which is all of them.

She has been offered two formal apologies from Registrar Vance, both accepted, and **the file still says
instrument error**, and that is exactly the thing House Vaun is describing.

**How you rise:** your house's hulls stay in the water. That is the entire mechanism, and it means
standing is inherited in practice and earned in theory and both statements are true.

**How you fall:** a hull of yours fails in service. Publicly, permanently, in the annual reckoning, and it
takes generations to recover, and this is precisely why nobody tries anything.

## Beliefs

**On death — the long service.** You are remembered for as long as something of yours is still running.
This is a real theology and it produces the only funeral rite in the galaxy that **improves with time**: a
house re-reads its founder's list every decade to add the years, and the reading gets better as the
centuries pass. Vaun will be commemorated for as long as the *Weight of Consideration* is in the water, and
every year of her service is another year of him.

The corollary is bleak and they accept it without complaint: a shipwright whose works all fail is
genuinely forgotten, deliberately, and no consolation is offered.

**On the Unarriving:** they were **inside** it. Their crossing took eleven days when everyone else's took
hours, so the *Weight of Consideration* was in the transition from beginning to end and came out the far
side, and her log is the only interior record in existence. They reported it correctly, in writing, in the
correct form, to three authorities. It was filed as instrument error.

The Weight's whole relationship with the rest of the galaxy runs through that sentence.

**On themselves:** settledness. That maturity is the correct end state of a craft and that four centuries
without a major innovation is evidence of having got it right. They are not defensive about this. They will
explain it to you at length and it is internally consistent.

**On the future:** a thousand years, and they mean it literally — the Weight of Ages is specified to that
service life, and nobody involved in laying it down expects to see it decommissioned, and that is
considered the appropriate relationship between a person and their work.

## Rituals

**Daily — the service reading.** Every house reads its own list each morning: which of its hulls are in
the water, where, and for how long. It is dull, it is administrative, and it is the closest thing to
prayer that Ordel Deep has. A hull lost is removed that morning, aloud, by name.

**Coming of age — the first member.** A young Titan's first contribution to a hull is a single structural
member, and their name is cut into it, and it will hold that ship together for as long as the ship exists.
Adulthood is the moment something depends on you. Most Titans can point to their member. Some houses keep
the offcuts.

**Death — the reading of works.** The deceased's hulls and their current service status, read aloud, and
**updated every decade**. It is the only rite in the galaxy that is not a moment but a subscription.

**War — the precedence order.** Before a Titan fleet engages, the full precedence order of every house
present is read aloud, in sequence, by seniority. It takes upwards of forty minutes. Nobody has ever
proposed shortening it, and no Titan admiral has ever considered that the delay might matter, because if
the engagement could not wait forty minutes it was not going to be won.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Terran Empire** — Careful, decent, and the office that wrote *instrument error*. Two apologies have been
tendered and accepted. The file is unchanged. We shall continue to raise it.

**Silicon Collective** — Their interval agrees with ours to three figures and we have never corresponded.
Analyst Nine cites us in every submission. We have instructed our delegation to acknowledge this formally
and the instruction has been outstanding for six years, which is not slow by our standards.

**Zephyr Swarm** — They find us funny. We are aware. We have decided to be flattered.

**Crystalline Entity** — The only people who have never once hurried us, and we have never once hurried
them, and in four hundred years the two peoples have exchanged perhaps nine substantive communications and
every one of them was a pleasure.

**Void Walkers** — Six hours to our eleven days, and because of the eleven days we hold the log and they do
not. Route-Holder Ash has read it nineteen times and has never said so publicly, and we know, and we have
never mentioned it, and that is the whole of the relationship.

**Mechanicus** — They build to be repaired and we build to last, and both work, and we have the most
cordial and least communicative relationship in the galaxy. We do not buy from them. They do not buy from
us. Neither has ever been offended.

**Bioform Collective** — Tender Vey called Ordel Deep *a beautiful argument against us*, which is the finest
thing anyone has said about our cities, and it was said by somebody who believes we are making a
fundamental error, and we have had it inscribed.

**Star Nomads** — They were told a figure they know to be false and they have not let it go. We were told
*instrument error* and we have not let it go. There is more between the Fleet and the Weight than either of
us finds comfortable.

**The Ancients** — They have read the log. We know they have read the log. They did not intervene when it
was dismissed, and we have concluded that they could not, and we would prefer to be wrong about that.

**Quantum Entities** — The only people who have never asked us to be certain. Our delegations get on
extremely well and produce nothing whatsoever.

**Shadow Realm** — They offered to sell us a fragment. We declined, on the grounds that a fragment is not a
work. In hindsight this may have been an error and House Kell will not discuss it.
