# The Twelve Peoples

Status: **PROPOSED**. Pure lore. **No mechanics, no numbers, nothing here is a gameplay proposal** —
unlike `13-wonders/`, none of this needs an engine conversation. It is writing reference.

`12-civilisations/` describes the twelve *places*. This describes the twelve *peoples*: what they
look like, what they are like in a room, who leads them today, what they believe, what they do on
purpose and by habit, and — the part that did not exist anywhere until now — **how each one regards
the other eleven.**

Each file covers six things:

1. **Aesthetic** — form language, palette, materials, insignia, how their people present themselves
2. **Personality** — the temperament of a typical member. Their humour, their virtue, their vice
3. **Leadership and structure** — who holds authority in **AU 74**, by name, and how you rise or fall
4. **Beliefs** — about death, about the Unarriving, about themselves, about what comes next
5. **Rituals** — four each: something daily, a coming-of-age, a death rite, and a war rite
6. **Attitudes** — eleven one-line readings of the other eleven races

## The four axes that generate every diplomatic story

Every relationship in the galaxy is some combination of these. If a scene needs tension between two
powers and you cannot remember why, it is one of these four.

**1 · The Registry question — who gets to count?**
Terra decides what things are called, what a tonne is, and how many died. It has no authority to do
any of this and does it anyway, because during the Shortening it was the only institution still
publishing. The Nomads dispute the casualty figure and cannot prove their case; the Titans have a
standing grievance over one filing; the Collective published a contradicting analysis and was
classified *Speculative*; the Ancients could settle all of it and will not.

**2 · The reckoning market — what is a person?**
Every empire's navigation burns refined crystal. The Crystalline Entity *is* crystal, and sells its
own inert dead, and the question of what counts as inert is their largest internal dispute. Eleven
powers conduct this trade very politely and never say the second sentence.

**3 · The Relighting — should the roads come back?**
Eleven empires say yes. The Bioform Collective says no, courteously, and has said so for
seventy-four years, and is patronised for it. The Shadow Realm says the dark is a *sanctification*
and is suspected of causing it. When the campaign reveals who was right, both relationships invert.

**4 · Visibility — who is allowed to be uncounted?**
Terra wants everything filed. The Shadow Realm has never given anyone a population figure. The Void
Walkers commit nothing to paper as a matter of faith. The Quantum Entities cannot be counted even in
principle. This axis is why the Intel branch is a *moral* argument and not just a mechanic.

## The defining relationships

Fifteen pairs that carry most of the galaxy's drama. Full detail in each file's attitudes section.

| Pair | The relationship |
|---|---|
| **Terra ↔ Nomads** | Mutual dependence and a wound. The count. Six downward revisions, each defensible |
| **Terra ↔ Titans** | One filing, AU 45, *instrument error.* Raised at every council for twenty-nine years |
| **Terra ↔ Silicon** | The Collective's paper was classified *Speculative*. Nine has never complained, which is worse |
| **Terra ↔ Ancients** | Terra holds their letter and cannot read it. They hold Terra's answer and will not give it |
| **Crystalline ↔ everyone** | Eleven quarterly reports contain a line item that is a person |
| **Crystalline ↔ Void Walkers** | The Standing writes in its own body to be read in ten thousand years. The Walkers write nothing. Mutual incomprehension bordering on disgust |
| **Bioform ↔ everyone** | The larder that will not use its leverage, and will not want the roads back |
| **Bioform ↔ Nomads** | The Collective fed three empires through the Shortening at cost and never mentioned it. The Fleet has never forgotten |
| **Mechanicus ↔ Shadow** | Stealth is worthless against a people who never knew what you had. The Realm's entire toolkit is inert here |
| **Mechanicus ↔ everyone** | They cannot sign a treaty, only accept a job. Eleven diplomatic services keep specialists for this |
| **Zephyr ↔ Nomads** | The same nine days barely dented one and ended the other. The Fleet brings it up |
| **Titans ↔ Void Walkers** | The slowest and the fastest. Each finds the other's relationship with time obscene |
| **Quantum ↔ Terra** | The Not-Yet called the Reading of the Names *"a civilisation gathering annually to eliminate its own possibilities."* Filed AU 22. Never withdrawn. Never answered |
| **Shadow ↔ everyone** | Universally suspected of the Unarriving, entirely innocent, thoroughly compromised |
| **Ancients ↔ everyone** | Twelve councils' worth of attendance and near-total silence, and a shrinking number of chairs |

### Who allies naturally

**Comfortable:** Terra–Bioform (both patient, both custodial). Nomads–Shadow (both operate outside
sovereignty; both sell things that are not objects). Silicon–Titans (both revere getting it right,
on wildly different timescales, and both were filed as wrong by the same office).

**Uncomfortable but frequent:** Terra–Mechanicus, because a work order is the most reliable
agreement in the galaxy once written. Crystalline–Bioform, the only two powers who think in
generations, who like each other and cannot coordinate.

**Structurally impossible:** anyone with the Quantum Entities on any question that dates the
Unarriving. Anyone with the Ancients on any question at all.

## Two rules for writing these people

**Nobody explains their own culture.** Not once, anywhere. A Mechanicus officer never says "we of
the Works believe reconnaissance is a moral failure" — a Mechanicus officer says *"no scouts.
Advance."* This is rule 3 in `03-themes.md` and it is the whole difference between good and terrible
faction writing.

**Every race is decent to somebody.** The Zephyr are horrifying and warm. The Shadow Realm are the
best hosts in the galaxy. The Ancients are the most destructive force in history and they are tired
and sorry and courteous. A roster where you can identify the villains is a roster that has stopped
being interesting.
