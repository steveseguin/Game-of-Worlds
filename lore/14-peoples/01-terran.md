# The Terran People

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/01-terran.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: repairable by strangers.** Terran design assumes the person fixing a thing will not
be the person who built it and will not have the manual. Flat sides, external fasteners, service
panels on every face, and **everything has a handle** — including things nobody will ever carry.
Nothing is streamlined. Nothing is beautiful. It is all legible.

**Palette:** olive, gun-steel, oxidised brass, warning amber. Green shaded lamps indoors. Beveled
metal everywhere, per the established visual language.

**Materials:** poured concrete at port scale; wood panelling and brass at office scale; and **paper**,
which is the Terran motif and the single most distinctive prop available to us. In a genre where every
bridge is a hologram, put a clipboard on it. Filed hardcopy, index drawers to the ceiling, pneumatic
tube terminals in the wall.

**Insignia:** a filing index tab. Not a bird, not a sword, not a star. A tab. Rank is a brass tab worn
at the collar, and the number of them is your grade, and they are cleaned rather than replaced.

**People:** uniform, worn correctly, not new. Terrans present as *unremarkable on purpose* — the
civil service aesthetic taken to a civilisation. Everything is labelled, including them: a Terran
carries a stamped identity card and produces it unasked.

## Personality

Decent, procedural, and exhausting.

A Terran in a room will establish what the question is, what the precedent is, and what the cost is,
in that order, before offering an opinion — and will genuinely not understand why this reads as
obstruction. They are the only people in the galaxy who report bad news accurately and without
softening, which everyone claims to want and nobody enjoys.

**Their humour** is dry, self-deprecating, and almost entirely about their own bureaucracy. The joke
about Terra is always told by a Terran, and it is always some version of *everyone else got a talent
and we got the paperwork*, and it ends with *do you know what I have noticed? We are still here.*

**Their virtue:** they do not boast, and they do not lose things.

**Their vice, and it is a serious one:** a Terran will let a thing go wrong *correctly* rather than
right it irregularly. Clerk Ames filed the letter that ended the galaxy, accurately, twenty minutes
before the end of her shift, and her defence forty-nine years later was that the classification was
correct — and she was right, and that is the vice, entire.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**Registrar Vance.** Chief archivist, and effectively head of state. Nineteen years in post, the
longest tenure since the Unarriving, and there is no legal process by which she could leave. She has
read every casualty roll personally. She has known for a long time that the count is wrong and has not
said so in a chamber.

**Sweep-Admiral Ito.** Commands expansion, and thinks the archive is a liability. Wants to trade
Registry access to the Nomads for four centuries of route knowledge — a deal that is genuinely good
and that genuinely ends Terran primacy. He argues it in open session. Vance considers it close to
treason. He is not a villain; he is the best argument for a bad idea in the setting.

**The eleven Deputy Registrars**, one per Gate, each with a dead lane and a maintenance bill and a
constituency. Terran domestic politics is a fight over which corpse gets funded, and the Ordel and
Vail delegations have voted as a bloc for sixty years on every question regardless of subject, because
their lanes might be reachable again if a Lamp is ever lit.

**How you rise:** by filing accurately for a very long time. Promotion is by seniority moderated by
audit, and the audit is real. There is no fast track and there is no patronage worth the name, and a
Terran career is thirty years of being correct.

**How you fall:** a finding of *irregularity* — not error. Errors are corrected and recorded and
survived. Irregularity is procedural deviation, and it ends careers regardless of outcome, including
when the deviation produced the better result. Especially then.

## Beliefs

**On death:** unsentimental and precise. A person is dead when the manifest is reconciled, and until
then they are **unreconciled**, which is a different word doing enormous work. Terra has no theology
of an afterlife. It has a filing status.

**On the Unarriving:** that it was a failure of *record*. Every shoal that killed a fleet was known
to somebody once; the knowledge existed and was lost. This is the founding conviction of modern Terra
and it is why they file, and it is also **false**, and they have built a civilisation on it.

**On themselves:** that they survived because they were lucky and fat, not strong. This is taught
deliberately in the naval academies — *we were lucky and we were fat* — as an inoculation against
pride, and it works, and it is the most attractive thing about them.

**On the future:** that if you write everything down carefully enough, nothing is truly lost. They
are wrong. They know, at some level, that they are wrong, and they keep writing.

## Rituals

**Daily — the reserve check.** Every Terran household keeps a pantry it does not need, and checks it,
and a bare shelf is a scandal. The Standing Reserve is audited publicly and reported like weather. It
is a hundred million people performing the same small superstition about hunger, and none of them
would call it a ritual.

**Coming of age — the first filing.** At sixteen a Terran files a document into the public
record — anything, however trivial, in their own hand, correctly classified — and it stays there
permanently, under their name, forever. Adults look their own first filing up. Most are embarrassing.
Nobody may withdraw one.

**Death — the Reading of the Names.** Annually, in every Gate, the unreconciled manifests are read
aloud. Four to nine days. Attendance is not compulsory and is universal; shifts are organised, and
children take turns. **It is read badly, by ordinary people, in ordinary voices**, and that is the
point — an actor would ruin it.

**War — the standing order.** Before a Terran fleet commits to an unswept sector, the chart is
transmitted home and the crossing is entered in the log *in advance*, with the expected loss. The
number is written down before anybody dies. Officers describe the practice as morale-destroying and
have never once proposed abolishing it.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Silicon Collective** — Correct, tireless, and impossible to thank. There is a standing internal
memorandum recommending the *Speculative* classification be revisited. It has been circulating for
three years.

**Zephyr Swarm** — Establish where they are. Beyond that, guidance is not possible.

**Crystalline Entity** — Treated with the elaborate care Terra reserves for a thing it is doing wrong
and cannot stop. Envoy postings are limited to eight months, officially for reasons of isolation.

**Void Walkers** — Admired professionally and considered close to criminally negligent. A people who
refuse to write things down are, to Terra, a people burning a library annually on principle.

**Mechanicus** — The most reliable partner in the galaxy and the least reachable. Terra maintains
fourteen specialists who draft nothing but work orders.

**Bioform Collective** — Quietly Terra's favourite, and both would deny it. Two custodial peoples who
think in generations. Terra does not understand the position on the Trellis and has stopped arguing.

**Star Nomads** — Indispensable, aggrieved, and correct. This is the relationship Vance loses sleep
over, and Ito wants to solve it with a trade that would end Terra's primacy.

**The Ancients** — Terra holds their letter in cabinet forty-one and cannot read it. Vance has
requested an audience nine times. Her grandmother's colleague asked thirty-one.

**Quantum Entities** — Settlement in stamps, in advance, in full. There is no policy on the AU 22
comment about the Reading of the Names because no Registrar has been willing to draft one.

**Titan Lords** — A grievance Terra knows it deserves. Vance has apologised twice in writing, both
accepted formally, and the file still says *instrument error*, and she has not been able to change
it, and that is exactly the problem the Titans are describing.

**Shadow Realm** — Suspected, courted, and never believed. Terra has bought four fragments and has
never worked out that there are eleven.
