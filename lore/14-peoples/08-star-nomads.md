# The Fleet

Pure lore. Place and economy are in `../12-civilisations/08-star-nomads.md`.

---

## Aesthetic

**Form language: interior.** The Nomads are the only people whose aesthetic is entirely inward-facing,
because nine centuries of living aboard means nobody has ever cared what a convoy looks like from
outside. Corridors, low ceilings, hammocks and fold-down berths, everything padded, everything within
reach, nothing wasted.

**And every surface has been decorated by somebody.** This is the defining fact. Hand-painted panels,
stitched hangings, carved fittings, layered over each other by three generations of people who lived
in that exact corridor and had nothing else to do in transit. A Nomad interior is a **palimpsest of
personal taste**, which makes it the visual opposite of the Mechanicus quilt: the same accretion, but
every layer is somebody's affection rather than somebody's repair.

**Palette:** warm and saturated and completely uncoordinated — everything came from a different port.
Reds, ochres, deep blues, brass, textile in eleven traditions. Nothing matches. Nothing was ever meant
to.

**Insignia:** a convoy mark, hand-painted, and it is different on every hull because each was painted
by that hull's crew. A Nomad can identify a convoy at a glance and cannot explain how.

**People:** layered, patched, and adorned — jewellery from eleven worlds, worn all at once, most of it
inherited. Nomads dress like the inside of their ships.

**And on every vessel in the Fleet, without exception: a wall of names.** The unreconciled from that
convoy's lineage, engraved, in the main passage, where everybody has to walk past it.

## Personality

Warm, funny, mercantile, sentimental, and aggrieved.

A Nomad will feed you, tell you a story about a route, sell you something you did not intend to buy, and
somewhere in the middle of it mention the count. They are the best company in the galaxy and the
conversation always eventually arrives at the same place, and they know it does, and they do it anyway.

*Sit down, sit down. That is either integrity or bad manners and I have not decided.*

**Their humour** is the broadest and most generous of the twelve, and it is heavily about other peoples'
eating habits, and the Void Walkers are the standing target.

**Their virtue:** they cannot refuse somebody who came back. Keth asked Ilsa Rell a price for the
*Ninefold Patience*, received silence, and gave her the whole thing anyway — *write it down anyway.
Somebody should have it, and you are the only one who ever comes back.* That is the Fleet entire.

**Their vice:** **they cannot stop bringing up the count**, and it costs them. Every negotiation, every
council, every trade — the figure surfaces, and eleven empires have learned to brace for it, and the
bracing is now itself an insult. The Fleet is right about the count. Being right has not helped and they
will not stop.

## Leadership and structure — AU 74

**Berth-rights and convoy councils.** A convoy governs itself; the Fleet coordinates through councils
weighted by tonnage and seniority. It works well at convoy scale and has one devastating flaw at galactic
scale: **no territory, therefore no sovereignty, therefore no standing.** The largest single population in
the galaxy is seated in every forum as a *commercial party*, cannot bring a claim, cannot be a signatory,
and has lost every dispute that mattered.

**Convoy-Master Hallam of the Ring.** Senior voice in council, forty years in the Ring, and the closest
thing the Fleet has to a head of state — which she would dispute at length, because claiming to be one
would imply the Fleet is a nation, and the Fleet has spent seventy-four years being told it is not.
Hallam's project is sovereign recognition. She has brought it to council nine times.

**Keth of the Fleet.** Trace-broker, not a leader, and more consequential than most leaders. Holds the
*Ninefold Patience* in a berth he will not name and has held it for forty-three years without telling
the Registry, because the Registry would catalogue it and it would stop being theirs.

**How you rise:** berth-rights and reputation, which in the Fleet are the same currency. A broker who
sells a bad trace once is finished, because everyone finds out inside a season.

**How you fall:** you lose your berth. It is the only real sanction the Fleet has, it is imposed by
council, and two of the three sponsors of terraforming motions since AU 30 have suffered it — which is
the clearest evidence available of how seriously the Fleet means its refusal to want a planet.

## Beliefs

**On death:** a route ends. That is the formulation, and it is not evasion — a Nomad life is a sequence of
ports, and the funeral is the recitation of it. Nobody is buried on Kettering. **The eleven thousand
berths assigned to hulls that did not arrive in AU 0 have never been reassigned**, are swept and lit and
maintained, and are the largest line item in the Fleet's accounts, and have never been questioned.

**On the Unarriving:** the thing that happened to *them*, specifically, more than to anyone. They lost more
of their people in nine days than any other power lost in the whole Shortening — because home was a
schedule and almost nobody was at home — and they cannot document it, because they filed with each other
rather than with Terran ports. Which was a sovereign choice, made proudly, for nine centuries, and it
erased their dead.

**On themselves:** that a promise is worth more than a place. The chit — a bearer note redeemable at any
Fleet berth, backed by nothing but the word of somebody who might not arrive — is the galaxy's first
money and, to a Nomad, the most honest instrument ever written.

**On the future:** they want the roads back more than anyone. Not for trade. Because eleven thousand
berths are empty and the Fleet has never accepted that the people assigned to them are not somewhere.

## Rituals

**Daily — the tannoy.** Convoy announcements, read aloud in a formal cadence inherited directly from
Concord port protocol, for a schedule that no longer connects to anything. Arrival times, berth
assignments, docking sequences. Every day. It is not mourning; it is closer to keeping the language alive.

**Coming of age — the first manifest.** At fourteen a Nomad signs a manifest in their own name — real
cargo, real liability, usually trivial — and is thereafter a party who can be dealt with. **A Nomad's
majority is commercial**, not physical, and it is why fourteen-year-olds negotiate.

**Death — the recitation of the route.** Every port, in order, over a lifetime. A long-lived broker's
funeral takes most of a day and is attended by anybody who shared any part of it, which is how you learn
who your grandmother travelled with.

**War — the reading of the schedule.** Before a Fleet action, the old Concord timetables are read: the
services that ran through the contested sector, their times, and the names of the hulls that ran them.
It takes about twenty minutes and every crew stands for it, and its function is to remind everybody
present what a working galaxy sounded like.

**And the one nobody else does — the rite for a salted probe.** The Fleet holds a funeral for an enemy
probe destroyed by counter-intelligence. Everyone else finds this sentimental or affected. It is neither.
**A salted probe is a machine that was given a false schedule and flew into it**, which is precisely what
happened to the Nomad people — handed a timetable that turned out to be a lie, and an entire
civilisation flew into it on faith. They do not mourn the probe. They *recognise* it. It is also why the
Fleet will not salt, has never salted, and has lost sectors over the refusal.

## Attitudes to the other eleven

**Terran Empire** — Indispensable and unforgivable, and we deal with them daily. Vance is decent. Ito
wants to buy our charts with the archive and does not understand that we would rather have the count
corrected than the paper.

**Silicon Collective** — They offered to reconcile the manifests for free. We have not answered. Hallam
cannot decide whether accepting would be relief or surrender, and it has been eleven years.

**Zephyr Swarm** — The same nine days barely dented them and ended us. They know we are angry. They let us
be angry, which is somehow worse.

**Crystalline Entity** — We buy the least and ask the most, and we are the only customers who ask about
the determination. It is why Sarn will see us.

**Void Walkers** — Our only real friends and our direct competitors. They think we are shopkeepers, we
think they are romantics, both true, and neither of us will sit at the other's table hungry.

**Mechanicus** — They do not require routes, which we find genuinely upsetting, and they have listened
politely eleven times. Fourth said it enjoyed our persistence, which was not authorised, and Keth has kept
the transcript.

**Bioform Collective** — They fed three empires through the Shortening at cost and never invoiced it. We
remember AU 12 and AU 14 and AU 19. We bring it up. They wish we would not.

**The Ancients** — They owe us thirty-one names in a berth on Kettering and several million elsewhere, and
they attend every council, and they have never once looked at us.

**Quantum Entities** — Some of them say it has not happened yet. We have a hull with thirty-one places set
in the galley. We do not discuss it with them and they have never raised it, which is the single most
tactful thing any Not-Yet has ever done.

**Titan Lords** — They were told *instrument error* and they have never let it go, and neither have we, and
we have more in common with Ordel Deep than either of us finds comfortable.

**Shadow Realm** — Sable gave a fragment away for nothing to somebody who could not pay. We have never
forgotten it and we have never told anyone, and it is why the Fleet has never joined an accusation
against them.
