# Lore Status

Last reviewed: **2026-07-26**

## Intent

Game of Worlds is becoming a strategy game whose fiction is generated by its constraints:

- travel is blind, so exploration is a wager;
- crystal buys knowledge and movement, not magic;
- each race's refusal is more important than its bonus;
- sweeping a hazard converts loss into permanent shared infrastructure;
- the player should leave history behind, not merely consume a prewritten history.

The strongest idea is the fit between mechanics and setting. The largest risk is that a large,
fast-growing corpus can appear authoritative while its delivery state and internal hierarchy remain
unclear.

## Current delivery

### Shipped

- Race-specific advisor reactions and revised event language.
- Map tooltips that name and explain all eleven sector types.
- An in-game Codex with premise, sectors, the twelve races, and key vocabulary.
- Curated shoal naming: the sweeper chooses a permanent chart name, the server records the namer and
  turn, and known map snapshots carry the name.
- Tests that pin race constants, sector types, advisor coverage, naming containment, schema migration,
  protocol registration, and browser reachability.

### Production-shaped but incomplete

- `17-the-feed/`: approximately 108 proposed Rell variants keyed to real engine events. The shipped
  game borrows its vocabulary and some lines, but there is no complete data-driven variant rotation.
- `22-act-one.md`: three mission scripts, but no campaign/scenario runtime consumes them.
- `24-anthology/`: Codex-sized material, but only the compact hand-written Codex is reachable in game.
- `18-naming-the-dark.md`: the naming moment is shipped; later battle, movement, probe, and build
  messages still mostly use sector numbers instead of permanent chart names.

### Design only

- Wonders and their exclusive technologies/hulls (`13-wonders/`).
- Artifact discovery and the Unattributed (`27-the-unattributed.md`).
- Most campaign structure, voice recording, and narrative art production.

## Decisions needed

### 1. Who is the advisor?

**Conflict:** `05-characters.md`, `06-campaign.md`, `08-open-questions.md`, `09-production.md`, and
`19-canon-and-variance.md` lock Rell as the only voice. The shipped `public/js/advisor.js` instead gives
all twelve races distinct faction registers.

Both choices are coherent, but they imply different products:

- **Rell-only:** strongest authored protagonist and cheapest voice production; best fit for the
  Terran-only campaign.
- **Faction adjutants in multiplayer, Rell in campaign:** respects player race identity and the shipped
  implementation, but the canon and production plan must explicitly separate the two channels.

Recommendation: adopt the second model. It preserves Rell's authored campaign role without making a
Mechanicus or Bioform player sound Terran. Do not record voice until this is explicit.

### 2. Is the Unstable Star dangerous?

`server/lib/map.js` describes dangerous radiation, and the Codex/tooltips call it dangerous, but type 3
has no hazard flag and applies no damage. Decide one:

- implement a distinct radiation mechanic with clear counterplay; or
- make the description atmospheric and explicitly non-damaging.

Until then, the player-facing wording overpromises a mechanic.

### 3. Does `artifact` become a system?

The map generator stores artifact values on some worlds, but nothing consumes them. `25-crystal.md` and
`27-the-unattributed.md` offer a strong interpretation, not an implemented fact. Approve a mechanic and
cost it before promoting any artifact fiction into player-facing copy.

### 4. Is the campaign a real product target?

The campaign has a premise, structure, protagonist, and three detailed missions, but no scenario format,
mission runner, or delivery milestone. Either:

- commit to a small vertical slice using `22-act-one.md`; or
- keep campaign material explicitly archival and focus narrative effort on multiplayer delivery.

The current middle state invites more mission writing without a path to play it.

## Issues found in this review

### Fixed

- Shoal naming read `activeGames[gameId].turn`, a field that is not authoritative and normally does not
  exist. Names were therefore recorded on turn 1 and the intended naming deadline never closed. Naming
  now uses `gameState.turns[gameId]`, with a regression test.
- The picker allowed repeated renames during its nominal window. A `namechosen` schema flag now makes
  the curated choice genuinely one-time.
- Persisted chart names were available only after a focused `sector::` response. Known `mapstate::`
  entries now carry the encoded name, namer, and turn, so reconnects and other observers can see the
  shared chart rather than only the original sweeper.

### Still open

- Most server messages do not resolve a sector number to its chart name.
- The Codex delivers only a small fraction of the useful material and has no deeper browse path.
- `04-factions/` has only two full doctrine bibles; this is a real gap, but lower priority than delivery.
- `02-timeline.md` and `20-master-timeline.md` duplicate eras. Their roles are now documented, but future
  date edits still require discipline.
- File-level status labels are inconsistent (`CANON`, `READY TO USE`, `DESIGN PROPOSAL`, `Pure lore`).
  This README defines the replacement two-axis model; files should migrate when next edited.
- The corpus was produced very quickly and has not had an independent line-level prose edit. Tests guard
  mechanics and selected vocabulary, not repetition, tonal sameness, or accidental continuity claims.

## Recommended order

1. Decide advisor identity and Unstable Star behaviour.
2. Finish chart-name use in event messages.
3. Choose campaign vertical slice versus multiplayer-only narrative delivery.
4. If campaign proceeds, build one scenario format and ship one mission before writing more missions.
5. Expand the in-game Codex from the anthology only after there is navigation, unlock logic, or a clear
   player reason to read it.
6. Defer the ten remaining military doctrine bibles, Wonders, and artifact mythology until the delivered
   layer catches up.
