# Interludes — the funny ones

Status: **PROPOSED**. Production-bound: briefing-screen scenes and codex entries, no new systems.

This exists to fix a defect the report card has carried since it was written. **Emotional range** has
been the lowest-graded thing in the writing for four revisions, and the con was specific and
unflattering: *"the Star Nomads are described as the funniest people in the galaxy and have never once
been funny; Liaison Fourth is potentially hilarious and is only ever used for pathos."*
`17-the-feed/06-refusals.md` went further and named the three scenes that were missing, then did not
write them.

Here they are.

**Why this is load-bearing and not decoration.** The craft literature is blunt about it: comic relief is
a buffer against cognitive overload, and contrast makes both the comedy and the gravity more potent.
A hundred and twenty thousand words of unbroken seriousness is not intensity, it is fatigue — and it
flattens the moments that are supposed to land. The rule stays exactly as written: **humour never
touches a loss.** All three of these are set where nobody has died.

---

## One — Four Hours at Ordel Deep

*A Titan house dining hall, **AU 25.** A table long enough to have weather. **Courier Wren** — Void
Walker, in her forties here; she was twenty-two and flying point when the Lamps went out — is seated at
the wrong end of it. **Shipmaster Vaun** presides, twenty-seven years into the same command, entirely at
ease. Wren has a manifest to deliver. It is in her jacket. It has been in her jacket for some time.*

> **VAUN:** You will take some of the root.
>
> **WREN:** I have eaten.
>
> **VAUN:** You will take some of the root.
>
> *(A vegetable the size of a child is brought. Wren looks at it. It is looked at for a while.)*
>
> **WREN:** Shipmaster. The manifest.
>
> **VAUN:** We do not discuss business in the first two hours.
>
> **WREN:** *(flat)* How long have we been sitting down?
>
> **VAUN:** Forty minutes.
>
> **WREN:** *Forty minutes* is Bell to Anselm's Reach. Forty minutes is the longest lane in the galaxy.
> I have crossed the longest lane in the galaxy in the time it has taken this table to arrive at
> vegetables.
>
> **VAUN:** *(pleased)* Then you will be comfortable. You are used to the duration.
>
> *(Silence. Cutlery. Somewhere, a great deal of stone.)*
>
> **WREN:** May I ask a question that is not business.
>
> **VAUN:** Please.
>
> **WREN:** Does anything on this world happen quickly?
>
> **VAUN:** *(considering it seriously, which is worse)* The *Forbearance of Kell* lost a keel step
> once, in a shoal, at speed. That was quick. Everybody involved found it distressing and we have not
> repeated it.
>
> **WREN:** So the answer is no.
>
> **VAUN:** The answer is that we have arranged for the quick things to be rare. You have arranged the
> opposite, and I would not presume to tell you it is wrong, and I notice that you are thirty years
> younger than me and look considerably worse.
>
> *(Wren, for the first time, laughs. It is short and it surprises her.)*
>
> **WREN:** Give me the root.

**Notes.** Two peoples whose entire relationship with time is incompatible, sitting at the same table,
both correct. Every rule holds: Vaun uses no contractions, Wren states transit times rather than
distances, business is not discussed in the first two hours. The joke is structural, not verbal — and
it turns warm in the last four lines, which is the point. Nobody has died.

*Placement: codex, unlocked on first contact with either race. Or the briefing for a Titan mission,
where a player about to fight the slowest fleet in the galaxy first learns what slow means to them.*

## Two — What Is A Scout For

*Not a scene. A document: eleven answers Liaison Fourth collected across thirty-six years, filed in the
appendix to Yard Nine's variance, in the order received. Fourth's own annotations are the last line of
each.*

> **The question, as put to eleven empires:** *What is a scout for?*
>
> **Terran Registry.** "A scout is for establishing the facts of a sector prior to committal of a
> substantive force." — *This is a description of a scout. It is not an answer.*
>
> **Silicon Collective.** "To reduce the variance." — *We asked what the variance was for. They said
> that was a different question. It was not.*
>
> **Zephyr Swarm.** "We do not understand. Some of we goes. Then we know." — *This is the only answer
> that was not evasive and it is the one I understand least.*
>
> **Crystalline Entity.** "You are asking why the brief hurry. I have four hundred years of an answer
> and you would not survive the delivery." — *I offered to wait. Elder Sarn said that was the correct
> response and then said nothing for eleven months. I do not believe I was being mocked.*
>
> **Void Walkers.** "It is for arriving first." — *I asked first at what. The courier had left.*
>
> **Bioform Collective.** "So the thing that goes ahead is the thing you can bear to lose." — *Filed.
> Reconsidered. Filed again.*
>
> **Star Nomads.** "So you know what the route is worth before you pay for it, love." — *He also
> attempted to sell me a scout. I do not require a scout. He was aware of this.*
>
> **The Ancients.** "There were scouts." — *The Emissary said nothing further and I did not ask
> further, because it did not appear to be withholding. It appeared to be finished.*
>
> **Quantum Entities.** "It would tell you what you would have found, had you gone." — *I have modelled
> this sentence for two shifts and I believe it may be correct.*
>
> **Titan Lords.** "We do not build one. Ask a house that does." — *This is the same answer my own shift
> gave me. I have not reported the coincidence.*
>
> **Shadow Realm.** "What a good question." — *That is the entire response. It was given warmly, over an
> excellent meal, and I was returned to my transport four days later having learned nothing and enjoyed
> myself. I have thought about this more than any of the others.*
>
> **Twelfth entry, unattributed, added in AU 74:** *You send a scout so that the thing that dies is
> small. I do not know whether that is a reason. I have not been able to establish whether it is a
> reason. I would like somebody to build it and tell me.*

**Notes.** This is the funniest thing in the folder and it is also the saddest, and it is the same
material doing both — which is the whole argument for tonal range. Eleven civilisations
characterised in one line each, plus a machine's deadpan marginalia, plus the reveal that the twelfth
answer is Fourth's own and it took thirty-six years.

The Shadow Realm entry is the best joke in the project: a four-day diplomatic incident summarised as
*"What a good question,"* and the liaison genuinely enjoyed it.

*Placement: codex, unlocked when Yard Nine's variance surfaces — Act Three. It pays off Fourth's
thirty-six-year question from `10-the-long-file/06-mechanicus.md` and `15-series-twelve/06-mechanicus.md`.*

## Three — Complaint Regarding the Absence of Kitchens

*A Registry intake form, filed from Sarn's World. Reproduced with its box headings, because the boxes
are the joke.*

> **REGISTRY FORM 41-C — POSTING CONDITIONS, ADVERSE**
>
> **Filed by:** Envoy Third Class Aubrin, Sarn's World station.
> **Duration of posting to date:** six months, eleven days.
> **Nature of complaint:** *(one box, ruled)*
>
> There are no kitchens on this planet.
>
> **Elaboration, if required:** *(one box, ruled)*
>
> I wish to be clear that this is not a supply failure. I have supplies. I have eleven months of
> supplies and a heater. What I do not have, and what nobody on this world has ever had, is a *kitchen*
> — a room in which food is made, by somebody, for somebody else, and then eaten by both.
>
> The Standing does not eat. There is nothing wrong with this. It is simply that I had not understood,
> before six months ago, how much of what I thought was language turns out to be about meals.
> Yesterday I attempted to explain the phrase "breaking bread" to Elder Sarn and got as far as
> "breaking" before I had to stop and think about whether the rest of it was true.
>
> **Requested remedy:** *(one box, ruled)*
>
> None available. I am aware.
>
> **Additional remarks:** *(one box, ruled)*
>
> Elder Sarn has been extremely kind about this. It rang, once, very low, for most of an afternoon,
> which I am told is what they do when they cannot help. I sat on it and ate a tinned fish.
>
> I would like the record to show that it was the best posting I have ever had.
>
> ---
>
> *(Below, in a different hand:)*
>
> **INTAKE — Chart-Warden's note.** Classification: Commercial — Conditions — Advisory. No action
> available. **Retained in full**, contrary to the standing instruction to abstract 41-C filings to a
> single line, on the grounds that the abstraction would have read *"no kitchens"* and would have been
> accurate.
>
> Envoy Aubrin's rotation was extended twice, at her request. — *R.*

**Notes.** Three of twelve worlds have no food culture at all — Sill, the Works, Sarn's World — and
`12-civilisations/README.md` records that Terran envoys independently filed the same complaint about
all three. This is that complaint, and it turns out to be a love letter.

It also does something the folder needed and could not get from testimony: it shows the Registry
*working properly* and being kind, which is the counterweight to Ames filing correctly and ending the
galaxy. Rell breaks a formatting rule to preserve something that is not evidence of anything. Fourth
Terran in the margin-motif chain — Halloway, Yard Nine, Rell at 8-F, Rell here.

*Placement: codex, unlocked on first contact with the Crystalline Entity.*

## The rule these three are written under

Restated because it is the one that will get eroded first:

**Humour never touches a loss.** Not obliquely, not with a lightening of tone, not once. Hazards,
battles, probes and notifications are humour-free permanently. Comedy lives at tables, in markets, in
paperwork, and in the gap between civilisations that cannot understand each other — places where the
worst available outcome is embarrassment.

All three of these are set at a meal, in an archive, and inside a filing form. That is not a
coincidence and it is not a limitation. **It is where this setting is funny**, and it is funny for the
same reason it is sad: because these are careful people, doing their jobs, in a galaxy that does not
make it easy.
