# Timeline — Historical Lineage

Status: **PROPOSED**.

A warning before the dates: **a timeline is a tool, not a deliverable.** Nobody plays a game
for its chronology, and every hour spent on the fourth century of the Trellis Concord is an
hour not spent on a line of dialogue. This document exists to stop us contradicting ourselves,
and it is deliberately thin in the deep past and thick in the last hundred years, because
that is where the game is set.

Dates are given in **AU** — *After the Unarriving* — the only calendar all twelve empires
share, because it is the only event all twelve empires witnessed. Negative years are BU.
The present day is **AU 74**.

That is itself a story fact worth noticing: the galaxy's shared calendar starts at a
catastrophe. There is no year zero of anybody's founding. Everyone counts from the same nine
days.

---

## The eras

### The Long Trellis — before records
**Status: deliberately vague. Keep it that way.**

Every civilisation's oldest surviving records already assume the Trellis. There is no
discovery narrative anywhere in the galaxy. Twelve species, on twelve different timelines,
each reached the stars and found the roads already swept and already lit.

This should feel wrong to the player, and eventually it should feel *deliberate*. The
Trellis was not found by anyone. It was **waiting**, and it was maintained, and for most of
history nobody thought to ask by whom.

The Ancients know. The Ancients have known the entire time.

### The Concord Centuries — roughly BU 900 to BU 60
The high period. Lit transit made the galaxy effectively one place. Trade was continuous, war
was rare and small, and every one of the twelve races specialised into the thing it was best
at, because there was no reason not to.

This is the crucial fact about the Concord, and it is why the game's races are so lopsided:
**specialisation was safe, so everyone over-specialised.** The Star Nomads gave up planets
because routes were guaranteed. The Crystalline Entity built no weapons because nobody could
reach them. The Zephyr Swarm scaled to numbers only continuous supply could feed. The
Mechanicus stopped building scouts, because on a lit lane, what is a scout for?

Every capped tech branch and every locked hull in `RACE_ACCESS` is a decision made during the
Concord, when it was correct.

### The Quiet Decade — BU 60 to BU 0
In hindsight, warnings. At the time, maintenance faults.

Lamps began going dark in ones and twos, at the fringes, in no pattern that any admiralty
could find. Lanes were re-routed. Insurance rates rose. Two minor wars were fought over
re-routed traffic. The Ancients, who had never in recorded history requested anything, quietly
began asking every empire to reduce transit volume, and were quietly ignored, because the
request came with no reason attached.

**Campaign beat:** the Ancients' request survives in the Terran Registry, filed under
commercial correspondence. The player can read it. It is four sentences long, it does not
explain itself, and it is the most damning document in the galaxy.

### The Unarriving — AU 0, nine days
Not "the Lamps went out." That is what the histories say, and it is wrong, and the difference
matters enormously for how we write about it.

What happened is that **ships stopped arriving.**

The Lamps did not visibly fail; there was no galaxy-wide signal, no flash, nothing anyone
observed. What people observed was that a freighter due at midday did not make orbit. Then
neither did the next one. Then a relief squadron sent to look for the freighter also did not
arrive. For nine days, every vessel in superluminal transit anywhere in the galaxy ended its
journey in a dark it had not been built to survive, and the only evidence available to anyone
was an absence in a docking schedule.

**The number lost is not known.** The count was kept by the Trellis. Every empire's figure is
an estimate, every estimate is politically useful to whoever published it, and the discrepancy
between the Terran figure and the Nomad figure has been a diplomatic wound for seventy years.

Write it always as an absence, never as an explosion. The horror of the Unarriving is
administrative: it is a list of names of people who were supposed to be somewhere.

### The Shortening — AU 0 to AU 30
The collapse, and it was not military. It was logistical.

Interdependent economies built on guaranteed transit unwound in weeks. Colonies designed to be
fed from three systems away starved in front of full warehouses that were four days' dark
transit distant. Populations that had never needed to be self-sufficient discovered what they
had traded away. More people died in the Shortening than in the Unarriving itself — by every
estimate, by a wide margin — and this is the part of history that the empires do not
memorialise, because famine has no enemy in it.

By AU 30 the galaxy had contracted to what could be reached blind. Most of the map went from
"routine" to "unvisited" inside a single lifetime. **The fog of war in the game is this. It is
not ignorance. It is retreat.**

### The Sweeping — AU 30 to present
The recovery, and the game's actual present tense.

Somewhere around AU 30, admiralties across the galaxy independently arrived at the same brutal
insight: *if you cannot see the shoal, you can still own it.* Send a fleet, accept the losses,
hold the sector, sweep it, and you have bought one safe span forever. It is slow, it is
expensive, it is paid in hulls, and it works.

Seventy years of it have produced the current galaxy: small pockets of thoroughly charted,
perfectly safe space around each capital, and beyond them the Deep, into which every empire is
now expanding — and, for the first time since the Concord, running into each other.

That last clause is why there is a war. Not ambition. **Overlap.** Two empires sweeping toward
each other from opposite directions eventually arrive in the same sector, both holding a
legitimate trace, both having paid for it in dead crews. There is no diplomatic framework for
this, because during the Concord it could never happen.

### The Relighting — now
Every major power has a Lamp programme. None has succeeded. Most are secret. All of them are
about ninety per cent the same research, which is why Scientific Victory is a real victory
condition: the empire that finishes the tree first is the empire that can relight a lane.

Publicly, the argument is about restoration and prosperity. Privately, every admiralty
understands the strategic truth: **the first empire to relight a lane can move fleets at
Concord speed through a galaxy that cannot.**

Nobody is asking why the Lamps went out. The one people who could answer have said nothing
for seventy-four years.

## The three things a player should learn, in order

Structure, not chronology. This is the actual deliverable of this document.

1. **Hours 0–2: the galaxy is dangerous.** Taught by mechanics only. You lose ships to a
   shoal. Nobody explains history. You learn that knowing costs.
2. **Hours 2–10: the galaxy used to be safe.** Delivered as texture — an idiom, a ruin, an
   old chart with lanes on it that go somewhere you cannot reach. The player assembles the
   Trellis themselves before anyone names it.
3. **Hours 10+: it was switched off on purpose.** The campaign's turn. And the moment it
   lands, the player's own Lamp programme — the thing they have been building toward, the
   thing the victory screen rewards — becomes the most frightening object on the map.

If a player reaches hour ten and has not independently wondered "wait, who *built* the roads,"
the texture pass failed, and no amount of exposition in Act 3 will fix it.
