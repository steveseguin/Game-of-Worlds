# Registry Series 12 — Accounts of the Sweeping

Status: **PROPOSED**. Twelve testimonies, one per race. The companion to `../10-the-long-file/`, and
deliberately not the same book.

---

## What it is

Officially: *Registry Series 12, Accounts of the Sweeping.* Chart-Warden Rell refuses to nickname it.
The clerks in cabinet forty-one call it **the Second File** anyway.

Rell numbered it twelve because there are twelve peoples. A deputy queried the numbering as
non-sequential and was told, in writing, that the sequence was fine. It is the only sentimental act
anyone has ever caught Rell in, and it is on the record.

## How it differs from the Long File — the whole design

| | **Series 9 — the Long File** | **Series 12 — the Second File** |
|---|---|---|
| Collected by | Ilsa Rell, AU 28–68 | Chart-Warden Rell, AU 71–74 |
| Subject | The nine days | The seventy years since |
| Witnesses are | People who **saw** something | People who **do** something |
| Every entry is about | An ending | The work |
| Most are | Dead by AU 74 | Alive, and mid-sentence |
| The question behind it | *Why is the galaxy dark?* | *What does it cost to light one corner of it?* |

Ilsa collected accounts of a catastrophe because her own had no ending, and she went looking for
somebody else's that did, and none of them had one either. That is a book about grief.

Rell inherited the cabinet at thirty-one, read all eleven entries, and noticed something their
grandmother had not: **the file says nothing whatsoever about the people currently dying.** Series 9 is
seventy years of testimony about nine days. Meanwhile every season Rell signs notifications for crews
lost buying single sectors, one shoal at a time, and there is no series for them, and nobody has ever
asked a Bioform tender what it is like to lose a hull she raised.

So Rell started asking. Quietly, on their own time, in the margins of an operational post — which is
exactly how their grandmother started, and Rell is aware of that, and has not remarked on it.

## The structural payoff, and it is the inverse of the first book

The Long File's twelve facts assemble into the quarantine. Read all twelve and you know why the Lamps
went out.

The Second File's twelve accounts assemble into something nobody was looking for:

> **The Sweeping is working.**

Twelve accounts of unbearable, specific, itemised cost — and read together they show that the galaxy is
measurably safer than it was in AU 30. Shoals swept. Corridors held. Mouths charted by absence and
plotted around. Colonies that outlived the fleets that bought them. Seventy years of dying has actually
purchased something, and **nobody in the galaxy has noticed, because every empire only ever sees its own
losses.**

Rell notices. Rell notices because a Chart-Warden reads everybody's manifests, and because their
grandmother taught them to read eleven peoples' accounts side by side, and because they were looking for
something else entirely.

**And the edge on it:** if the galaxy can be made safe one swept sector at a time, then relighting the
lanes was never the only option, and the Bioform Collective has been saying so courteously for
seventy-four years. The Second File is the evidence for the Mother Grove's premise, assembled
accidentally by a Terran archivist who does not know that is what she has proved.

That inversion is the reason to write this second book rather than more of the first.

## Craft rules — same as Series 9, plus one

The six rules in `../10-the-long-file/README.md` all still apply: no testimony explains its own race;
grammatical signature over invented vocabulary; one concrete physical detail each; the interviewer is
audible only through the gaps; 350–500 words; nobody is lying.

**And a seventh, specific to this book: nobody here is finished.** Every witness in the Long File was
recounting a completed thing. Every witness here is in the middle of it — still on shift, still tending,
still watching — and not one of these accounts ends with a conclusion. They end with the witness going
back to work.

That is the difference between a book about the dead and a book about the living, and it is the reason
this is the more useful collection for the game: **the player is one of these people, not one of those.**
