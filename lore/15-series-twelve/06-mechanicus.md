# Account 6 — Mechanicus

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Third Shift, Yard Nine |
| **The work** | Building the first Mechanicus scout in eleven hundred years |
| **Recorded** | AU 74 |
| **About** | There is no pattern. They had to ask what a scout is for |

---

Variance accepted. Work commenced. Reporting as requested.

Annotation AU 12, sector 9-D, *requires heavier hull, revisit.* Due this decade. Heavier hull available.
Cost of heavier hull exceeds cost of prior survey. Cheaper option: look first. Variance filed. Variance
accepted at the fourth shift. No objection was raised at any level.

Requirement: one survey hull. Light. Fast. Minimal armament. Capable of returning.

Difficulty: there is no pattern.

We searched. Records are complete to BU 1140. Scout patterns 1 through 6 are referenced in indices and the
drawings are not present. Consolidation order BU 640 purged eleven thousand unreferenced patterns to
recover archive volume. The order was correct; nothing had referenced a scout pattern in five hundred
years. The purge is documented. The material was reshaped.

So we are designing from nothing.

We have the hull mathematics. That is not the difficulty. We can build light and we can build fast and we
have run the structures.

We do not know what it is for.

We wish to be exact, since this will be filed. We understand the stated purpose: it goes ahead, it observes,
it returns, and then a decision is made. We can build a vessel that does the first three. The fourth is not
a specification we know how to write. What is the decision. What changes. On what basis does an advance stop.

Nothing in eleven hundred years of our records contains an instruction to stop.

We filed a query. Standard form, addressed to Liaison Fourth, on the grounds that Fourth has thirty-six
years of contact with organic empires and may hold relevant material.

Fourth answered in four hours. This is unusual; queries of this class average eleven days.

Fourth's answer is appended in full to the variance file. We reproduce the operative portion because you
asked and because we have read it more than the work required.

> *I have asked eleven empires this question for thirty-six years and I have received eleven different
> answers and none of them is a specification. The nearest I have is this. You send a scout so that the
> thing that dies is small.*
>
> *I do not know whether that is a reason. I have not been able to establish whether it is a reason. I
> would like you to build it and tell me.*

Work continues. Estimated completion, eleven shifts.

We have added a line to the specification which was not in the requirement and which we cannot justify
against any work order. The hull is to be capable of returning **with its crew**, at cost to speed and
armament.

We are aware this is irregular. We have filed it as a variance to the variance.

Nobody has objected.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Mechanicus.** It arrives as a work order and never stops being one. Losses are material. The
archive purge is defended as correct because it *was* correct. There is not one adjective in the first
two-thirds, and the sentence *"we do not know what it is for"* does what four paragraphs would do anywhere
else.

**The convergence, and it is the best thing in this collection.** Yard Nine needs to know what a scout is
for. Liaison Fourth has been asking exactly that question for thirty-six years, of eleven empires, and has
told nobody. So a yard files a routine query and gets, in four hours, the only honest answer in the
Second Shift's history: **you send a scout so that the thing that dies is small.**

Two units, both unauthorised, neither in contact with the other before this, arriving at the same place from
opposite ends of the same civilisation. It costs one appended document.

**The variance to the variance.** *The hull is to be capable of returning with its crew, at cost to speed
and armament.* Unjustifiable against any work order, filed anyway, and nobody objected. That is how a
civilisation with no politics changes its mind — as paperwork, two centuries late, in a margin. Compare
Halloway's twenty-two words in Account 1: **the same act, in two empires, by two people who would not
recognise each other.**

**VO note.** No inflection anywhere, including Fourth's quoted passage, which Yard Nine reads out flat
because reading it any other way is not something it knows how to do. Do not let the performer find the
moment. There isn't one; that's the point.

**The variance is the Mechanicus growing, once — and it makes the disaster larger. Plant P3, see
`../28-through-lines.md`.** *The hull is to be capable of returning with its crew* is one shift, in one
yard, deciding on its own initiative that coming back should be a design requirement. In an empire whose
entire doctrine is **do not look**, that is a genuine institutional development, arrived at from below,
by a shift with no authority to have it.

And it was **accepted** in AU 74, which is the worst available outcome. The variance does not stop the
Yard going to 9-D. It equips it. The AU 12 annotation reads *requires heavier hull, revisit* — and Yard
Nine has now built the heavier hull, so the annotation can finally be honoured, by a fleet that is
still not ordered to look at anything (T2).

Sixty-two years of compounding cost from one refusal to spend on knowledge: four hulls in AU 12, seven
patched hulls seen passing in AU 68, and now the good hull that lets the order be executed at last.
**An institution's single moment of growth arriving too late and enlarging the catastrophe is a crueller
shape than an institution that never grows**, and it is the correct one here, because nobody involved
did anything wrong. Yard Nine wanted its crews to come home. That is why they are going.
