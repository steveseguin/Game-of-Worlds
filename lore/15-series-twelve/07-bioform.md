# Account 7 — Bioform Collective

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Tender Vey of the Long Season |
| **The work** | Raises hull-lines. Leads the Tellers |
| **Recorded** | AU 74 |
| **About** | A ten-year hull, and the berth she will not plant |

---

Eat something first. No — I am not being hospitable, you have been at this for three days and you are
going to sit in my berth-house and eat, and then I will answer you. I have watched your grandmother do
this to herself and I was too young to say anything.

Good.

You want to know about Cassa.

That was the hull. Ten years and four months, out of the Ilmen delta, and I raised her from a thin one — I
put her in the berth myself when I was twenty-nine and I tended her every season, and by year seven she
was the best thing in the eastern fleet and by year ten she was better than that. You do not understand
what that means and I am not going to be able to make you. She was not a good ship. **She was an old
animal, and she was mine, and there is not another one.**

We lost her at 3-D. Sweeping a shoal. Not a battle — a shoal, ordinary transit, the roll went badly, and
she was hit through the forward mass and she did not come out.

Now. You know our faith. Things should be allowed to complete, and death is composting, and there are no
monuments and there are no graves, and I have said all of that to grieving tenders for thirty years and I
believe every word of it.

So we recovered what came back and I composted it. Into a berth. Properly, with the whole delta present,
which is orthodox and is what she would — no. She would not have wanted anything. Ships do not want
funerals, they want water. That is orthodox too.

The berth is at the northern end of the Ilmen shelf. Good flow. Warm. It is, on any measure, the best
unused berth in the eastern delta.

I have not planted it.

Four years. I have had three tenders request it and I have refused all three and I have given each of
them a different reason and none of the reasons was true.

I know what that is. You do not need to be delicate with me; I run a movement about saying things out
loud. **It is a monument.** I have made a monument, in the one faith in the galaxy that forbids them, out of
a hole in the ground with nothing in it, and I have kept it for four years, and I would fight for it.

Ossa knows. She came up the shelf last spring — ninety-three years old, walked it herself — and she stood
at the northern end and looked at the empty berth and she said, *"Ah,"* and then she asked me about the
southern lines and we talked about the southern lines for an hour.

That is what she came for. To not say it. To let me know she had seen it and was not going to say it.

I have never been so angry with anybody in my life.

We are sweeping 3-D again next season. I have four hulls going and one of them is seven years old and I
raised her too.

Write down that the shoal is nearly clear. It is. That part is true and nobody ever puts it in.

## Notes

**Why it's Bioform.** She notices the interviewer's body first and feeds her before answering — and
mentions having watched Ilsa do the same thing to herself, which places three generations of Rells in one
sentence. Agricultural throughout, warm, tactile, and she corrects herself into orthodoxy mid-sentence
(*"she would have wanted — no. Ships do not want funerals, they want water"*) because the faith is genuinely
load-bearing for her.

**The heresy.** The leader of the movement demanding that the Collective say things out loud has kept a
silent monument for four years in a faith that forbids monuments, lied to three colleagues about it, and
will defend it. She identifies it herself, unprompted, without excuse. **That is how you write a reformer:
give them the exact hypocrisy their movement exists to attack, and let them name it first.**

**Ossa's visit is the best scene in either collection and it contains no dialogue worth quoting.** A
ninety-three-year-old walks up a shelf to look at an empty hole, says *"Ah,"* and then talks about
something else for an hour — and it is simultaneously the kindest and cruellest thing available to that
culture, and Vey's response is fury, and both women are right.

**The last two lines are the collection's thesis.** *Write down that the shoal is nearly clear. That part is
true and nobody ever puts it in.* One witness, unprompted, hands Rell the sentence the whole Second File
turns out to be about — and she does it as an afterthought while planning to send another hull she raised
into the same sector.

**VO note.** Warm, unhurried, and no self-pity anywhere. *"I have never been so angry with anybody in my
life"* is said fondly.
