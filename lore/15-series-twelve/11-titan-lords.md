# Account 11 — Titan Lords

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Shipmaster Ordry Vaun, House Vaun |
| **The work** | Master of the *Weight of Consideration*. Reads her house's list each morning |
| **Recorded** | AU 74 |
| **About** | The morning a hull came off her grandfather's list |

---

You will take the chair on the left. It is lower. You will be more comfortable in it than in the one you
were going to choose, and we will be some hours, and I would rather you were seated properly.

You have asked about the service reading and I am going to answer it correctly, which means I am going to
begin further back than you would like. I am told this is a family failing.

Every morning, every house on Ordel Deep reads its own list. Which of our hulls are in the water, where
they are, and how long they have been. It takes me eleven minutes. It took my grandfather nineteen. It is
dull, it is administrative, and it is the nearest thing this world has to prayer, and I have not missed one
in twenty-two years.

My grandfather died in sixty-nine. Shipmaster Vaun, who you have read, who took the *Weight of
Consideration* into the Ordel–Vail lane on the second day of the nine days and brought her out the far
end, and reported what he saw, in the correct form, to three authorities, and was told it was instrument
error.

His list had thirty-one hulls in it. That is what he left. Not a fortune, not an estate — a list, thirty-one
entries long, and every year that those hulls stay in the water is another year of him, and the reading gets
better as the centuries pass, and that is what we have instead of a grave.

Last year it went to thirty.

The *Forbearance of Kell*, four hundred and six years in service, structural failure at the keel step. No
loss of life. She was towed, assessed, and broken up in the autumn.

I removed her from the list on a Tuesday morning, aloud, by name, in eleven minutes, and then I went to
work.

He is smaller this year than he was last year.

That is not a metaphor and I would ask you not to file it as one. It is the arithmetic of our faith working
correctly. A man is the works that are still running; the works are finite; every one of them will fail
eventually, and in something between four hundred and two thousand years the last of my grandfather will be
towed somewhere and assessed and broken up, and there will be a morning when a Vaun reads a list with
nothing of his in it. That is the design. We are not troubled by it. We have simply arranged to be
diminished slowly rather than all at once, and I would put it to you that you have arranged the same thing
with rather less honesty.

The *Weight of Consideration* is seventy-six years in service and I expect her to make two hundred. She is
the last entry on his list that anybody outside this house has heard of, and she is the one carrying the
log.

Which brings us to your Registry, and I see that you know it does.

Two apologies. Both tendered by Registrar Vance, both in writing, both accepted formally by this house, and
I do not question her sincerity and I never have.

Section four of the incident file still reads *instrument error*.

I am not asking for an apology, Warden. I have two. I am asking for an amendment.

Now. That is the reading, which was your question. You have another one and you have been holding it for
half an hour, and we have hours, and there is food coming. Ask it.

---

## Notes

**Why it's a Titan.** Long formal sentences, no contractions anywhere, opens by rearranging the
interviewer's seating, and announces that beginning too far back is a family failing — which is Shipmaster
Vaun's opening move in Series 9, inherited. Business is not discussed early; food is coming; hurry is
vulgar.

**The rite paying off.** `../14-peoples/11-titan-lords.md` establishes that the Titan funeral is the only one
in the galaxy that *improves with time* — a house re-reads its founder's list every decade to add the years.
This is the other side of it, and nobody had thought about the other side: **the list can go down.**

*"He is smaller this year than he was last year."* And she explicitly forbids reading it as a
metaphor — it is the arithmetic of the faith working correctly, and she is not troubled, and she has
arranged to be diminished slowly rather than all at once, and she thinks the rest of us have arranged the
same thing less honestly.

**The ask.** Two Registrars' apologies, both sincere, both accepted, and the file is unchanged. *"I am not
asking for an apology, Warden. I have two. I am asking for an amendment."* That is the entire Terran–Titan
relationship in nineteen words, and it is a request only an archivist could grant, made to one, by
somebody who knows exactly what she is doing.

**Pair with Account 1.** Deputy Warden Halloway ended her career writing twenty-two words in a margin
because section four had no code for what happened. Ordry Vaun wants section four of a different file
amended. **Two accounts, one coded field, and the whole of Terran bureaucracy caught between them.**

**VO note.** Slow, patrician, and genuinely warm — she is being hospitable, not grand. The last line is an
invitation and it should land as generosity: she has known for half an hour that Rell wants something else,
and she has cleared the afternoon.

**Art beat.** The list. Thirty entries in a bound ledger, one struck through in a single clean line, the
years beside each one written in a different hand every decade.
