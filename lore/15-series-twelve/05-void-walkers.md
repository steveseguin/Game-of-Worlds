# Account 5 — Void Walkers

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Courier Sten |
| **The work** | Three lanes. Twenty-six years old |
| **Recorded** | AU 74 |
| **About** | The lane she was given at a funeral. She flew it as recited. It had changed |

---

Kettering to the Vail approaches, ninety minutes. That is what I was given. That is what she said, and she
said it in order, and she was four days dying and she got to the end.

Her name was Marn. She had nine lanes and I got three of them and two other couriers got the rest, and I
had the tally marks put on that evening, and I have never been prouder of anything and I am not going to
pretend otherwise just because of what comes next.

I flew it in seventy-two. Six hulls behind me.

I flew it exactly. I want that on your paper first, before anything, because I have had this conversation in
three rooms on Bell and every time somebody has looked for the place where I got it wrong. There is no
place. I recited it that morning like I recite it every morning, and I flew what I recited, entry to exit,
and the plot was hers and the plot was good.

Four hulls did not arrive.

The shoal at the second waypoint had moved. Not far. Marn flew that lane the last time in AU 59 and she flew
it clean and she was right, and thirteen years is thirteen years and gravel drifts, and I came through the
gap she gave me and the gap was not there any more.

Now. You are Terran, so you will want to know what the Guild did about it.

Nothing. There is nothing to do. There is no procedure, because a recitation cannot be *amended* — a lane is
what the dying said it was, and if I change it, whose lane is it? Mine? Marn gave me hers. She did not give
me permission to make a different one and call it hers, and she is not available to ask, and that is not a
gap in our law, that is our law working.

So I have three lanes and one of them kills people, and I recite it every morning, in order, entire,
including the second waypoint, exactly as she said it.

I have not flown it since.

I want you to write that down carefully because I have not said it out loud on Bell. I have not flown it and
I am not going to fly it, and I have not offered it to anyone, and I am twenty-six years old and when I die
it will die with me. Unrecited. Unwritten.

Which is the thing they strip Pell for. Same outcome. Different door.

Ash knows. Ash has never said anything to me about it, which is either kindness or arithmetic and I do not
need to know which.

Ninety minutes, Kettering to the Vail approaches. It was a very fine lane.

I have to go — I am carrying at the sixth hour and you have had longer than I said you could have.

---

## Notes

**Why it's a Void Walker.** Transit time in the first line and the last. Clipped, defensive before she is
accused, and she gives the operational facts before she gives the emotional ones and then denies the
emotional ones are relevant. And she leaves — she ends the interview herself, because she is carrying at the
sixth hour, which is exactly how Wren behaved twenty-two years earlier.

**The trap the whole race is in, in one account.** A recitation cannot be amended, and the reason is
*sound* — a lane belongs to whoever paid for it, and the dead cannot consent to a revision. So Sten's only
options are to fly a lane she knows is wrong, or to sit on it until it dies with her. She has chosen the
second, which produces the identical outcome to the Scrivener heresy the Guild strips people for.

*"Same outcome. Different door."* Five words, and it is the strongest argument against her own faith anybody
in the Guild has ever made, and she does not know she has made it.

**Read alongside Series 9's Wren.** Wren withheld the most valuable observation in history on professional
principle and died with it. Sten is twenty-six, is doing the same thing to a lane that kills people, and is
ashamed of it. **Two generations, one law, and the younger one can feel it.**

**And it pays off the Ash characterisation.** Ash privately agrees with the Scriveners and has calculated that
saying so would split the Guild, so she says nothing to Sten. *Either kindness or arithmetic and I do not
need to know which.*

**VO note.** Fast, faster than anyone else in this collection, no pauses. The only slow line is *"it was a
very fine lane."*

**Pair with Account 1, and it is the sharpest pairing in either book — see `../28-through-lines.md` T8.**
Deputy Warden Halloway lost eleven hulls on a **certified corridor** that had been safe for eight years
and forty-one transits. Sten flies a **recited lane** that Marn last flew in AU 59, fifteen years ago.

Terra writes everything down. The Void Walkers write nothing down and recite instead. **Both doctrines
fail identically, for the identical reason** — Law 10, a trace degrades — and each empire is privately
certain the carelessness belongs to the other. Halloway has no *code* for a chart that was right when
filed. Sten has no *procedure* for a recitation that cannot be amended. Two people, two opposite
civilisations, the same fifteen-or-forty-one crossings of borrowed confidence, and they will never meet.

The answer to both is Law 6 and neither doctrine can reach it: a shoal you **hold** is safe forever,
because knowledge about ground you stand on cannot go stale. The player solves Halloway's problem and
Sten's problem all game, one sector at a time, and must never be told that is what they are doing.

**Forward plant, and do not resolve it here (P2).** Sten has three lanes, one of them kills, and if she
dies they pass on. The payoff belongs to a later pass: **Sten refuses to recite the third lane** — the
first Void Walker to withhold a bequest — which is Wren's act twenty-two years later and in the
opposite direction. Wren broke doctrine to save herself; Sten breaks it for a courier she will never
meet. And it costs her the thing that makes it hard: the only way to stop Marn's lane killing people is
to let that part of Marn end.
