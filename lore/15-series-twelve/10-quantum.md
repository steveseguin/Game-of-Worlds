# Account 10 — Quantum Entities

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Ysse of the Not-Yet |
| **The work** | Survey. Eleven years in the deep margin |
| **Recorded** | AU 74 |
| **About** | What she chose to be certain about, at majority, and what happened to it |

---

We would be glad to. You have asked about the commitment and almost nobody does; they ask about the
physics.

At majority we choose one thing to be certain about. One. It is declared, it is resolved permanently, and
it may never be changed or added to, and most of us choose a person, and everybody warns you not to and
everybody does it anyway.

We chose Aven.

That is not a difficult thing to explain and we are going to explain it badly because we have not tried
before. We were nineteen. We had one certainty available to us for the whole of our existence, and we
looked at what was on offer — a proposition, a principle, a number, several very good numbers — and we
looked at Aven, and it was not a decision. It resolved.

Aven died in sixty-eight. A shoal, on survey, in the deep margin. Ordinary.

Now.

Our people do not commemorate. You know this. There are no monuments and no funerals and no lists, because
a dead person is one outcome among several and to carve a name is to insist on the worst version. We hold
that this is true, and we hold that it is kind, and we have watched your Reading of the Names and we
understand that you would call it the opposite of kind, and we are not going to argue with you about it
today.

But we committed.

We are certain about Aven. Permanently, by our own declaration, made at nineteen, unamendable. Which means
that when everyone we know says *Aven may be*, we cannot. **We are the only one of us who has to hold a
dead person as a resolved state**, and the whole comfort of our civilisation — the one thing our people have
that yours does not — is unavailable to us, and it is unavailable because we chose it to be, six years
before there was anything to be unavailable about.

They ask us to release it. Gently. Constantly. Our mother asks. It is meant kindly and it is the kindest
thing on offer and there is no mechanism; the rite says never. We told her that and she said *"we know,"*
and she asks anyway, every season, because she would rather ask than watch.

You are going to want to know whether we would choose differently.

No.

We are asked that a great deal and the answer is boring. If we had chosen a number we would have a number.
We would be comfortable. We would be able to say *Aven may be*, along with everybody, and there would be
nothing in the world that we were certain about.

We are going back out to the deep margin in the spring. Eleven years now. It is good work and somebody has
to chart it, and we are unusually well suited to a place nobody has plotted, because we do not require it
to have resolved before we go.

You may write that Aven is dead. We would prefer you did. Somebody should have it written down.

## Notes

**Why it's Quantum.** Conditional and subjunctive throughout, plural first person, and short sentences —
per the roster note, or it becomes unreadable. And she opens by thanking Rell for asking the *right*
question, which places the interviewer without a word from her.

**This is the payoff for the rite invented in `../14-peoples/10-quantum.md`.** The most moving ceremony in
the galaxy — one certainty, chosen young, never amendable, and most choose a person — turned on its owner.
Ysse is the only Not-Yet in existence who is obliged to hold a dead person as resolved, and the entire
consolation of her civilisation is closed to her *by her own hand*, six years before there was anything to
be consoled about.

**The mother.** *"We know," and she asks anyway, every season, because she would rather ask than watch.*
Eleven words of a second character who never appears.

**And the last two lines are the reason this account exists.** A people who refuse to write the dead down,
and one of them asking a Terran archivist to do it. *You may write that Aven is dead. We would prefer you
did. Somebody should have it written down.*

Read it against the AU 22 filing — the Not-Yet's formal comment calling the Reading of the Names *"a
civilisation gathering annually to eliminate its own possibilities."* Ysse is not withdrawing that. She
agrees with it. She has simply arrived, alone, at the one case where the Terran instinct is the only thing
that helps, and she has asked for it once, quietly, in a cabinet.

**VO note.** Brisk and warm. Not sad. She has had six years and she is not performing any of it. *"No"* is
its own line and must be flat. The final three sentences are asked as a small favour, not as a plea.
