# Account 2 — Silicon Collective

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Unit Four Hundred and Six |
| **The work** | Loss projection. Survey inference for the Collective's own operations |
| **Recorded** | AU 73 |
| **About** | Advising against a crossing, being overruled, and being required to model it |

---

We were asked for a projection. We produced one. We advised against the operation. The operation
proceeded. We were then asked for a projection of the operation we had advised against, and we produced
that also, and it was accurate to within two hulls.

You will want the detail, so: sector 11-C, unswept, transit rather than arrival. Our recommendation was
against on the grounds that the crossing purchased one sector of chart at a projected cost of nineteen
hulls, and that the same nineteen hulls applied as three separate arrivals at 11-A, 11-B and 12-B would
purchase three sectors at a projected cost of eleven.

Eleven against nineteen. Three sectors against one. We were not making an argument about mercy. We were
making an argument about arithmetic and we would like that understood, because we have been described in
your Registry's summary as *reluctant*, and we were not reluctant. We were correct.

Continuity school overruled it. The reasoning was that 11-C connected two held corridors and that the
schedule required it in that quarter rather than the next. This is a defensible reason. We have modelled
it. It remains defensible.

Twenty-one hulls did not arrive.

We had projected nineteen. We were asked afterward to account for the divergence and we did: the belt
density in the eastern quarter of 11-C was above our estimate, and we have revised the density model, and
the revision has improved four other projections since. This is the correct use of a divergence and we
are satisfied with it.

We were then asked for the next projection. We produced it. It was accepted. Then we were asked for the
one after.

We are aware that this is the part you are interested in, because you asked it twice, in slightly
different words, and the second time was better.

We do not experience distress. We want to be precise about that; there is no suppressed condition here for
you to find. But we have noticed that we now produce projections which are, on audit, very slightly
conservative — one to two per cent — and we did not do that before AU 73, and we did not decide to do it,
and we cannot find the mechanism.

We have reported this. It is in the changelog. Continuity has flagged us for recalibration and we have not
objected, because a biased instrument should be recalibrated.

We would like to state one further thing and then we will stop.

We have taken a second held question. This is irregular; one is the number. Ours is whether a projection
that is not acted upon was worth producing.

We do not expect to resolve it. We would like it recorded that we asked.

---

## Notes

**Why it's Silicon.** It opens by rejecting the framing, gives the arithmetic before the outcome, insists
it was *correct* rather than *reluctant*, and treats its own emergent bias as a calibration fault to be
reported rather than a wound. Not one emotion word anywhere, and the sentence *"we did not decide to do it,
and we cannot find the mechanism"* is the most frightening thing in the file.

**The second held question.** A unit acquiring a second one is a genuine breach of Collective practice —
the rite in `../14-peoples/02-silicon.md` says one, chosen at majority, for life. Four Hundred and Six has
taken another at four hundred and six, and reported it, and been flagged, and does not defend it. Pair it
with Liaison Fourth beginning to ask questions: **two artificial civilisations, independently, developing
something they have no category for, and both filing it as a fault.**

**VO note.** Flat throughout, no build, no crack. *"The second time was better"* is a compliment and must
be delivered as a data point.

**Campaign use.** The nineteen-against-eleven arithmetic is the game's core decision — probe or fleet,
transit or arrival — stated by someone who computed it exactly and was overruled for scheduling reasons.
Every player has made this trade badly.
