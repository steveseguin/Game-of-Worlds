# Account 8 — Star Nomads

| | |
|---|---|
| **Witness** | Berth-Keeper Onn, Kettering |
| **The work** | Keeps the empty berths. Conducts the rite |
| **Recorded** | AU 72 |
| **About** | The salted probe, and the position she read aloud |

---

Sit anywhere. Not there — that one's assigned.

I keep the berths. Eleven thousand of them, assigned in AU 0 to hulls that did not arrive and never
reassigned, swept and lit and maintained, and it is the largest line in the Fleet's accounts and no council
in seventy-four years has queried it. I have had this post nineteen years. I know which berth is which
without looking.

So when a thing needs burying, they bring it to me.

Your probe. Yes. Terran hull, Registry mark, one of yours — a survey probe out of Vail Gate in AU 71,
three hundred crystal, and it went into a shoal at 8-F at full closing speed and there was not much of it.

It had been salted. You know that; that is why you are here. Shadow work, and very good work, and I will
say for them that it was clean: a false trace, well built, internally consistent, filed where your people
would find it and trust it. The probe flew what it was told. It did exactly what it was built to do, all
the way in, at speed, believing.

We recovered eleven kilograms.

Now you want the rite and I will tell you the rite, because eleven empires think we do this to be
picturesque.

We took it to berth four thousand and nine. Empty since AU 0. About thirty of us — anybody in port who
wanted, and there is always more than you would think. We put the eleven kilograms down.

And then I read out its last recorded position.

That is the ceremony. That is all of it. **I read the position the probe believed it was in, out loud, to
thirty people who all knew it was wrong.**

It thought it was at 8-E, on a clean corridor, four minutes from arrival. That is what its own log said. It
was somewhere else entirely and it was already gone by the time it recorded that, and I stood in an empty
berth and read out a wrong position in a formal voice, slowly, twice.

You are Terran, so: yes, I know it was a machine. I know it did not experience anything. Nobody here is
confused about that.

We do not do it for the probe.

In AU 0 my great-grandmother's convoy had a schedule. Printed, certified, thirty ports, times to the
quarter hour. She believed it. Everyone she knew believed it, because it had been true for nine hundred
years, and they flew it, all of them, on faith, into something that had already changed.

The Fleet's entire dead were given a false position and flew it.

So when somebody does that on purpose, to anything, we bury it and we read the wrong position out loud in
front of witnesses. That is the whole of it. It is not sentiment. It is the only thing in the galaxy we
have ever refused to do to anybody, and we have lost sectors over the refusal, and we will lose more.

Berth four thousand and nine still has eleven kilograms of your probe in it, and it is still assigned, and
it will not be reassigned.

Your grandmother came here in fifty-nine. Different berth. She read one out with me.

## Notes

**Why it's a Nomad.** Warm, direct, mercantile in structure — she gives you the operational facts, then the
ceremony, then the price of it — and she assumes the Terran objection and answers it before it is raised.
And she cannot resist the last line, which is a gift and also a small, precise act of leverage.

**Series 9 finally paid off.** The salted-probe rite has been referenced four times across the folder as
*"the only people who do this."* This is the first time it is shown, and the reveal is that the ceremony is
**one sentence read aloud**: the position the probe believed it was in, spoken slowly, twice, to thirty
people who know it is wrong.

Nothing about it is picturesque. It is the cheapest possible ritual and it is unbearable.

**The reason underneath is the Fleet entire.** Their dead were given a false schedule and flew it on faith.
So a machine that was lied to is buried by the only people in the galaxy who recognise the specific manner
of its death — which is also why the Fleet will not salt, has never salted, and loses ground over it.

**Ilsa was here.** *She read one out with me.* One line, and it means Ilsa Rell spent forty years collecting
accounts of the nine days and at least once stood in an empty berth on Kettering and performed somebody
else's rite for somebody else's machine. Rell will have read that sentence in their own transcript.

**VO note.** Steady, practical, unhurried. The read-out itself — *8-E, clean corridor, four minutes from
arrival* — should be delivered flatly, as a quotation, and it will do the work on its own.

**Art beat.** Berth four thousand and nine: an enormous empty docking cradle, swept and lit for
seventy-four years, and eleven kilograms of wreckage on the floor of it.
