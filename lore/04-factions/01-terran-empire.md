## Doctrine

The Terran position is that the galaxy's problem is not danger but **forgetting**. Every shoal
that kills a fleet was known to somebody once. Every mouth on the chart was flown around
routinely for eight hundred years. The knowledge existed and the knowledge was lost, and Terra's
entire strategic doctrine is the belief that the fastest way out of the dark is to stop losing
things.

So they file. Obsessively, and at real cost. A Terran survey fleet carries redundant record
crews, transmits partial charts home before every crossing, and has standing orders to abort an
advance rather than risk the loss of an unduplicated trace. Other empires find this
absurd — the Void Walkers consider it cowardice and the Zephyr Swarm cannot parse it at all —
and the Terrans keep doing it, because they are the only power whose expansion is *cumulative*.
Nobody else's knowledge outlives their veterans.

The mechanical expression is total flexibility: no capped branch, no forbidden hull, every
bonus exactly 1.0. Not because Terrans are gifted, but because **somebody wrote down how to
build all of it**, and they have the document.

## History

**Under the Concord.** Terra was the largest junction on the Trellis — not the richest world or
the strongest power, but the one the most lanes ran through. That made it the galaxy's clearing
house: standards bureau, insurance market, arbitration court, and above all the **Registry**,
the shared archive of measures, ship plans, casualty rolls and verified traces that twelve
species found it easier to maintain in one place than twelve.

Terra grew fat on being useful. It also grew a civil service of frankly monstrous
sophistication, which turned out to be the thing that saved it.

**The Unarriving.** Terra lost proportionally fewer people than anyone except the Titan Lords,
for the unheroic reason that it had eleven months of everything sitting in orbital warehouses
when the schedule stopped. Terran popular history treats this as providence. Terran naval
history is blunter, and the phrase used in the academies — *we were lucky and we were fat* — is
taught deliberately, as an inoculation against pride.

**The Shortening.** Terra's great moment, and it is a bureaucratic one. As the interdependent
economies unwound, the Registry kept publishing: casualty figures, surviving traces, standards
for the new blind-transit hulls, allocation schedules for empires that had lost their own
records. It had no authority to do any of this. It did it anyway, for thirty years, and the
result is that Terran-standard is the galaxy's standard and the Terran figure is the galactic
figure, and eleven other powers resent it in eleven different ways.

**Now.** Terra is the largest single power and not the strongest. Its fleet is competent and
unremarkable. Its real weapon is that when a Terran commander plots a route, she is drawing on
seventy-four years of accumulated survivals by people she never met — and when a Void Walker
plots the same route, she is drawing on her own memory, which is better, and which will die
with her.

## The refusal, and what it costs

**Terra refuses to specialise**, and the refusal is not humility. It is a policy, enforced by
the Registry, and it has produced a fleet that is never the best thing on the battlefield and
never the wrong thing either.

The cost is real and should be dramatised: Terra loses every straight fight it picks. A Terran
battle line meeting Titan Lords loses. Meeting Void Walkers it is outrun; meeting Mechanicus it
is outlasted; meeting the Swarm it is simply outnumbered. Terra wins wars by **arriving in the
right sector with an adequate fleet**, which requires knowing the map better than the enemy,
which is the one thing it does better than anyone.

The internal argument this generates is the Terran story engine: *the archive is why we are
still here* versus *the archive is why we are slow.* Both sides are correct.

## Government and cast

Terra is governed by **the Registrar** — formally the chief archivist, effectively the head of
state. The office was never meant to rule. It acquired power during the Shortening because it
was the only institution still functioning, and nobody has quite got round to taking it back.

**Registrar Vance** *(seed character)* — nineteen years in office, the longest tenure since the
Unarriving. Believes, correctly, that the Registry is the only thing keeping twelve empires
speaking a common language. Is privately terrified by something she found in the commercial
correspondence files and has told no one. Speaks like a woman who has read every casualty roll
personally, because she has.

**Sweep-Admiral Ito** *(seed character)* — commands expansion. Thinks the Registry is a
liability and says so in open session. Wants to trade traces to the Nomads for reach, which
Vance considers close to treason. Not a villain — the best argument for a bad idea in the game.

**Chart-Warden Rell** — the player's adjutant. See `05-characters.md`. The most important
character in the project.

## Voice

Institutional passive. File references. Scrupulous, slightly exhausting fairness. Terrans
report bad news accurately and without softening, and they always name the cost.

**Rules:**
- Passive constructions where another race would use "I".
- Always cite the source or the file. Terrans do not assert; they *reference*.
- Losses are stated as numbers and followed by a procedural consequence.
- No boasting, ever. A Terran victory report reads like an inventory.

**Samples across five registers:**

*Event feed —*
> Sector 6-B logged. Trace provisional pending a second crossing. Four hulls did not arrive;
> next of kin notified per standing order.

*Briefing —*
> The Registry has three traces into this cluster and two of them were filed by the same
> captain, who is dead, so treat them as one. We will pay for the third ourselves.

*Registrar Vance, in council —*
> I have the figure. I have always had the figure. What I do not have is a reason to believe
> it, and I would rather publish a number I distrust than let eleven empires invent twelve.

*Sweep-Admiral Ito, arguing —*
> With respect to the Registrar: the Nomads will sell us four hundred years of route-knowledge
> for a copy of a document we are not using. We are the richest hoarders in the galaxy and we
> are expanding at the speed of a filing cabinet.

*A junior officer, off duty —*
> Everyone else got a talent. We got the paperwork. Do you know what I have noticed? We are
> still here.

## How they fight

- **The whole roster, and no favourites.** All nine hull classes, every branch to maximum. A
  Terran fleet is whatever the situation called for, which makes them the hardest empire to
  scout and the easiest to match once scouted.
- **Combined arms is the doctrine, not a preference.** Terran tactical writing treats an
  unbalanced fleet as an error regardless of results.
- **They fight on charted ground on purpose.** The Terran way to win a battle is to have swept
  the sector two seasons earlier. Their operational art is logistics and foreknowledge, and a
  Terran caught in an unswept sector is a Terran who has already been out-generalled.
- **They are the best at holding.** Orbital Engineering unlocks turrets and Warp Gates, and
  Terra builds both more than anyone, because a fortified charted sector is the physical form of
  the whole doctrine.

## Art notes *(for the Pass 6 art bible)*

The command-station art direction in `docs/art-direction/` is essentially a Terran interior, so
this race sets the visual baseline everything else deviates from.

- **Palette:** olive, gun-steel, oxidised brass, warning amber. Beveled metal, per the
  established visual language.
- **Material:** stencilled serial numbers on everything, including things that don't need them.
  Panels are labelled. Labels are labelled.
- **The Terran motif is paper.** They are the only power in the galaxy with physical documents,
  and a Terran command deck should have clipboards, filed hardcopy, a wall of index drawers
  behind the officer's shoulder. In a genre where every bridge is a hologram, a clipboard is
  the most distinctive object we could put on screen — and it says the entire faction in one
  prop.
- **Silhouette:** flat-sided, boxy, functional. Terran hulls look like they were designed by a
  committee to be repairable by strangers, because they were. Nothing is streamlined. Nothing
  is beautiful. Everything has a handle.
- **Insignia:** a filing index tab. Not a bird, not a sword, not a star. A tab.

## Three story hooks

1. **The four-sentence letter.** In the Registry's commercial correspondence, filed under
   routine shipping enquiries, is the Ancients' request from BU 8 that all empires reduce
   transit volume. Vance has read it. It explains nothing and it is dated. Whoever proves what
   it means owns the galaxy's politics — and the file copy is the only one, which makes the
   Terran archive a military target for the first time in its history.
2. **Ito's trade.** The Nomads will sell four centuries of route-knowledge for archive access.
   The deal is good. The deal also ends Terran primacy, because the Registry's power is that
   it is *the only copy*. A campaign mission where the player decides this is worth building.
3. **The count is wrong.** Somebody in the Registry has been quietly correcting the Unarriving
   casualty figure downward for sixty years — not to deceive, but because each revision was
   defensible. Cumulatively it is a lie of some millions, and the Nomads have always known.
