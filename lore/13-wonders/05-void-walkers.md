# Void Walkers — The Whole Recitation

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

---

## The Wonder: **The Whole Recitation**

The Void Walker Wonder is their apostasy.

They write it down. All of it — every lane, every crossing, every lit-era route held in the memory of
every surviving courier — into one place, in one archive, on Bell. Four hundred years of route knowledge
that has never existed outside a living head, committed to a record for the first time in the history of
the people whose entire religion holds that **what is written can be taken, and what is taken is no
longer yours to give.**

It is not built by engineers. It is built by the dying. The Recitation is assembled from funeral
recitations — old couriers speaking their lanes to a scribe instead of a successor — and every entry in
it is somebody's last act and somebody's broken vow. The Scriveners have wanted this for forty years and
been called heretics for it, and the Wonder is the moment they win, and the Guild's response is grief
rather than celebration.

**What it does [P].** On completion, you gain **every trace on the map** — including lanes nobody has
flown since AU 0, routes to sectors you have never visited, and the shortest safe crossing between any
two points either you or the lit era ever charted. Movement costs collapse. You know the road network of
a galaxy that no longer exists, and most of it still works.

**Maintenance [P].** It must be **recited**. A continuous drain on couriers — represented as an ongoing
crystal cost and a standing requirement to keep fast hulls in service — because an unspoken route decays,
and the Recitation decays exactly the way a courier's memory does. Stop reciting and entries go stale,
and a stale trace is a rumour with paperwork.

**How it dies — and this is the important part.** The Whole Recitation is the **only Wonder that can be
stolen.**

It is a document. An enemy who takes the archive sector does not destroy it; they **copy it**, and they
gain every trace it holds, and you keep yours. There is no version of losing it that is not also arming
somebody. Espionage against a completed Recitation is the single highest-value intelligence operation in
the game, and the Shadow Realm exists.

Wren was right. That is the joke and it is not a kind one.

**The danger.** Four hundred years of lit-era routes includes lanes to places nobody has been since AU 0,
and some of those lanes were closed for a reason. The Recitation does not know which. It is a complete
map with no annotations, and it will confidently route a fleet somewhere the Ancients spent everything to
make unreachable.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Deceleration Doctrine** *(Propulsion)*
Codifies what Courier Wren did and her doctrine forbade: your **arrival** hazard rolls improve
substantially. Per Law 2 this buys seconds, not minutes, and it does nothing for transit rolls — crossing
a shoal is still a coin toss, because at that speed nothing helps. It is the only technology in the game
that makes stopping somewhere safer than passing through it, and it is named after a woman who was
disgraced for surviving.
> *Everything I was taught says do not decelerate. I stood on it.*

**2 · Inherited Lanes** *(Propulsion / Intel)*
You begin the game already holding several traces at random across the map, and gain more as your
couriers age. Memory from the lit era, arriving unbidden. Nobody else starts a game knowing anything.
> *I have not flown it since the Lamps, so now it is a guess. I do not deal in guesses.*

**3 · The Whole Recitation** *(Propulsion — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
The Scrivener formalism: a notation capable of recording a route completely enough to be flown by someone
who did not learn it. Deriving it took eleven years. Agreeing to use it took forty.
> *Forty-one petitions. The forty-second was granted by people attending a funeral.*

## Signature hulls

**Bell–Anselm** — Named for the longest lane in the galaxy. The fastest hull in the game by a clear
margin and it carries **no weapons at all**. Pure courier: it exists to arrive, and it is the only hull
that can outrun a pursuit reliably. Void Walker hulls are named for routes they no longer fly, and this
one is named for the route nobody flies.

**Vail Forty** — A convoy lead that **grants its speed to the fleet it moves with**, at the cost of its
own armament. It is why a Void Walker battle group moves like a courier, and it is also why killing the
Vail Forty strands everything behind it at ordinary pace.

**Ordel Short** — Carries the manoeuvre. An Ordel Short may **abort a crossing mid-transit and return to
its origin** for a heavy crystal cost, once per movement. Note carefully what this does not do: the hull
still takes any transit roll for sectors it has already entered. You cannot un-cross a shoal. You can
only decline to finish.

## Notes

**Access check.** `ARMOR: 2` and `WEAPONS: 3` hold — two of the three signature hulls are *unarmed or
lightly armed*, which is the opposite of an uplift. No Dreadnought appears here and none ever will;
there is no Void Walker word for a hull that slow that is not an insult. Per Law 22 there is no
prescience anywhere in this file: Deceleration Doctrine buys seconds of *sight on approach*, exactly as
the physics allows, and nothing more.

**Why the theft clause must survive balancing.** A Wonder that arms your enemy when they take it is the
only mechanical expression of the Void Walkers' entire tragedy, and it will be the first thing a
balance pass tries to remove. Do not let it.

**Campaign.** The Recitation is the best possible payoff for Wren's testimony: she refused Ilsa Rell
three times on professional principle, died in AU 71 with the Anselm lanes, and forty years of
Scrivener petitions later her people build exactly the thing she called theft — and it is the only
reason anyone can still find the lane she would not describe.
