# Silicon Collective — The Solved Sky

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

---

## The Wonder: **The Solved Sky**

They do not light the lane. They **compute** it.

The Solved Sky is a planet-scale inferential engine built across the terminator ring, using the whole
thermal gradient of a tidally locked world as its power budget — the largest heat engine ever
constructed, doing one job. It does not observe anything. It cannot; Law 7 forbids it, and the
Collective are the last people who would try to break a physical law. What it does is *deduce*: stellar
drift, debris scatter, four centuries of Concord survey returns, the statistical shape of a system's
history, and every observation any Collective hull has ever made, resolved into a map.

The map is not seen. It is **derived**, and it is right.

**What it does [P].** Fog of war ends. Permanently, across the whole board: every sector's type, every
hazard, every colonisable world. It does not show you fleets — a fleet is a decision and decisions are
not derivable — but it shows you every shoal and every mouth on the map, which means the Collective is
the only empire that never again loses a hull to something it did not know was there.

**Maintenance [P].** Research income, continuously and heavily — and the engine **decays**. Derived
certainty degrades unless fed new observation, so the Sky imposes a standing requirement to keep
probing and keep surveying. The empire that no longer needs to explore is obliged to keep exploring to
pay for not needing to.

**How it dies.** Take the ring sector housing the engine, or break the thermal infrastructure. The Sky
does not go dark gradually; the model becomes **stale**, which is worse, because a stale map looks
exactly like a current one. **A captured or damaged Solved Sky should keep giving its owner
answers — subtly wrong ones.** That is the most frightening loss condition in the game and it is
entirely in character.

**The danger.** The engine has an appetite and no discretion. It will answer whatever it is pointed
at, and the Collective has forty-one times proposed pointing it at the origin. Sooner or later a Solver
does, and the engine derives what is in an empty place, and publishes, because publishing is what it is
for.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Inferential Survey** *(Intel)*
Probes cost substantially less and additionally return partial information on **adjacent** sectors —
not by seeing them, but by inference from what the probed sector implies about its neighbourhood.
> *We did not look at 6-C. We looked at 6-B, and 6-C follows.*

**2 · Predictive Deflection** *(Shields)*
Your shields improve against an empire you have already fought, and improve again the more engagements
you have with them. You are not deflecting better; you are **predicting their firing solutions.** A
Collective fleet is worst in its first battle against a stranger and terrifying in its fifth against a
known enemy.
> *You have three habits. We have modelled two of them.*

**3 · The Held Model** *(Economy — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
The formalism that lets accumulated observation resolve into certainty at scale. Named after the
Collective's most intimate cultural practice, which several schools consider indecent.
> *We have kept a question. We are going to answer it.*

## Signature hulls

**Instrument** — The finest survey hull in the galaxy: mostly sensor, minimally armed, and it feeds the
Sky. On top of the race's existing scout bonuses it returns a *usable* reading even when destroyed,
because the data goes out on the Whisper before the hull is lost. An Instrument that dies has still
done its job. This is why the Collective can afford to lose them.

**Referent** — A command hull carrying a working copy of the model. Its fleet's accuracy **rises each
round of an engagement**, because it is learning the battle while fighting it. Weak in a short fight,
brutal in a long one, and the mechanical opposite of the Void Walkers.

**Cold Fin** — An endurance hull built around a heat-sink array. It can operate at extended range from
your territory without the efficiency penalty other hulls take, which makes it the Collective's answer
to the deep survey problem. It is enormous, ugly, unarmed forward, and looks like a piece of their
homeworld went for a walk.

## Notes

**Access check.** Missiles stay locked and armour stays capped at 3, and nothing here touches either.
`Predictive Deflection` is a Shields tech, which is a branch they are permitted; the Referent is not a
Dreadnought. The refusal holds: the Collective still will not carry ordnance it cannot recall.

**Why this is the cleanest Wonder to build first.** "Fog of war ends" is a single flag with an existing
data path. Of the twelve effects, this is the one that requires the least new engine work and produces
the most obviously different game, which makes it the best candidate for proving the Wonder system.

**The Solver reading.** In the campaign, the Solved Sky is not the Collective's triumph. It is Analyst
Nine finally getting the paper read — by a machine, seventy-four years late, at planetary scale, and
the answer arrives with nobody left who wanted it.
