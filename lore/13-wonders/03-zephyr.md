# Zephyr Swarm — The Ten Thousand Mouths

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

---

## The Wonder: **The Ten Thousand Mouths**

Every other Wonder is an attempt to end the dark. This one is not.

The Ten Thousand Mouths is a hatchery. A single nest, grown across an entire tectonic basin, doing
nothing but producing hulls — and it produces them at a rate that makes the dark **irrelevant** rather
than solved. The Swarm's answer to a galaxy that eats ships is not to stop losing ships. It is to
arrive at a rate the galaxy cannot keep up with.

It is the only Wonder that does not try to relight anything, and it is the only one whose builders do
not think of it as remarkable. There is no ceremony at its completion. It is a large trough.

**What it does [P].** A large, permanent, free output of light hulls every turn, delivered to the
Wonder's sector, forever, at no metal cost. Not a discount — *free hulls*, continuously, and enough of
them that the Swarm's arithmetic finally works: throw enough at a shoal and the survivors hold it, throw
enough at a fleet and the survivors win.

**Maintenance [P].** Metal, at a rate that consumes most of a normal empire's income — and, critically,
**it must keep being fed by expansion.** The Mouths draw down the basin's accessible ore, so the upkeep
requirement *rises* over time unless you take more territory. It is a Wonder that makes you unable to
stop.

**How it dies.** Take the basin. It is not defensible in any conventional sense — the Swarm cannot build
capital ships and cannot fortify heavily — so the Mouths are protected by exactly one thing: the volume
of what comes out of them. Cut the flow for two turns and it can be walked into.

**The danger.** The Swarm does not need the Trellis and never did. What the Mouths produce is a
civilisation that will eventually cross every hazard in the galaxy by filling it, including the shoals,
including the lanes, including the ones the Ancients cannot close fast enough. **The Swarm is the only
power that can reach the origin without solving anything**, and it will get there by accident, cheerfully,
and it will not understand what it has done, because there is no word for it.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Saturation Doctrine** *(Weapons)*
Your fleet's damage scales with **hull count** rather than per-hull quality. A hundred Motes outdamage a
Titan line, and each individual Mote is still pathetic. Weapons research remains capped at 3 and this
does not raise it — it changes what the number is multiplied by.
> *Each of we is not much. There are a great deal of we.*

**2 · Density Consensus** *(Economy)*
Production rises with concentration: the more of your fleet and population occupy one contiguous region,
the higher your metal income. Spread out and it collapses. This is the Swarm's politics rendered as an
economy — decisions are made where the most of them are, and so is everything else.
> *Where we are thickest, we are most.*

**3 · The Hatch Sequence** *(Economy — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
Not a design. A *rate* — the discovery of how fast a basin can be made to produce before it stops
producing at all.
> *We asked how many. The answer was more.*

## Signature hulls

**Mote** — The cheapest hull in the game by a wide margin. Near-free, nearly useless, dies to anything,
and **counts**. Under Saturation Doctrine a cloud of Motes is a real weapon, and a Mote crossing a shoal
is the cheapest information in the galaxy: send four hundred, and whichever ones report are the chart.

**Brood** — A light frigate that produces one Mote per turn while stationed. Not a carrier — the Swarm
cannot build carriers and this is not one; it is a hull that is slowly becoming several hulls, which is
a distinction the Swarm considers obvious and everyone else finds unsettling.

**Chorus** — A light hull that grows stronger with every other Chorus in the same fleet. One is worse
than a frigate. Twenty are worse than nothing else. It is the only hull in the game whose stat line
cannot be read in isolation, which is the point.

## Notes

**Access check — the important one.** `SHIPS: 1` holds. There is **no capital hull here and there never
will be**, and none of these three is a Battleship, Dreadnought, Intruder or Carrier. BU 400 stands: six
named hulls were lost in a decade and it caused *a fault in the whole*, and the Swarm has not laid down a
hull that could be mourned since. `SHIELDS: 1` also holds — nothing here protects an individual.

**The Remainder problem.** The Mouths make fragments more common, not less. A civilisation producing
hulls at that rate loses connection to more of itself, more often, and every one of those fragments has
no legal category and no way home. The Wonder that guarantees the Swarm's survival also guarantees a
steady production of the one kind of suffering it cannot perceive.

**Tone note.** Write the Swarm's Wonder cheerfully. That is what makes it frightening.
