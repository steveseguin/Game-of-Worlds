# Titan Lords — The Weight of Ages

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

## The Wonder: **The Weight of Ages**

Every other Wonder is infrastructure. This one is a **ship**.

The Weight of Ages is a single vessel, laid down on the deepest bedrock at Ordel Deep, built to a
thousand-year service life by forty houses working to one specification for the first time in four
hundred years. It is the largest object any civilisation has ever moved, and the Titan Lords do not
consider it a Wonder. They consider it a **work**, which is their word for the only thing that
constitutes evidence of a life.

Their faith holds that you are remembered for as long as something of yours is still running. The
Weight of Ages is a bid for a thousand years of being remembered, and every house that contributes a
member to it has secured its precedence for longer than most empires have existed.

It is not a Wonder that ends the Dimming. It is a Wonder that **outlasts** it.

**What it does [P].** It is a mobile safe corridor. Any fleet moving **with** the Weight of Ages takes
no hazard losses at all — not reduced, none — because the Weight goes first and is large enough that a
shoal is weather. This is Shipmaster Vaun's argument as a mechanic: *I do not offer that as seamanship. I
offer it as mass. Anything smaller would be part of the Vail shoal.*

Per Law 4 nothing here shields anything; the Weight simply absorbs, physically, and files the damage.
Per Law 5 it **cannot cross a collapsar**, and no amount of mass ever will.

It is also, incidentally, the most dangerous warship ever built.

**Maintenance [P].** Metal, enormously, forever. And **time**: the Weight moves at a fraction of the pace
of anything else in the game, taking many turns per sector, and it cannot be hurried by any technology
because `PROPULSION: 1` and four hundred years of a solved launch problem.

**How it dies.** With extraordinary difficulty, and it can be **outmanoeuvred trivially.** The Weight of
Ages is unkillable and always late. An opponent who accepts that they will never win a battle it attends,
and simply fights everywhere else, beats it — and every Titan strategist knows this, and says so, and it
changes nothing, because the alternative is building something that does not last.

**The danger.** It is the only Wonder that can reach the origin and survive the trip there.

Everything else in the galaxy that goes deep enough gets eaten by the crossings. The Weight of Ages does
not. Which makes it, entirely against its builders' intent, the **delivery mechanism** for somebody
else's plan — the only hull that could carry a Lamp, or a Sower, or a Closing component, or a Solved Sky
instrument, into the deep dark and arrive with it intact.

The Titan Lords will not be the ones to open the door. They will be the ones who drove.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Integral Lift** *(Economy)*
Capital hulls cost substantially less metal. This is the founding achievement of the modern Titan era —
BU 400, the lift architecture built into the vessel rather than solved with drives — and it is the last
major innovation anyone on Ordel Deep produced, and every house alive regards it as the high-water mark
of the craft.
> *We solved it. There has been no call to solve it again.*

**2 · Structural Honesty** *(Armor)*
Further durability, and it explicitly does **nothing** for shields and cannot combine with them. Titan
engineering culture holds that a member which carries no load is a lie about the building, and a
deflector is a lie about a hull.
> *It will be hit. We have arranged for that to be survivable.*

**3 · The Weight of Ages** *(Armor — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
The thousand-year specification. Forty houses, one drawing, and a precedence arrangement that took
nineteen years to negotiate and is considered the finest diplomatic work in Titan history.
> *You will forgive us for not attending in the season you would prefer.*

## Signature hulls

**Line Hull, *Consideration* class** — Priced on service life: it gets **cheaper to maintain the longer
you keep it**, which is the Titan currency rendered as a mechanic. A Consideration bought in the opening
turns is nearly free by the endgame, and selling one is the closest thing the Weight has to a financial
instrument.

**Deep Keel, *Forbearance* class** — The heaviest single hull available outside the Wonder. It halves its
own hazard losses through mass alone — physical, not shielded, Law 4 intact — and it is how a Titan fleet
crosses a shoal at all without the Weight of Ages present.

**Long Table, *Sufficiency* class** — A command capital. While present, no friendly fleet in the sector
can be forced to withdraw, and defensive performance rises. It is named for the furniture: business is not
discussed in the first two hours, and hurrying is the rudest thing a guest can do.

## Notes

**Access check, and it is absolute.** `ships: [4,5,6,7,9]` holds — **there is nothing small here.** No
Frigate, no Destroyer, no Scout, no Intruder, not in the Wonder, not in the signature hulls, not ever. At
1.9 gravity structural minimums scale and the Titan shipwright's art has no small form, and this is the
one race whose refusal is a *craft tradition* rather than a doctrine, which makes it the least likely to
bend and the most important to hold.

`PROPULSION: 1` holds: nothing in this file makes them faster. The Weight of Ages is slower than
everything, on purpose, permanently.

**Vaun.** The *Weight of Consideration* is still in service at seventy-six years — that is the business
model, not sentiment — and Shipmaster Vaun holds the only interior record of the Unarriving because his
crossing took eleven days when everyone else's took hours. Terra filed it as instrument error. The Weight
of Ages is named the way it is because a Titan house does not forget a filing, and if the campaign wants
that grievance paid off, this is the object it is paid off with.
