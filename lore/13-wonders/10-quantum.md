# Quantum Entities — The Unresolved Lane

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

## The Wonder: **The Unresolved Lane**

They do not build a road. They decline to have needed one.

The Unresolved Lane is a route between two sectors you hold that exists in a genuinely unresolved
state — it is there when you use it and not there when anyone else looks. Not hidden. Not cloaked.
**Unresolved**, in the specific bounded sense Law 21 permits: a structure in a high-flux environment
whose extent is a probability distribution rather than a boundary, scaled up to span sectors.

It is built deep, shielded, and ambiguous, the way every Not-Yet settlement is built, and its
construction progress cannot be reported accurately even by its owner. The Registry's survey of a
completed Unresolved Lane returns a different answer every time and all the answers are competent.

**What it does [P].** Your fleets move between the linked sectors at drastically reduced cost, and
enemies **cannot see it, use it, probe it, or attack it.** There is no lane sector to take. There is no
structure to raze.

Note carefully what it does **not** do: it does not carry a fleet past a hazard. Per Law 21 a shoal does
not care where you probably are, and any transit rolls the route implies are rolled normally. The
Unresolved Lane is cheap and invisible. It is not safe.

**Maintenance.** Not resources. **Being unobserved.**

Observation degrades it. Every successful enemy probe over your territory, every espionage success against
you, every scouted sector adjacent to its endpoints pushes the Lane closer to resolution — and a resolved
Lane is an ordinary route, visible and usable by anybody. The Not-Yet Wonder is maintained by
**counter-intelligence**, permanently, and it is the only Wonder whose upkeep is paid in the Intel branch.

**How it dies.** It is never destroyed. It **resolves.** Which is worse, because the transition is not
announced — a resolved Lane behaves exactly like an unresolved one until the turn an enemy fleet arrives
along it.

**The danger.** It resolves the wrong way.

Eventually, statistically, once, in one direction, and whatever the Ancients put across the road resolves
*with you inside it.* Osk's testimony has the shape of it: the Lamps did not fail, something was put
across the lane and then the lights were turned off so nobody would see what they were driving into. The
Unresolved Lane is a route that refuses to commit to whether it is occupied.

For nine days nothing arrived anywhere. And then something did, and it was not on the schedule, and the
Not-Yet have never been able to establish where.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Deferred Position** *(Intel)*
Your fleets' destinations are not revealed to any enemy until arrival. Other empires can see that you have
moved and cannot determine where to, because your movement order genuinely has not resolved into a
destination until it completes. It is an intelligence effect with no hazard interaction whatsoever.
> *Had we gone to six-B, we would have lost four. We may have.*

**2 · Non-Collapsed Yards** *(Economy)*
Your production output **varies**, turn to turn, averaging meaningfully above baseline. You cannot
forecast it. Neither can anyone else, which is why Terra insists on settlement in stamps, in advance, in
full.
> *It is slightly better than specified some of the time. We did tell you.*

**3 · The Unresolved** *(Shields — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
The formalism for holding a structure unresolved across a distance greater than a settlement. Derivable
only in the flux, testable only by not testing it, and the source of a constitutional objection from the
Not-Yet fraction that has never been withdrawn.
> *We would rather not be present for it. We may not have been.*

## Signature hulls

**Perhaps** — A hull that **may or may not participate in each round** of an engagement. Genuinely: it is
resolved per round. Its expected damage is good and its actual damage in any given battle is a coin
sequence, and the Not-Yet consider planning around expected values obviously correct and every other
admiralty finds it intolerable.

**Two-State** — Resolves on arrival into either a warship or a freighter, and you do not choose. Cheap for
what it is, because half the time it is the wrong thing. Nomad brokers will not accept them as cargo
haulage and say so politely.

**Retrodiction** — On being destroyed, it retroactively was not carrying what the enemy recorded. Their
battle telemetry for that engagement is **wrong** — wrong composition, wrong strength, wrong conclusions —
and they will plan their next engagement on it. It does not come back. Per Law 15 and Law 17 nothing here
regenerates or resurrects; what changes is the *record*.

## Notes

**Access check, and it is the strictest in the folder.** Per **Law 21**: nothing here exceeds one sector
of positional uncertainty, nothing evades a hazard roll, nothing phases into held space past defences,
and nothing phases a hull carrying something that must arrive intact. The Unresolved Lane is explicitly
**cheap and invisible but not safe**, which is the whole discipline — the obvious version of this Wonder
would let them ignore shoals, and that version is forbidden.

`ARMOR: 2` holds and **no Dreadnought appears here or ever will**, because a thing that definitely exists
in one place can be definitely destroyed. The Not-Yet have no flagship, no capital, and no monuments.

**Their politics makes this Wonder nearly unbuildable, and that is correct.** A meaningful fraction of the
polity has not experienced the Unarriving and will not consent to policy premised on it — a seventy-four
year quorum failure. The Unresolved Lane requires them to act on a shutdown a substantial part of them
does not accept has occurred. Getting it built at all is a constitutional event.
