# Bioform Collective — The Mother Grove

*Numbers marked **[P]** are placeholders pending a balance pass.*

---

## The Wonder: **The Mother Grove**

The only Wonder that does not try to relight the road.

The Mother Grove makes the road unnecessary. It is a berth-grove of a kind the Collective has never
raised before — not a nursery for hulls but a nursery for **places** — and what it produces is seed
stock that can be planted in a shoal.

Ossa said it plainly in AU 49, and nobody listened, because it was the least useful sentence in the Long
File: *plant something there and in nine seasons the shoal will be a garden and nobody will remember the
price.*

The Grove is that, industrialised, over centuries, by people who are not in a hurry.

**What it does [P].** Hazard sectors adjacent to your territory **convert**, over many turns, into safe
and productive ones — permanently, without being swept militarily, without a fleet dying to buy them. A
seeded shoal stops being a shoal. It becomes a colonisable world with a modest yield, and it stays that
way for everyone, forever, including your enemies.

It is the only positive-sum Wonder in the game. It is also the slowest thing in the game by a wide
margin.

**Maintenance [P].** Seasons, and uninterrupted possession. The conversion cannot be rushed, cannot be
paid to accelerate, and **resets if the sector changes hands.** There is no surge option. Nine years is
nine years, which is the same constraint that governs every Bioform hull and the same reason nobody is
truly afraid of them.

**How it dies.** It barely can. The Grove itself is a soft target — the Collective cannot build orbital
defences and never will — but killing it does not undo what it has already converted. **Every sector the
Mother Grove finished stays finished.** An empire that destroys the Grove inherits a galaxy with
permanently fewer death traps in it, which is the most Bioform outcome imaginable: they win by having
been there.

**The danger — and it is not a danger to anyone.**

This is the correct answer. The Mother Grove ends the Dimming's *effects* without reopening a single
lane, without relighting anything, without touching the quarantine. Nobody dies. Nothing is unsealed.

**And it takes centuries, so nobody will choose it.**

That is the thematic keystone of the entire folder, and it should never be balanced into
competitiveness. Eleven empires have a fast Wonder that reopens the road and one empire has a slow
Wonder that fixes the problem, and the game must let the player feel that the correct path was
available and too patient to take. The Collective knew this in BU 40. They rerouted their hulls, said
nothing, and started growing.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Deep Berth** *(Terraform / Economy)*
Raises the growth rate of every hull you own and shortens maturity. Still measured in years. It is the
difference between a nine-year cruiser and a seven-year cruiser, which the Collective regards as an
enormous advance and every other race regards as a rounding error.
> *You cannot hurry a thing into being older.*

**2 · Seeding** *(Terraform)*
Held hazard sectors slowly become colonisable. The Wonder's precursor and useful on its own — it is the
only technology in the game that changes what a sector *is*.
> *That is how a place becomes known. Plant something there.*

**3 · The Whole Season** *(Terraform — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
Named for the faith. The formalism for growing something that raises places rather than ships, and the
first Bioform technology in recorded history intended to outlast its grower.
> *Things should be allowed to complete. We are going to allow a galaxy to.*

## Signature hulls

**Elder** — A hull grown past ordinary maturity. **One at a time**, empire-wide: enormously strong,
unique, and irreplaceable within a decade. Losing your Elder is the most permanent loss any race can
suffer, because there is no shipyard that can answer it — only nine more years. Bioform veterans are
not upgrades. They are old animals.

**Sower** — Carries seed stock and plants it. Unarmed, slow, precious, and the vehicle by which the
Mother Grove's conversion actually reaches a sector. Killing Sowers is the only reliable way to slow the
Grove, and it is how every war with the Collective is actually fought.

**Thin One** — A juvenile hull, released early, weak now and strong later. It is named after Ossa's
testimony — the lines that flew the deep lanes and came back *thin* — and it is the cheapest way into a
Bioform fleet, and it is a bet on surviving long enough to be worth it.

## Notes

**Access check.** `ORBITAL: 0` holds absolutely — the Mother Grove is a grove, not a fortification, and
it cannot be defended by turrets, and the Collective will still never build a Warp Gate. That last point
is the whole race: **they have a Wonder and it is not a piece of the Trellis.** `SHIELDS: 2` holds. No
Carrier appears here; the Sower is not one.

**They have no Lamp programme, and now they have an alternative.** For seventy-four years the Collective
has been patronised for saying courteously that the galaxy is better as it is. The Mother Grove is what
they were doing instead of arguing, and when the campaign reveals why they were right, this is the object
that proves they were not merely obtuse.

**Recommended as one of the four Wonders to build first.** Not for the mechanic — hazard conversion is
real engine work — but because without it the Wonder system says only *"twelve ways to win."* With it,
the system says *"eleven ways to win and one way to be right,"* and that is a different game.
