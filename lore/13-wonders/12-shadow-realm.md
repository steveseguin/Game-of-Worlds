## The Wonder: **The Assembled Frame**

They buy it back.

For seventy years the Unlisted have been selling imagery of eleven Ancient vessels working at a relay
beside a collapsar in BU 13 — a frame here, a frame there, eleven buyers, eleven fragments, and not one
buyer holding enough to make a sentence out of. That was not caution. That was pricing, and it kept the
lights on, and the Keepers have called it a slow catastrophe conducted for operating expenses for three
generations.

The Assembled Frame is the Brokers admitting the Keepers were right.

It is the only Wonder with **no construction site.** There is nothing to build. It is assembled by
acquisition — bought back, traded back, stolen back, extorted back, one fragment at a time from eleven
different holders who each paid for it and each know what they have. Progress is measured in fragments
recovered, and every fragment is a separate negotiation with a separate empire, and none of them will
part with it cheaply now that they know somebody wants the set.

**And so it announces a count instead of a coordinate** (Q10g). Every other Wonder tells the galaxy who
has begun and where. The Frame has no where, so the galaxy is told **how many of the eleven are
recovered**, updated as that number changes, and never a location. This is not a concession for having
no site; it is a worse thing to hear. *Somebody has begun in sector 34* can be discounted by anyone far
enough away. *Seven of eleven* cannot be discounted by anybody.

The Unlisted will note, correctly and without pleasure, that the empire which refuses to be counted has
become the only empire in the galaxy that the galaxy counts. Ossa is said to have called the public
tally *"the first honest ledger anybody ever kept about us, and we did not get to write it."*

**How they are stopped.** Not by siege — there is nothing to lay siege to. The Frame's progress **is**
the fragments, and a fragment sits on ground and changes hands with it (Q10b). So there is no single
target and there are up to eleven soft ones, each worth one step backwards. Fine-grained rather than
decisive, which is the correct way to be at war with people nobody can find.

And when it is complete, they can finally read it. Sable was honest about that in AU 67, which was the
one time in recorded history an Unlisted volunteered the truth: *we cannot read them either.*

**What it does [P].** Total intelligence. Every empire's holdings, technology levels, fleet
compositions, and — the part that decides games — **Wonder progress**, continuously and accurately. Plus
the ability to **sabotage** another player's Wonder: not destroy it, but push it into dormancy by
interfering with its maintenance, which is the difference between a war and an accounting error.

It is the only Wonder that acts on other Wonders.

**Maintenance [P].** Owed answers — a continuous diplomatic cost, and the Frame requires the other
empires to **keep dealing with you.** An Unlisted player at war with the galaxy cannot maintain it. The
Realm's Wonder needs the room to stay in the room, which is a genuinely hostile constraint for the
faction whose entire competency is not being in the room.

**How it dies.** It cannot be attacked, because there is nowhere to attack. It can only be
**out-negotiated**: any empire holding a fragment can refuse to sell, and a Frame missing one fragment
does nothing at all. Eleven veto holders, and every one of them has a reason to think about it.

**The danger — and it is only to them.**

The Assembled Frame is the end of the Shadow Realm.

Their strategic position rests on a single fact: nobody knows the size of the thing they are dealing
with. Their armour is capped, their durability is the worst on the roster, their cities cannot be counted
from orbit and never have been, and the Realm is a bluff that has never been called at scale. Their one
irreplaceable asset is the fragments — and the moment they are assembled and read, the asset is
**information**, and information gets out.

Sable said it plainly: *the day somebody assembles them is the day the only thing we have stops being
ours.* The Unlisted Wonder tells them exactly what the Ancients did, five years before the four-sentence
letter, and it costs them the only reason anyone has ever left them alone.

They will build it anyway. That is the most human thing about the least trusted people in the galaxy.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Owed Answers** *(Intel)*
Against an empire you have out-spied, you may **compel one true answer** — a holding, a tech level, a
fleet position, a Wonder's progress — at a time of your choosing. It is their internal currency made
external: debt denominated in questions, transferable, inheritable, and always honoured.
> *I notice you did not ask me how many I have. Shall we discuss what I would like?*

**2 · No Long Sightlines** *(Intel)*
Your fleets' destinations and compositions cannot be determined by any empire, by any means, at any
intel level. It is the architecture of Sable's Dark as a doctrine: no sightline in a Shadow city exceeds
the length of a single room, at any scale, by design.
> *You may look as long as you like. You will see this room.*

**3 · The Assembled Frame** *(Intel — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
Not a technology so much as a **reading method** — the analytical framework that turns eleven unrelated
frames of seventy-year-old imagery into a sequence. Deriving it was the easy part. Buying the frames is
the Wonder.
> *We have had seventy years and we cannot read them. Here is your fragment. I chose it kindly.*

## Signature hulls

**the Unlisted** — Does not appear in enemy fleet counts. At all. An opponent scouting a sector
containing Unlisted hulls receives an accurate report of everything *else* in it, which is far more
dangerous than an obviously incomplete one, because the report looks complete. This is the race's
existing stealth signature taken to its endpoint: they hide composition, and this hull is composition
that is not there.

**Frame** — A fast raider that **steals a trace on arrival**, taking route knowledge from whoever holds
the sector. Cheap, fragile, and the reason no empire leaves its charts near a Shadow border.

**Courtesy** — When it wins an engagement, the loser receives a **false battle report**: wrong losses,
wrong strengths, wrong conclusions, delivered with impeccable formatting. It is named for the ethic
— never let a guest know what you know, and never lie to them, and simply answer a slightly different
question, generously, at length.

## Notes

**Access check.** `ARMOR: 2` holds and **no Dreadnought and no Carrier appear here or ever will.**
Nothing on Sable's Dark ever needed to survive being seen and shot at, and their durability stays at
×0.8 — the Assembled Frame gives them omniscience and does not give them a single point of armour, which
is precisely the trade their whole civilisation is.

**The Mechanicus problem, and it should stay unfixed.** Every stealth capability in this file is
**worthless against the Second Shift**, who have no scouts, no intruders, and no intelligence on anyone
including themselves. You cannot hide composition from someone who never knew what you had. A Shadow
player facing the Mechanicus has spent their whole game on a toolkit that does nothing, and that
asymmetry is one of the best things in the design.

**Sable.** The AU 67 testimony is where this Wonder comes from: a Broker giving a fragment away for
nothing, choosing a good frame deliberately, telling the honest reason instead of a clever one, and
noticing that Ilsa Rell looked tired one year before she died. If the Frame is ever completed in a
campaign, the last fragment should be the one Sable gave her — sitting in cabinet forty-one, in the
Registry, where nobody thought to look.
