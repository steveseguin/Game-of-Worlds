## The Wonder: **The Reconciled Schedule**

They finish the count.

The Reconciled Schedule is an archive to end the Registry's — every manifest, every berth assignment,
every convoy log, every bill of lading, from every empire and every Fleet lineage, reconciled against
each other until the ledger closes. Departures matched to arrivals. Names matched to berths. The
unreconciled resolved, one entry at a time, across seventy-four years and eleven jurisdictions.

It is a memorial and it is also, unavoidably, **the most complete map of routes ever assembled**,
because a manifest is a record of where something went.

It is not built on Kettering. It is built in the eleven thousand empty berths that were assigned to hulls
that did not arrive in AU 0 and have never been reassigned — the ones the Fleet has swept and lit and
maintained for seventy-four years as the largest line item in its accounts, unquestioned. The Wonder is
what those berths were always for.

**What it does [P].** You gain traces derived from **every empire's history**, not just your own: any
route any power has ever flown and filed becomes available to you, including sectors you have never
visited and lanes whose owners are dead. You may move through any sector any empire has ever swept.

Movement stops being a thing you pay for and becomes a thing you look up.

**Maintenance [P].** Chits, continuously — and this is the only Wonder with a **diplomatic** upkeep. The
Schedule requires other people's records, so it must keep buying them: a standing payment to every convoy
and, critically, to other empires, who may refuse. A Nomad player at war with everyone cannot maintain
it. The Wonder that fixes the count needs the galaxy to keep talking to them.

**How it dies.** Take Kettering, or cut the payments. But the Schedule is copied by design — the whole
point is that it exists in more than one place — so destroying it is unusually hard and unusually
partial. What you can do is **make it stale**, and a stale Schedule is a book of routes that were true
once, which is exactly the thing that killed them the first time.

**The danger.** It corrects the count.

Six downward revisions in sixty years, each one individually defensible, cumulatively a lie of several
million — and Registrar Vance has known the whole time, and the Nomads have known the whole time, and
nobody has said it in a chamber. The Reconciled Schedule says it, with citations, in public, to eleven
empires at once.

**Terra does not survive that politically.** The Registry's authority rests entirely on being trusted to
count, and the Nomad Wonder is a peer-reviewed demonstration that it cannot be. This is the only Wonder
whose danger is not to the galaxy but to a specific ally, and the Nomads know precisely what they are
detonating, and they have thirty-one names in the *Ninefold Patience*'s galley to weigh against it.

## Signature technologies

**1 · Chit Clearing** *(Economy)*
You may convert metal to crystal and crystal to metal at a loss, at will. Nobody else can. The Fleet
invented money and it invented it as a *bearer instrument redeemable anywhere*, which is liquidity, and
liquidity is the one economic advantage available to a people with no ground.
> *Backed by the promise of someone who might not arrive. The most honest paper ever written.*

**2 · Trace Brokerage** *(Intel)*
On establishing contact with another empire you acquire some of their held traces, and you may purchase
more openly through diplomacy. You are not stealing. You are **buying**, at a fair price, from people
who would rather sell.
> *My mother's cousin flew it in sixty-eight and came back with nine of twelve. Mind what you offer me.*

**3 · The Reconciled Schedule** *(Intel — capstone, Wonder prerequisite)*
The reconciliation formalism: a method for matching eleven incompatible filing systems against each
other well enough to close a ledger. Terra could have built this. Terra had no reason to.
> *You are holding several million that nobody will ever name.*

## Signature hulls

**Ninefold** — A proposed mobile berth. A colony vessel that can **decolonise and move on**, carrying its
settlement with it. This would be a new Wonder mechanic, not an extension of any live colony-ship flag.
It is named for the convoy lineage that lost the *Ninefold Patience*, and Nomad hulls are always named
for routes or losses.

**Hallam's Ring** — A fast, cheap runner that **generates income while under way** rather than while
docked. A Nomad fleet in motion is a Nomad fleet earning, and standing still is the only thing that
costs them money.

**Slow Consort** — Carries the wall of names. While present in a sector, losses your fleet takes there
are **recorded** — converted into permanent trace knowledge of that sector rather than simply spent. It
is the least efficient hull in the Fleet and no convoy will sail without one.

## Notes

**Access check.** `TERRAFORM: 2` and `ORBITAL: 1` hold. Nothing here makes them want a planet: the
Ninefold is a colony hull that *leaves*, which is the opposite of settlement, and three council motions
to fund terraforming have failed since AU 30 with two sponsors losing their berths. They have all nine
standard hulls already and the worst possible reason to use them.

**The best emotional payoff available in the folder.** When the Schedule closes, the *Ninefold Patience*
gets an entry. Thirty-one names, matched to a berth, seventy-four years late — and the hull is in that
berth, undamaged, with thirty-one places set in the galley, and it has been there for forty-three years
because the Registry would have catalogued it and it would have stopped being theirs.

**And the rite.** The Nomads hold a funeral for a salted probe because a salted probe is a machine given
a false schedule that flew into it, which is what happened to their entire people. The Reconciled
Schedule is the Fleet finally correcting the timetable. Everything about this Wonder is that one idea.
